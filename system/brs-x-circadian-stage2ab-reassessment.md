## Stage 2A: propositions and evidence

| Proposition | Evidence and measured links | Decision/claim boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Evening light changes melatonin secretion | Gooley 2011: retrospective analysis of 116 healthy 18–30-year-olds; room light below 200 lux vs dim light below 3 lux; approximately 90 minutes shorter secretion, later onset in 99% | PM1-F1 retained and appraisal corrected; acute output suppression is not equivalent to enduring phase reset. |
| Timed light resets central circadian phase | Khalsa 2003: 21 adults; 6.7 h light pulse across internal phases; pre/post constant-routine melatonin phase; 5.02 h peak-to-trough phase-response amplitude | PM1-F2 added. Phase-dependent response establishes the timing route, not one best daily prescription. SCN neuronal phase itself not directly recorded. |
| Evening device exposure affects timing, sleep and alertness | Chang 2015: light-emitting eReader vs printed book; measured delayed circadian timing, sleep-onset and next-morning alertness effects | Independent comparison in PM1-F2; device-specific context retained; no general screen-dose equivalence or inherited phenome rating. |
| Retinal light reaches circadian machinery | Berson 2002 retinal recordings; Fu 2005 Rpe65/melanopsin mouse manipulations and retinal analogue rescue | PM1-F3 added. Intrinsic photosensitivity/photopigment dependency supported; adult human dietary provision and adequacy effects not quantified by these experiments. |
| Feeding changes peripheral timing | Wehrens 2017: ten men, 13-day ordered protocol; early then six days late meals, 37 h constant routines; glucose and adipose PER2 measured | PM2-F1 corrected/expanded. No significant changes in measured melatonin/cortisol; does not prove all central clocks unaffected or all peripheral tissues shifted equally. |
| Feeding can reset peripheral clocks separately from central timing | Stokkan 2001 rats: liver shifted ~10 h within two days while SCN remained light-linked; Damiola 2000 mice: tissue clocks shifted up to 12 h, tissue adaptation differed | PM2-F2 added. Mechanistic triangulation, not human dose extrapolation; uncoupling can occur. |
| Feeding hormones connect exposure to cellular clock machinery | Crosby 2019 cellular/mouse manipulation: insulin/IGF-1 induced PERIOD and reset rhythms; mistimed signalling disrupted organisation | Included in PM2-F2; inferred relevance to the human meal manipulation, not direct human proof of mediation. Does not prescribe raising insulin or changing a nutrient. |
| Timed feeding produces metabolic protection | Hatori 2012 mouse high-fat diet remains connected evidence | Useful outcome evidence retained; not equivalent to measuring human peripheral phase or proving benefit mediated by a clock shift. |
| Food timing and obesity are associated | Garaulet/Gómez-Abellán 2014 is a review, not a new primary human phase experiment | Connected evidence retained; source type corrected to Review. No phase or causal mediation admission. |

## Remaining gaps and next steps

1. Vitamin A provision: Fu establishes photopigment dependency/rescue, whereas PMID 11562477 reports preserved SCN light-induced gene expression in vitamin A-depleted RBP-mutant mice. Compare residual tissue retinoids, visual-cycle compensation and endpoints before deciding dietary provision or a conditional inadequacy claim. Do not reject the supported biochemical chain merely because no adult human supplementation trial exists. No substance identity-based projection published from this unresolved candidate.
2. PM2 beneficial alignment: comparative, phase-resolved human interventions needed to specify optimum alignment/window and durable health benefit; a shift alone is insufficient. Insulin/IGF-1 mediation in the Wehrens humans remains inferred.
3. PM1 parameter transfer: phase, duration, spectrum, prior light and individual sensitivity need fuller appraisal before quantitative real-world light prescriptions; Phillips 2019 variability is a relevant next source, not a universal lux cutoff.
4. Assess candidate sleep/alertness phenome relationships from Chang independently; do not inherit old scores or treat one exposure result as a completed phenome assessment.
5. Reconcile FM/hub integration, full SOP frame and connected descriptions; supersede obsolete outline instructions and verify retirement/redirects across registries. These review notes remain open rather than being lost in the PM rewrite.
6. Existing shared SOP disclosure mapper does not project stored reader_description/description_finding_id, although it renders all five fields and bibliography links. Reported infrastructure gap; no shared-code change in this two-PM run.
