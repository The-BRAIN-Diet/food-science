## Propositions

The mission is efficient electron transport that makes ATP without excessive electron leakage. The inherited overview also claimed that macronutrient and cofactor sufficiency link meals directly to cognitive energy. The mechanistic basis named CoQ10, iron, riboflavin, niacin, glucose, fatty acids and amino acids without separating the chain from fuel routing or NAD synthesis. Published connections to methionine-cycle flux, glycaemic variability, NAD metabolism and creatine buffering were bare or associative. Phenome rows used the ADHD cybrid paper as if it were an attention and cognitive-energy result.

## Corpus, then targeted retrieval

Attached sources were Crane (2001), Tardy et al. (2020), Verma et al. (2016) and Öğütlü et al. (2022). Crane supports a biochemical role for coenzyme Q10 in electron transport. Tardy is a narrative review of vitamins, minerals, energy and fatigue; it does not measure these complexes. Verma is the only human mechanistic measurement. Öğütlü reviews ADHD mitochondrial papers and does not add an independent chain measurement.

The corpus did not establish the complexes, the carriers, the metal and flavin centres, or the sites of electron leak. Targeted retrieval stopped at three sources:

- Vercellino and Sazanov (2022) for the five complexes, two mobile carriers, proton-coupled ATP synthesis, iron–sulfur clusters, haem and complex IV copper.
- Curtabbi and Enríquez (2022) for FMN as the NADH entry cofactor of complex I, including the statement that FMN abundance has not been shown to limit the complex.
- Brand (2016) for eleven leak sites and the resting-muscle dominance of complexes I, II and III.

Stopping rationale: those three sources answer the defining chemistry. Further supercomplex or disease-gene papers would not change the participant list or the claim ceiling.

The overview was rewritten because the meal-to-cognition sentence was stronger than the findings. That is a claim correction inside the same mechanism, not a new PM scope.

## Connections

NAD metabolism supplies NADH to complex I. Creatine buffering stores ATP made by the chain; it is not electron transport. Methionine-cycle flux and glycaemic-variability control were not shown to change the rate of these complexes. Those links stay published with that boundary.
