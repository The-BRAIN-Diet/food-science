**Contract:** `system/kc-page-evidence-review-contract.md`

**Corpus (existing page only):** Kyriazis et al. 2022
(`kyriazis_impact_2022`); Rose et al. 2018 (`rose_butyrate_2018`).  
**PM evidence:** not modified. PM ↔ iKC applicability remains an independent
Stage 2B decision.

## Page coherence

**Candidate constraint:** amino acids, fatty acids and glucose as one
macronutrient-substrate pool supporting all listed mitochondrial mechanisms.

**Adjudication:** **supported after narrowing.** The Kyriazis review supports a
shared, adaptable mitochondrial fuel-availability constraint involving glucose
and fatty-acid oxidation across fasting and differing dietary patterns. It does
not establish dietary amino-acid availability as part of that shared limiting
fuel resource. Rose tests butyrate exposure in cultured lymphoblastoid cells
under oxidative stress; it is not evidence that butyrate or the broader
fatty-acid class is an indispensable dietary constraint.

The surviving page-level constraint is **glucose and fatty-acid availability as
alternative oxidizable fuels**. The title was aligned with the route and retained
concept of availability. The page remains one default iKC whose `ikc_id` equals
`BRS4(KC1)`.

## iKC definition

- **What is constrained:** access to glucose- and fatty-acid-derived oxidizable
  carbon for mitochondrial ATP production and adaptive fuel selection.
- **Resource:** a flexible fuel-substrate resource, not every macronutrient or
  metabolite that can enter mitochondrial metabolism.
- **Why it is a constraint:** fasting and dietary patterns alter substrate
  availability and mitochondrial fuel use; inadequate access can restrict
  oxidation or switching.
- **Shared-domain rationale:** glucose and fatty acids are alternative fuel
  classes used across bioenergetic and substrate-flexibility processes.

## Constituent adjudication

### Glucose — admitted

- **Input Type:** `substrate`
- **Biological Role:** oxidizable carbon substrate contributing to the fuel
  resource used for ATP production and switching between carbohydrate and lipid
  oxidation.
- **Evidence Source:** `kyriazis_impact_2022`
- **Limitation:** dietary-pattern review; no universal intake threshold, proof
  of ordinary limitation in every population or benefit from additional intake.
- **Status / ceiling:** `nutritionally-constrained`;
  `dietary-provision`.

### Fatty acids — admitted

- **Input Type:** `substrate`
- **Biological Role:** oxidizable lipid substrates contributing to the shared
  fuel resource and shifts toward fat oxidation.
- **Evidence Source:** `kyriazis_impact_2022`
- **Limitation:** no universal requirement across every connected mechanism,
  optimal fatty-acid composition or benefit from increasing intake in
  fuel-replete people.
- **Status / ceiling:** `nutritionally-constrained`;
  `dietary-provision`.

### Amino acids — excluded

- **Input Type:** `substrate`
- **Candidate Role:** carbon and anaplerotic contribution to mitochondrial
  energy metabolism.
- **Evidence Source assessed:** `kyriazis_impact_2022`
- **Reason:** the attached review does not establish dietary amino-acid
  availability as a shared limiting fuel resource across the proposed BRS4
  mechanisms. Biochemical use and mitochondrial enzyme renewal are insufficient
  for strict membership.
- **Status / ceiling:** `unsupported`; `biological-dependency`.

### Butyrate — excluded

- **Input Type:** `substrate`
- **Observed Role:** in-vitro exposure altered mitochondrial function during
  oxidative stress in autism-derived lymphoblastoid cell lines.
- **Evidence Source:** `rose_butyrate_2018`
- **Reason:** the source is condition- and model-specific modulation, not
  evidence of an indispensable dietary fuel, KC membership or support for the
  broad fatty-acid class.
- **Status / ceiling:** `modulatory`; `modulation-demonstrated`.

## Governing limitations and stopping rationale

- Only the two existing page sources were used.
- Kyriazis is a broad review of dietary patterns and mitochondrial physiology,
  not a constituent-specific intake or deficiency study.
- Rose is an in-vitro butyrate exposure study and cannot establish dietary fuel
  membership.
- PM applicability was not researched or inferred from the connected list.

Assessment stopped when page coherence, every proposed constituent, the
butyrate evidence claim, public Summary and proposed scope were adjudicated from
the existing corpus. No unresolved proposition requires a KC change-control
flag.

## Verdict

**Canonical after narrowing.** `BRS4(KC1)` now represents glucose and fatty-acid
fuel availability. Glucose and fatty acids are admitted; amino acids and
butyrate remain explicit excluded atoms. PM evidence was not modified.
