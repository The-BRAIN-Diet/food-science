## Why the attached corpus could not carry the claim

The overview cited Bulut 2007, Fielding 2005, and Johnson 2014 for vitamin E and membrane peroxidation.

- Bulut 2007 compares malondialdehyde in an adult ADHD cohort. It does not assign the difference to vitamin E.
- Fielding 2005 measures lycopene absorption from tomatoes cooked with olive oil.
- Johnson 2014 reviews lutein and zeaxanthin in visual and cognitive function. The bibliography abstract does not analyse vitamin E chain-breaking.

Those three papers are the wrong construct. They are removed from the finding and from the Overview. They remain in the bibliography only where a phenome card still points at them, and that card’s rationale now says the marker is not a vitamin E result. The confidence score was not changed.

## Search

Niki 2014 (Free Radic Biol Med 66:3–12) reviews kinetic and in vivo evidence that vitamin E is the principal lipophilic peroxyl-radical scavenger, that it inhibits lipid peroxidation by breaking chain propagation, and that it is not an efficient scavenger of hydroxyl, alkoxyl, nitrogen dioxide, or thiyl radicals in vivo.

Traber and Atkinson 2007 (Free Radic Biol Med 43:4–15) argue that the vitamin function of alpha-tocopherol is that scavenging, which protects polyunsaturated fats in membranes.

Klein 2011 (JAMA 306:1549) is the SELECT prevention trial: 400 IU of vitamin E a day was associated with more prostate cancers than placebo. The endpoint is cancer, not peroxidation. It is the ceiling on a high-dose reading.
