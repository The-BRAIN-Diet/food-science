## Type D — BRS2(KC2)

Applicability proposition: inadequacy of the shared methionine and cysteine pool constrains MAT-dependent SAMe synthesis capacity.

Established link: methionine is the MAT substrate (PM3-F1).

Not established: that inadequacy of the named pool reduces human MAT flux. Substrate necessity is not that constraint. Targeted checks:

- Shivapurkar and Poirier (1983) lowered hepatic SAMe with methyl-deficient amino-acid diets, but methionine-deficient arms replaced methionine with homocystine and SAMe tracked dietary methyl content.
- Methionine–choline-deficient rat models lower hepatic SAMe and methionine repletion can raise it, but choline removal confounds the sulfur amino-acid pool.
- Zeisel-type choline deficiency can lower hepatic SAMe with only a small methionine change, which points at methyl-donor supply rather than this pool.
- Corrillero Bravo et al. (2022) found no SAM separation after a four-week vegan versus meat-rich pattern, and PM3-IC1 already bars reading SAMe concentration as MAT flux.

Disposition: **unresolved**. PM3 no longer lists KC2, and §3.1.3 reads “No mapping established.” No constituent or food list is published.

## Coverage and stopping rationale

The reaction inventory was already evidenced. The open Type D question was whether pool inadequacy, as distinct from substrate identity, constrains MAT flux. The retrieved deficiency models do not isolate that pool or measure flux, and the attached human diet contrast does not show modulation. Further methyl-deficiency literature would repeat the same confound. Stopping here.
