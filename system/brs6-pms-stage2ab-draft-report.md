**Findings and sources:**

- **PM1-F1: Cereal structure and processing change the timing of meal-glucose appearance.** The tested rye bread and high-slowly-digestible-starch cereal product reduced early model-derived meal-glucose appearance in their respective comparisons. Tracer studies distinguish early appearance from later release and from blood concentration; processing can redistribute arrival without establishing a smaller total absorbed amount. Boundary: Small acute product comparisons; the rye result does not isolate fibre, and the biscuit study does not establish a generic wholegrain or biscuit benefit.
  - [Östman et al. (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30636184/): Early calculated appearance was lower with wholemeal rye despite similar glucose profiles. Access: Primary abstract/results retrieved; full methods not independently extracted unless noted.
  - [Vinoy et al. (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24015715/): High-slowly-digestible-starch biscuits had lower exogenous appearance at 90–150 min and higher appearance at 210 min than extruded cereal. Access: Primary publisher/PubMed abstract retrieved; full methods not independently extracted.


- **PM1-F2: Meal sequence and a protein preload can delay gastric delivery.** The studied fish-first sequence and whey preload delayed gastric emptying in separate acute comparisons. Breath-test and scintigraphic measurements support an upstream delivery mechanism; glucose lowering also involves insulin and incretin responses, not delivery alone. Boundary: Protocol- and population-specific; the 55 g whey preload added energy and is not a general protein requirement.
  - [Kuwata et al. (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4742500/): Fish first delayed breath-test gastric emptying and reduced excursions in the diabetes group. Access: Full Methods/Results retrieved: randomized fish/rice comparison; fixed third-day meat condition.
  - [Ma et al. (2009)](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article-pdf/32/9/1600/665011/zdc00909001600.pdf): Scintigraphic gastric emptying was slowest after the preload; glucose AUC fell while insulin and gut-hormone responses increased. Access: Full primary three-page report retrieved: methods and results, scintigraphy.


- **PM1-F3: Food structure and cooling affect digestibility, with entry flux still inferred.** Pasta structure survived mastication and simulated digestion; cooling the tested chickpea pasta increased measured resistant starch. Both comparisons also measured lower post-meal glycaemia. Structural and digestibility measurements support the entry pathway without requiring one end-to-end assay. Neither experiment directly quantified systemic appearance, so lower glycaemia is not itself a flux result. Boundary: Keep the exact pasta matrices and preparations. Cooling may reduce digestible quantity as well as alter timing; do not call the comparison equal absorbed carbohydrate.
  - [Vanhatalo et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8971003/): Pasta retained larger particles and protein-embedded starch; glucose AUC was lower than bread/couscous. Access: Full primary methods/results retrieved, including particle and hydrolysis measurements.
  - [Bojarczuk et al. (2024)](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/3a10/7c5f9655ad61a53c68439e8bc91436be39ff.pdf): Resistant starch increased from 1.83 to 3.65 g/100 g and GI fell from 39 to 33. Access: Full primary Methods §2.3–2.4 and Results §3.2–3.4 retrieved; analytical RS versus human glucose kept separate.
  - Context/contrary evidence: Hodges et al. (2020). Fresh/cold/reheated pasta comparisons test the same preparation candidate. Retrieved primary abstract/results. The cold-versus-hot AUC comparison was not significant; no appearance flux or proof of a universal cooling effect.

- **PM1-F4: Defined fibre preparations affect appearance differently.** The tested beta-glucan meal shifted exogenous appearance later; a guar/chickpea flatbread blend modestly reduced four-hour exogenous appearance without significantly changing its primary half-absorption-time endpoint. Fibre identity, dose and matrix matter. Larger changes in endogenous production or disposal must not be described as intestinal slowing. Boundary: Do not generalise these preparations to all fibre, all guar doses or psyllium; the blend cannot isolate guar from chickpea flour.
  - [Nazare et al. (2009)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/mnfr.200800023): Less exogenous glucose appeared in the first 120 min and more later, with no difference in the overall quantity appearing. Access: Primary publisher/PubMed abstract retrieved; full methods not independently extracted.
  - [Boers et al. (2017)](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/effect-of-fibre-additions-to-flatbread-flour-mixes-on-glucose-kin/): The 4% blend reduced four-hour exogenous appearance AUC by 11%; half-absorption time did not differ significantly. Endogenous production fell more strongly. Access: Primary publisher/PubMed abstract retrieved; full methods not independently extracted.
  - Context/contrary evidence: Rigaud et al. (1998). Fourteen healthy volunteers, randomized crossover 7.4 g psyllium; radiolabeled solid/liquid emptying and blood markers. No measured emptying delay. Intestinal slowing was an interpretation of flatter blood curves, not a measured appearance endpoint; do not admit a psyllium emptying claim.

- **PM1-F5: Sugar linkage can change appearance without establishing lasting clinical benefit.** Isomaltulose produced slower oral-glucose appearance than sucrose in the assessed type 2 diabetes tracer/clamp comparison. The lower systemic appearance also included endogenous-production and first-pass uptake changes; systemic appearance is not a direct intestinal transport measurement. Boundary: Eleven adults with type 2 diabetes under experimental clamp conditions; acute substitution does not establish a need to add sugar or a long-term benefit.
  - [Ang and Linn (2014)](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165%2823%2904787-1/fulltext): Estimated absorption duration was about 50 min longer with isomaltulose; oral appearance was lower, alongside altered endogenous production and first-pass uptake. Access: Primary Table 2 and study design retrieved; clamp, oral appearance and systemic flux distinguished.
  - Context/contrary evidence: Brunner et al. (2012). A 12-week randomized double-blind 50 g/day substitution trial directly tests whether acute effects translate to longer-term HbA1c. No significant HbA1c advantage. This limits the clinical claim, rather than negating the separate acute appearance finding.

- **PM1-F6: Vinegar has a supported but context-dependent gastric-delivery link.** Vinegar with a starchy meal reduced glucose/insulin responses and the paracetamol emptying-marker response in healthy volunteers. This triangulates a possible gastric-delivery mechanism; the marker is indirect and neither absolute emptying nor meal-glucose appearance was quantified. Boundary: Further slowing is not always desirable: an ultrasound pilot found additional delay in people with diabetic gastroparesis.
  - [Liljeberg and Björck (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9630389/): Relative glycaemic and insulin indices were 64 and 65, with a lower paracetamol response consistent with delayed emptying. Access: Primary publisher/PubMed abstract retrieved; full methods not independently extracted.
  - Context/contrary evidence: Hlebowicz et al. (2007). Ten people with type 1 diabetes and gastroparesis; ultrasound showed further emptying delay with vinegar. Defines an adverse-context boundary, not a beneficial entry-rate intervention for this group; pilot and ordered exposure periods.

- **PM1-F7: A fibre-rich food need not slow meal-derived appearance.** Twenty grams of mycoprotein did not change six-hour exogenous appearance in the retrieved mixed-drink tracer trial. Total appearance and disposal changed while meal-derived appearance and blood concentrations did not; a fibre-rich exposure cannot be assumed to slow entry. Boundary: Twenty-four adults, with reliable dual-tracer exogenous estimates in a 12-person subset; the result does not exclude different matrices or doses.
  - [Whelehan et al. (2024)](https://d-nb.info/1348587032/34): Exogenous appearance was unchanged over six hours; total appearance and disposal increased by 5% and 9%, while glucose and insulin concentrations did not differ. Access: Full primary Methods/Results retrieved, including tracer subset correction and Figure 2.


- **PM1-F8: Gastric delivery and intestinal transport are distinct parts of entry.** Controlled glucose delivery experiments and human glucose–galactose malabsorption evidence identify gastric delivery and functional intestinal transport as separate controls. Delivery to the duodenum is upstream of uptake; systemic appearance follows absorption and first-pass handling. Blood concentration additionally depends on endogenous production and disposal. Boundary: These machinery studies do not establish a dietary sodium requirement, a mineral intervention or a KC admission.
  - [Schirra et al. (1996)](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/118411): Delivery profile and GLP-1 responses differed with the route/rate of glucose delivery. Access: Primary publisher/PubMed abstract retrieved; full methods not independently extracted.
  - [Turk et al. (1991)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2008213/): A transporter defect establishes that intestinal glucose uptake requires functional transport machinery. Access: Primary publisher/PubMed abstract retrieved; full methods not independently extracted.


**Search coverage and stopping rationale:** Queried tracer glucose appearance, cereal processing and starch digestibility, matrix/mastication, cooking/cooling, beta-glucan/guar/psyllium formulations, protein preloads, vinegar and delayed-emptying contexts, isomaltulose, mycoprotein, intestinal transporter defects and controlled gastric/duodenal delivery. Used primary publisher/PubMed records and full primary PDFs where available; publisher/PMC access blocks and abstract-only sources are explicitly recorded. The defining release/delivery/digestion/uptake links are now assessed. No systematic-review or comprehensive dose-response claim. Broad extrapolations remain unresolved rather than rejecting an entire class. The newer [Rogowicz-Frontczak et al. (2026) trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41978202/) was screened from its primary abstract/discussion: 32 pump-treated adults with type 1 diabetes, measured resistant starch and interstitial glucose, but no appearance flux. Lower excursions included an earlier—not later—peak (65 versus 125 min). It therefore supports preparation responsiveness but cannot substantiate a universal slowing claim. Full-method access was blocked; preparation-specific digestible dose, insulin context and longer observation remain follow-up questions. No new separate admission was derived from it.

Primary findings and measured/inferred links:

- **PM2-F1: Short training reduces measured excursions.** Seven days of aerobic training reduced excursion frequency, magnitude and duration in type 2 diabetes. Continuous monitoring tests variability more directly than one fasting value. Boundary: Small before/after study; no randomized inactive control or cognitive endpoint.
  - [Mikus et al. (2012)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22311420/): Primary abstract/results retrieved; full methods not independently extracted unless noted. CGM excursions decreased without measurable fitness or body-composition change. Limitations: No parallel control; do not infer brain energy stability.
- **PM2-F2: A meal protocol affects acute glucose variability.** Fish before rice reduced within-meal glucose variability in the diabetes group. This tests the excursion pattern, while PM1 considers its upstream emptying link. Boundary: Acute sequence-specific effects do not establish day-long stability.
  - [Kuwata et al. (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4742500/): Primary Methods/Results retrieved. Glucose standard deviation was lower with fish first in the diabetes group. Limitations: The healthy comparison and long-term response must not be assumed equivalent.
- **PM2-F3: Variability and oxidative stress are associated.** An observational diabetes study linked glucose fluctuation measures with urinary oxidative-stress markers. The association motivates assessment; it does not demonstrate that flattening glucose improves cognition. Boundary: Nonrandomized association; marker and causal endpoint differ.
  - [Monnier et al. (2006)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16609090/): Primary abstract/results retrieved; full methods not independently extracted unless noted. Greater excursions correlated with oxidative-stress marker excretion. Limitations: Do not label this an induced-glucose-fluctuation experiment; inherited key has a wrong PMID.
- **PM2-F4: Sitting breaks lower a meal response without proving a volatility endpoint.** Brief walking interruptions reduced postprandial glucose and insulin exposure versus continuous sitting. This supports activity-related meal responsiveness; AUC alone does not measure volatility. Boundary: Nineteen overweight/obese adults, acute laboratory comparison; no synthesis/disposal tracer.
  - [Dunstan et al. (2012)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3329818/): Full primary methods and results retrieved. Post-meal glucose and insulin AUC were lower. Limitations: Do not substitute average exposure for CGM variability or insulin sensitivity.

KC2: **unresolved definition/applicability; audit-only**. The canonical review finds a broad micronutrient/lipid checklist rather than a coherent shared pool. This does not reject individually supported nutrient relationships. No iKC is invented or admitted by association. Public §3.1.3 uses “No mapping established.”
