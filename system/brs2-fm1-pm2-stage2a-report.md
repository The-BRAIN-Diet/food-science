# BRS2-FM1-PM2 Betaine/BHMT Remethylation — Stage 2A Report

## Scope

Stage 2A treated the existing page prose and the four attached references as propositions, not as established answers. The mission defined the intended scope. It was not treated as evidence that the mechanism achieves that objective.

Attached keys reviewed first: `tao_huang_effect_2015`, `olthof_low_2003`, `derbyshire_role_2023`, `millichap_diet_2012`.

None of those four establishes the BHMT reaction, the zinc site, or choline oxidation. Targeted retrieval was therefore required for those three questions.

## Propositions and dispositions

| Proposition | Disposition | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| BHMT transfers a methyl group from betaine to homocysteine, forming methionine and dimethylglycine, without folate or B12 in that reaction | Supported | Evans et al. (2002); Breksa and Garrow (1999); Obeid (2013). PM2-F1. |
| Zinc is a catalytic metal of human BHMT | Supported as an active-site ion | Evans structures (zinc activates homocysteine; Cys217–Cys299 disulfide expels zinc). Breksa: Cys217, Cys299 and Cys300 are zinc ligands; alanine substitution abolishes activity. Not evidence that dietary zinc controls the enzyme. |
| Choline oxidation to betaine is an upstream supply route, not the BHMT reaction | Supported | Obeid (2013): irreversible oxidation by choline dehydrogenase and betaine-aldehyde dehydrogenase. Salvi and Gadda (2013): human choline dehydrogenase makes betaine aldehyde; the aldehyde dehydrogenase completes betaine. PM2-F2. |
| Olthof plasma homocysteine lowering is tissue BHMT flux or a clinical benefit | Not supported | Olthof et al. (2003) measured plasma homocysteine in healthy adults. No enzyme activity, traced flux, attention or cognition endpoint. PM2-IC1. |
| Plasma homocysteine is specific to BHMT | Contradicted as a reading of the biomarker | Huang et al. (2015) lowered plasma homocysteine with vitamin B12 and fish oil, without betaine. da Costa et al. (2005) raised post-load homocysteine after choline depletion without measuring BHMT, and mouse liver betaine did not fall. PM2-IC1. |
| Inadequate betaine availability, including conditional failure of choline provision, can constrain BHMT-dependent remethylation reserve | Supported with limits | Da Costa et al. (2005) supplies the human depletion → lower betaine → impaired challenge response → repletion edge. Setoue et al. (2008) adds hepatic-betaine depletion and choline/betaine rescue in rats; Strakova et al. (2011) adds a specific BHMT-inhibition perturbation with the expected homocysteine and SAM effects. Human BHMT biochemistry supplies the reaction link. PM2-F5. |
| Folate inadequacy directly constrains BHMT | Not supported as a direct reaction claim; indirect interaction unresolved | Folate is not a BHMT reactant. In folate-deprived rats, Liu et al. (2012) measured lower hepatic betaine, much higher dimethylglycine and partial correction by betaine despite increased ex-vivo BHMT activity. In-vivo inhibition was inferred rather than measured, and human applicability was not tested. |
| Separation from PM1, PM3 MAT, PM4 flux, PM7 PEMT and BRS1 acetylcholine | Supported as boundaries | PM1 is methionine synthase (folate and B12). PM3 is methionine plus ATP to SAMe. PM4 is combined cycle throughput. PM7 is SAMe-dependent phosphatidylethanolamine methylation. Acetylcholine uses unoxidised choline. |
| ADHD diet reviews are direct BHMT outcome evidence | Not supported | Millichap and Yee (2012) review restriction diets, fatty acids, iron and zinc. Derbyshire and Maes (2023) review choline in neurodevelopment. Neither tests BHMT or betaine supplementation against attention or clarity. PM2-F3 and PM2-F4. |

## Retrieval questions and stopping rationale

Questions:

1. What reaction does human BHMT catalyse, and does that reaction use folate or vitamin B12?
2. Is zinc a metal of human BHMT, and what happens to activity if the ligands are removed?
3. Is choline oxidised to betaine upstream of BHMT, rather than inside the BHMT reaction?
4. Does the Olthof trial measure tissue BHMT flux or a clinical outcome?
5. Do the attached ADHD and choline reviews test this reaction against attention or clarity?
6. Does a non-betaine intervention also move plasma homocysteine, so the biomarker cannot be read as BHMT-specific?
7. Does the da Costa depletion study contain a supported chain from nutrient inadequacy through donor availability to challenged remethylation capacity even without a tissue-flux assay?
8. Does folate inadequacy have a material indirect interaction with BHMT, rather than only pathway proximity?
9. Does the methionine–cysteine pool constrain BHMT capacity, or does methionine restriction elicit compensation?
10. Can targeted pool-rescue and BHMT-inhibition evidence narrow the attribution left open by the human plasma endpoint?

Stopping rationale: the reaction, zinc ligands, choline-oxidation edge, plasma-homocysteine boundary and phenome gap were each adjudicated. The focused follow-up re-read the complete da Costa intervention rather than treating its blood endpoint in isolation, then retrieved targeted primary studies for folate, methionine, hepatic-betaine rescue and specific BHMT inhibition. That was sufficient to distinguish (a) an established betaine/choline constraint chain, (b) an unresolved indirect folate interaction and (c) non-application of the methionine–cysteine pool. A direct human isotope-resolved BHMT assay could narrow attribution but is not required by the current Type D contract. Further food-composition searches or general supplement trials would not change these decisions.

Coverage is sufficient for Stage 2A. Schema validation is not that completion.

## Connections

§5.3 sibling links now state the relationship:

- PM1: parallel methionine-synthase route; folate and B12 are not cofactors of BHMT.
- PM3: methionine from BHMT can enter the later adenosyltransferase step; BHMT does not catalyse that step.
- PM4: this PM is one remethylation step, not combined cycle throughput.

§5.2 keeps BRS1-FM2-PM6. Choline oxidation supplies betaine; unoxidised choline is the acetylcholine substrate; betaine is not converted back to choline.

The previous BRS1-FM3-PM7 neuronal-membrane DHA link was removed. Its sentence described homocysteine disposal through transsulfuration, which is not a BHMT–DHA relationship and is not this reaction. No replacement cross-BRS link was invented. §5.1 records only the methionine handoff into SAMe formation.

## Unresolved after Stage 2A

- Whether ordinary betaine or choline intake changes human tissue BHMT flux outside controlled depletion.
- Whether folate inadequacy constrains human BHMT capacity indirectly through betaine/DMG state rather than merely shifting demand between remethylation routes.
- Whether dietary zinc availability limits BHMT in people.
- Direct phenome evidence for attention or cognitive clarity.
- Complete kinetic characterisation of purified human choline dehydrogenase (Salvi and Gadda note that the purified active enzyme has been difficult to obtain). The oxidation route is still supported at pathway level.
