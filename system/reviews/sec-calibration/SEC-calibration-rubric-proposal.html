2. Define the proposition before assigning factors

Record the exact claim, population/model, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome, time window and claim type. Distinguish biochemical necessity, dietary supply, metabolic modulation, functional response and clinical benefit. A biochemical claim does not require ADHD trials; a claim of ADHD benefit does require relevant functional evidence. Composite Findings must not inherit a narrow component's score.

Assess the evidence body supporting that proposition. Identify the load-bearing studies, their dependencies and contradictions. Do not average per-study scores or choose the highest study score. If materially different bodies cannot be reconciled without arbitrary weighting, leave the synthesis unscored and state the blocker. Reviewers document factor-specific reasons and evidence locations. Resolve disagreements explicitly rather than averaging reviewers' judgements.

Unknown is not zero. Zero records an assessed limitation, not missing extraction. If allocation methods, uncertainty or cohort independence cannot be established from available evidence, record the factor as unresolved and do not calculate a complete SEC total. Missing confidence intervals do not automatically mean P0: seek standard errors, paired estimates, model uncertainty, raw-data analyses or appropriate biochemical measurement controls. Do not invent intervals from a P value or choose a universal sample-size threshold.

Sample size belongs to informativeness, not automatically to bias protection. Funding alone is not a bias score. Lack of randomisation is not automatically B0 for every scientific claim. A precise null or equivalence claim can have high confidence when its design and bounds actually support that conclusion.

SEC assessment status and provisional acceptance

  • Not scored: no complete defensible D/B/P/R assessment exists; one or more factors may be unresolved.
  • Conditional estimate — under calibration: a numerical working estimate exists, but at least one factor is conditional. It is not an accepted provisional SEC.
  • Codex-assessed provisional SEC: all four factors have defensible evidence-based judgements for the same exact proposition; an identified Codex process has completed and versioned the assessment. Human expert approval is not required.
  • Scientifically reviewed SEC: an identified qualified human subsequently evaluated and accepted the assessment. Record identity, qualifications relevant to the review, date, outcome, assessed record version and changes. Codex completion, user wording approval and passing tests do not establish human scientific review.

Provisional acceptance gate: define the exact proposition; justify all four factors under the existing anchors; proportionately appraise pivotal evidence and attempt bounded recovery; distinguish supporting, contradictory, contextual and unresolved evidence; consider study independence and relevant limitations; record raw score, applicable caps and adjusted score; complete an identified, versioned and traceable Codex assessment in Evidence & Audit (or the existing stage report until canonical integration). Do not require exhaustive retrieval or full access to every historical paper. A documented limitation does not automatically prohibit a provisional score. An unresolved essential factor does. Never replace unknowns with zero/default one or promote a plausible total without completing the gate.

Established factor scores are justified anchor judgements, not measured quantities. Conditional factor estimates name the verification that could change the judgement; unresolved factors lack even a defensible bounded estimate. Keep confidence level, SEC assessment status, existing evidence-review status and correction status independent. Low confidence can be completely assessed; high confidence can await human review. Neither provisional nor human-reviewed SEC automatically changes the PM evidence-review field.

Backward-compatible record: retain existing factors, raw/adjusted totals, rationale, process, source identities and history. In the isolated audit only, add assessmentStatus (not-scored, conditional, codex-provisional, scientifically-reviewed) and optional humanReview; absent status means Not scored, never inferred from a number. Completed scores record factor states and completion explicitly. Structural checks verify declared completeness, not scientific adequacy or human qualifications. This is assessment metadata, not an evidence atom or a canonical Finding schema migration.

4. Proposed caps — policy choices requiring approval

Raw SEC = D + B + P + R, range 0–8. Proposed caps are ceilings, not subtractions. Preserve the raw total and every factor; display an adjusted level only after explicit adjudication. Do not apply additional undocumented downgrades or penalise the same issue twice through a generic deduction.

D0No ordinal confidence for the asserted proposition; display “Insufficient evidence for this claim”, preserving the diagnostic raw total.Bias protection, precision and replication of a different endpoint cannot rescue a claim never tested. This is not automatic rejection under Stage 2B.
D1Adjusted confidence at most 5/8 — Moderate.Prevents a high-confidence label concealing a material inferential bridge. The ceiling of 5 is a proposed governance choice, not an empirically validated boundary.
B0Adjusted confidence at most 2/8 — Very Low.Replication cannot by itself repair a shared critical systematic error. The specific ceiling requires approval.
P0Adjusted confidence at most 4/8 — Low–Moderate.Direct but uninformative evidence should not receive High confidence in a directional/magnitude claim. This is a proposed ceiling; narrowing the proposition requires separate adjudication.
R0No extra decrement; maximum raw total is already 6/8.An exceptionally rigorous, direct, informative single trial can reach 6, not 8. Do not invent a second independent study or double-penalise unreplicated evidence.
Several applicable capsUse the most restrictive approved ceiling; retain all reasons.No arbitrary stacking of deductions. A scope failure or unresolved factor can withhold the ordinal display regardless of arithmetic.

Labels: 1 Extremely Low; 2 Very Low; 3 Low; 4 Low–Moderate; 5 Moderate; 6 Moderate–High; 7 High; 8 Very High. These are ordinal working labels, not probabilities or equal-interval measurements.

Raw zero: If all four factors have actually been assessed as zero, preserve “Calculated raw SEC 0/8 — insufficient evidence.” Do not relabel it Extremely Low (1). Separately display “Not scored — assessment not performed / unresolved factors” where no complete calculation exists. 5. Reviews, dependence and conflicting evidence

  • A systematic review organises and appraises its underlying evidence; it is not an additional independent replication of the included trials. Assess its search, bias and synthesis methods without counting review plus trials twice.
  • Build a study/cohort dependency account using existing evidence-dependency notes. Reanalyses, overlapping participants, shared controls and follow-up reports are not independent datasets. Technical repeats are different from independent laboratories or new cohorts.
  • Several weak studies do not automatically outweigh a rigorous trial. Shared critical bias can justify B0 despite a large study count; selection/publication bias matters even if published results agree.
  • Credible contrary evidence prevents R2 for a uniform benefit claim until differences are explained. Different substitution/addition protocols or endpoints are not automatically contradictory replications. Do not rewrite a canonical claim solely to improve its score.
  • Reproducible heterogeneity may support a separate bounded proposition about context dependence, but requires its own adjudication. No averaging positive and null effects into a confidence score.

Additive-model limitations: the same raw total hides very different weaknesses; a one-point change can cross a verbal boundary; factor judgements remain partly qualitative; equal weights are not validated; directness and precision can interact; an apparently well-replicated result can share systematic bias.
