2. LPC-DHA / MFSD2A barrier transport makes phospholipid carrier form relevant.
3. Phospholipid versus triglyceride delivery can change accretion (Liu).

## Supported claims

- DHA is a structural neuronal-membrane PUFA (McNamara and Carlson).
- LPC-DHA is an MFSD2A transport substrate; phospholipid carriage matters
  (Patrick).
- Phospholipid DHA produced higher gray-matter accretion than triglyceride DHA
  in neonatal piglets (Liu).

## Constrained claims

- Liu is animal delivery-form evidence, not a human dosing rule.
- Patrick and Liu show PC-DHA / phospholipid-DHA carrier form. They do not show
  that choline intake constrains DHA incorporation.
- Huss cannot isolate membrane DHA from magnesium and zinc.

## Rejected claims

- Phospholipid-DHA is a second independent clinical lever or ADHD protocol.
- The attached corpus demonstrates that choline intake limits human neuronal
  DHA incorporation.

## Unresolved claims

- Human habitual-diet confirmation of the porcine phospholipid-versus-triglyceride
  accretion contrast.
- Whether one-carbon / PEMT chemistry constrains this PM (Type D; Stage 2B).

## Questions and stopping rationale

Questions:

1. What structural job does DHA perform in neuronal membranes?
2. What transport chemistry distinguishes phospholipid carriage?
3. Does a controlled comparison show delivery-form accretion differences?
4. Does Huss measure membrane DHA?

propositions. Choline-as-intake-constraint remains unestablished rather than
