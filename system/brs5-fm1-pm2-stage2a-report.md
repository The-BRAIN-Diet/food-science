## Evidence used

- Cani et al. 2007, Diabetes 56(7):1761–1772, doi 10.2337/db06-1491, PMID 17456850. Mice, four weeks. About 2.7-fold on the 72% fat diet; about 1.4-fold on a 40% fat diet, which was not the main arm. Subcutaneous infusion 300 μg/kg/day is not a meal. CD14 mutant mice resisted most features. Animal data, not a human food rule, not a barrier measurement.
- López-Moreno et al. 2017, J Agric Food Chem 65(35):7756–7763, doi 10.1021/acs.jafc.7b01909, PMID 28793772. 75 adults with metabolic syndrome, four diets for 12 weeks, then a matching fat challenge. Four-hour plasma lipopolysaccharide rose after the saturated-fat challenge only. Binding protein unchanged. Absorption is inferred.
- Ghanim et al. 2009, Diabetes Care 32(12):2281–2287, doi 10.2337/dc09-0979, PMID 19755625. Healthy lean adults, n=10 per meal. A 910 kcal high-fat high-carbohydrate meal raised plasma lipopolysaccharide; an American Heart Association meal rich in fibre and fruit did not. Small, acute, mixed. Not a fibre isolation and not a replication of the saturated-fat-only result.
- Mohammad and Thiemermann 2021, existing `mohammad_role_2021`. Narrative review. Connected/supportive. Mixes barrier leak with circulating lipopolysaccharide. Not the measurement of the 2.7-fold result.
- Jiang 2018 and Brown 2025, existing keys. Brown: 411 Danish 18-year-olds, glycoprotein acetylation and a tryptophan pathway, not lipopolysaccharide.
- Batey 2024, existing key. Perspective on administered lipopolysaccharide and monoamines. Not dietary containment.

## Retrieval and stopping

Primary records: PubMed 17456850 (Cani), 28793772 (López-Moreno), 19755625 (Ghanim; journal line confirmed from the PubMed summary as Diabetes Care 2009, doi 10.2337/dc09-0979).

Further fibre and binding-protein trials were not read in full and were not added as findings. They were enough to stop a fibre requirement: a prebiotic secondary analysis (PMID 39733508) did not change lipopolysaccharide-binding protein overall; a six-month Mediterranean or healthy-eating trial (PMID 25903259) did not change mean serum binding protein; a cross-sectional fibre association (PMID 36791082) is not a requirement. Stopping here avoids treating abstracts as primary study assessments.

Intestinal alkaline phosphatase was not admitted. Bates 2007 (zebrafish) and later alkaline-phosphatase work show dephosphorylation toward less-toxic lipid A in some settings. Komazin and colleagues (Journal of Biological Chemistry, substrate structure–activity) report that calf intestinal alkaline phosphatase has only minimal activity on unmodified enteric lipopolysaccharide; phosphate release was largely from labile phosphoethanolamine modifications, and the 1- and 4′-lipid A phosphates become susceptible after de-O-acylation. That is a reason to leave the step unresolved, not a public mechanism.
