# BRS1-FM1-PM1 Amino-Acid Availability & Prioritisation — Stage 2A Overview Report

## Scope

This pass applied the current **Evidence-supported Overview (Stage 2A)**
contract to `### Overview` only. It did not perform or claim completion of a
full Stage 2A mechanism review. PM1 still lacks authored Scientific Finding
objects, so the broader Stage 2A Finding, pathway, Phenome and connection review
remains open.

The Mission defined intended scope but was not treated as evidence. Existing
Overview prose, frontmatter, Stage 2B classifications and attached references
were assessed as propositions rather than inherited authority.

## Claim-by-claim adjudication

| Overview proposition | Disposition | Evidence and consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Food protein supplies indispensable amino acids, including phenylalanine and tryptophan | Supported at dietary-provision ceiling | Mariotti et al. (2019) addresses protein and amino-acid adequacy; Moughan and Lim (2024) establishes digestible indispensable-amino-acid scoring. |
| Phenylalanine can be converted to tyrosine | Supported | Matthews (2007) establishes human phenylalanine and tyrosine kinetics. |
| PM1 ends before blood–brain transport and neurotransmitter conversion | Supported boundary | Fernstrom (2013) locates competitive LNAA brain entry at LAT1; Aquili (2020) treats tyrosine and tryptophan as downstream monoamine precursors. |
| Protein quantity, quality and distribution across the day set the neurotransmitter-precursor ceiling | Partly supported; narrowed | Quality and indispensable-amino-acid coverage are supported. The attached distribution studies concern protein utilisation and whole-body balance, not PM1-specific neurotransmitter-precursor availability. Distribution was removed from the Overview. |
| Inconsistent or incomplete intake causes every downstream monoaminergic pathway to inherit a shortfall | Overstated; removed | Substrate dependency is biologically relevant, but the universal causal wording exceeds the assessed provision evidence and bypasses transport and conversion controls. |
| Extra protein raises brain monoamines or improves ADHD | Not established | The provision and protein-quality sources do not measure either outcome. The public copy states this limitation without converting absence of evidence into evidence of no effect. |

## Public Overview result

The rewritten paragraph is 67 words before citations. It answers the
mechanism's job, defining provision boundary and dietary claim ceiling. Exactly
three non-duplicative bullets provide:

1. the mechanism boundary;
2. the evidence and measurement boundary; and
3. biological relevance separated from supplementation or ADHD benefit.

Readable citations are claim-local and resolve to PM references [1], [2], [4],
[10] and [11]. The frontmatter `summary` was aligned with the adjudicated
paragraph without embedding citation markup.

Rendered local verification confirmed one explanatory paragraph, exactly three
bullets and valid targets for every Overview citation link.

## Coverage and stopping rationale

All references already attached to PM1 were screened for the propositions
needed by the Overview. Mariotti et al. (2019), Moughan and Lim (2024),
Matthews (2007), Fernstrom (2013) and Aquili (2020) adequately establish the
limited public claims: dietary provision and protein quality, the
phenylalanine-to-tyrosine relationship, precursor identity, and the boundary at
brain transport and downstream conversion.

No targeted external retrieval was needed for this Overview-only pass because
unsupported distribution and universal-shortfall claims were removed rather
than preserved through adjacent evidence. Search stopped once every retained
substantive statement was either supported by an attached source or expressed
as a limitation of what the provision evidence measures.
