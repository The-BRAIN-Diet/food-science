# BRS3-FM1-PM2 Stage 2A — 6 October 2026

## Question

Does this page govern endotoxin crossing the gut barrier, or fermentation products?

## Corpus

Mohammad and Thiemermann 2021 review diet-linked metabolic endotoxemia: a changed gut lining can let lipopolysaccharide into blood, and that lipopolysaccharide activates Toll-like receptor 4. The review suggests dietary interventions. It does not test one.

Cavaliere 2022 gave butyrate to obese mice and saw a partial fall in cortical neuroinflammation. It did not measure lipopolysaccharide translocation, and it is not a fibre feeding study.

Grüter 2023 is propionate in peripheral nerve, not the blood–brain barrier. The public reference label had named the wrong organ. Hoyles 2018 is the propionate blood–brain barrier paper. It does not measure endotoxin spillover, and it does not show propionate stimulating norepinephrine. That phenome sentence was corrected. The confidence score was not changed.

Li 2024 is sodium butyrate in a lead-neuroinflammation model. It is not fibre and not translocation.

## Search

Koh et al. 2016 (Cell 165:1332–1345) establish that gut bacteria ferment dietary fibre to short-chain fatty acids, including butyrate. That fact was retrieved because the page had been using the wrong papers for it.

Human fibre and endotoxin: a prebiotic trial in young adults who ate little fibre did not lower lipopolysaccharide-binding protein overall (a subgroup with high baseline values fell). A fibre meta-analysis reported lower serum lipopolysaccharide with extreme heterogeneity and no change in lipopolysaccharide-binding protein. Neither clears a requirement that fibre lowers translocation.

## Findings

- PM2-F1. Barrier change can raise plasma lipopolysaccharide and Toll-like receptor 4 signalling. Primary: Mohammad and Thiemermann 2021.
- Koh 2016 and Cavaliere 2022 are connected support. They are excluded as translocation evidence. The old PM2-F2, which treated butyrate and propionate experiments as fibre provision, is withdrawn.
