# PM8 Stage 2A/2B endogenous-dependency reassessment

**Stage 2A/2B draft — not approved.** 5 October 2026. This supersedes the previous draft's dietary decisions, not its retained source evidence. Preview: http://localhost:3112/docs/system/stage2b-preview/brs1-fm4-pm8-gaba-glutamate-stage2ab-draft

Mission preserved verbatim: **“Keep excitatory glutamate and inhibitory GABA signalling matched so neural networks operate with stability.”** Canonical PM8, other PMs, FM roll-ups, shared schemas/instructions and accepted review status remain unchanged.

## Diagnosis of the previous pass

**Execution failure, not a contract defect:** the microbiome-focused search displaced the foundational coverage assessment. The draft still mentioned circuit regulation, so it did not literally rename the mission; its dietary candidate assessment nevertheless narrowed neuronal B6/PLP and glutamate/glutamine questions to microbial production. “Brain GABA synthesis chemistry is separately assessed in PM9” became an invalid stopping rationale. The current contract's **Foundational dietary coverage and targeted retrieval** requires checking omitted substrates/cofactors against the mission. Sibling ownership permits evidence reuse; it does not prohibit PM8 applicability.

**Admission reasoning error:** the report's “oral exposure→measured central GABA-related outcome” argument prematurely applied Direct distance classification. Step 3 of the **Relationship-first workflow** explicitly asks whether the endpoint represents **this PM-governed state**. “At the level claimed” permits bounded science; it does not permit replacing matched signalling with a metabolite concentration and then calling that a requirement. `defined dietary exposure` is a descriptor, not admission evidence. Type B remains a valid contract category; it was misapplied in this draft, rather than prohibited globally.

**No universal efficacy rule was found, but its practical effect was an omission:** the previous pass did not explicitly state that endogenous dependencies require a successful supplementation trial. It simply left them unadjudicated while treating PM9 ownership and lack of demonstrated circuit correction as reasons not to fill the coverage gap. The contract says, under **Claim thresholds**, “Do not require clinical benefit to establish a supported dependency”; under §6.1 it says that absence of intervention/dose-response evidence must not remove a legitimate capacity relationship. These clauses now govern the endogenous decisions.

**Evidence gaps are separate:** oral-to-central tracing, normal-intake adequacy thresholds, benefit in replete humans and microbial mediation remain unresolved where not measured. These gaps limit stronger claims; they do not erase enzyme necessity. **Rendering/validation:** valid five-atom records did not prevent the earlier scientific classification error. No shared renderer or architecture change is required to correct it.

## Stage 2A: the bounded endogenous chain

1. **Synthesis:** intracellular glutamate → PLP-dependent GAD65/67 → GABA. Human enzyme work supports B6-vitamer conversion to PLP. Nutritional B6 deficiency experiments affect GAD and GABA-T activities and regional pools; this is not selective enhancement of inhibition. New PM8-F11.
2. **Supply, recycling and release:** astrocytic glutamine synthesis/export and neuronal uptake/conversion replenish glutamate available for vesicular GABA synthesis. Liang's activity-dependent inhibitory-current blockade/rescue experiment connects compartment-specific supply to inhibitory efficacy, not merely to a bulk biomarker. New PM8-F12.
3. **Clearance:** astrocytic GLT-1 uptake limits extracellular glutamate. Knockout instability supports the need for clearance; it does not identify a dietary input. New PM8-F13. PM10 remains the detailed clearance owner.
4. **Signalling regulation:** KCC2-dependent chloride handling, receptor responses and synaptic feedback remain PM8-F4; magnesium's voltage-dependent NMDA block is assessed separately in PM8-F16. Neither becomes a food-intake requirement from a bath-ion experiment.
5. **Specific additional route:** riboflavin → FMN → PNPO-dependent B6-phosphate conversion → PLP. New PM8-F14. Direct pyridoxal phosphorylation bypasses PNPO, so this is conditional route support, not universal dependence of every PLP molecule on that step. Thiamin-deficiency/shunt evidence is assessed in PM8-F15 without generating a generic energy/B-vitamin requirement.

These are load-bearing contributors to the mission. PM8 is not expanded into every enzyme, vesicle protein or energy pathway. PM9 and PM10 retain their distinct functions; their evidence is used to explain dependencies rather than importing their nutrient lists.

## Intervention qualification and placement

**Diet-Supported** is retained as the inherited, limited evidence qualification, now supported by B6 provision chemistry and deficiency influence on the inhibitory arm, not by counting oral studies. The spreadsheet allows inheritance and provides no quantitative threshold distinguishing this label from alternatives. Its limited definition is recorded rather than silently expanded. There is no basis for Diet-Dominant or equal route dominance. Principal-route selection remains **not established**; dependency does not show intervention responsiveness in replete humans.

Existing conflict remains: the opt-in schema layout puts groups in §3 when no principal route is established, while the user's standing training instruction puts admitted dietary requirements before Overview. The draft preserves its existing §1 placement, correct visible numbering and canonical IDs; the qualification/selection record is separately stored in `dominance-assessment.json`. No contract or scientific ranking is changed to solve presentation. This integration limitation remains explicit.

## Evidence coverage, access and stopping rationale

Read current governing contract/schema, actual PM8/PM9/PM10/FM4 records and relevant KC definitions. Reused 18 previously appraised microbial/intervention sources; retrieved eleven additional primary sources for enzyme conversion, nutritional deficiency, inhibitory recycling/release, clearance, intestinal glutamate handling and ionic regulation. These access gaps prevent detailed dose, population or full mechanistic-effect appraisal, but do not negate the explicitly reported reaction and blockade/rescue results.
