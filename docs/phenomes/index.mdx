# BRAIN Phenome Registry

## From Biological Regulation to Functional Expression

The BRAIN Framework examines how interconnected biological regulatory systems contribute to cognitive, emotional, behavioural and adaptive functioning.

The Phenome Registry organises these functional expressions into **15 active phenomes from 18 defined**, including attention, motivation, emotional regulation, stress resilience, sleep regulation and cognitive energy stability.

PH007, PH012 and PH013 are retained as retired historical records.

Each phenome is connected to relevant Process Mechanisms (PMs) across the six Biological Regulatory Systems (BRSs), allowing shared biological contributions, functional relationships and evidence gaps to be examined without reducing complex outcomes to individual mechanisms.

## Shared Functional Domains Across Conditions

**A central feature of the BRAIN phenome approach is that functional domains can be relevant across multiple clinical conditions without being defined by those diagnoses.**

ADHD, anxiety and depression frequently overlap clinically and may involve difficulties in some of the same functional domains. Rather than creating separate phenomes for each disorder, BRAIN examines these shared dimensions while preserving the distinctions between the conditions and their underlying biology.

### Why this matters for BRAIN

These overlapping domains allow BRAIN to investigate how different Biological Regulatory Systems may contribute to similar functional difficulties across diagnostic boundaries.

For example, stress resilience, perseverative thought and emotional regulation can be relevant to ADHD presentations accompanied by anxiety or depressive symptoms. Pleasure and interest capacity provides another functional lens through which motivational and depressive difficulties can be examined.

**This approach enables investigation of shared functional targets without treating ADHD, anxiety and depression as the same disorder or assuming that they respond to the same dietary intervention.**

## Phenome Development and Evidence Governance

The registry was developed within the BRAIN Framework and benchmarked against established research approaches, including the NIH Research Domain Criteria (RDoC), to examine construct coverage, distinguish overlapping domains and refine functional definitions.

RDoC informs this comparison but does not define the BRAIN taxonomy.

Mechanism-to-phenome relationships undergo evidence review, distinguishing biological relevance from the confidence and evidence level supporting each connection. A mapped relationship does not establish that changing dietary intake will improve the associated functional outcome.
