### Overview

Food protein supplies indispensable amino acids—the amino acids the body cannot make in sufficient amounts—including phenylalanine and tryptophan. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Phenylalanine can also be converted to tyrosine. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) After a protein meal, dietary phenylalanine appears in the circulation. [[6]](#pm-ref-6) Tyrosine and tryptophan are precursors later signalling can use for dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin. [[2]](#pm-ref-2) Protein that covers those amino acids is the supported dietary route. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)

- **Benefits:** These amino acids, including tyrosine made from phenylalanine, are how meal protein supplies the pool that dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin pathways draw on. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) That capacity is not evidence that extra protein improves attention or mood. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)
- **Implementation Notes:** Indispensable amino-acid supply and tryptophan are the dietary requirements; food protein supplies these amino acids. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Tyrosine is made from phenylalanine, so it is not classed as an indispensable amino acid. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) No neurotransmitter-targeted intake dose is established. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[6]](#pm-ref-6) Each meal does not have to supply the full set. [[5]](#pm-ref-5)
- **Biological Relevance:** Those amino acids still have to cross into the brain before they are used to make dopamine, noradrenaline or serotonin - see [PM2](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation).

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

Phenylalanine-4-hydroxylase (PAH) contributes to endogenous tyrosine supply. [UniProt C1](#pm-curated-pah) and [Rhea C2](#pm-curated-rhea) establish reaction identity, not an independent dietary cofactor admission.


### Summary

Later signalling can use tyrosine and tryptophan only after those amino acids are available in the circulation. A protein meal is what puts dietary phenylalanine there, and phenylalanine can be converted to tyrosine. Brain entry and neurotransmitter synthesis come after that.

#### (Circulating supply)

Gorissen et al. (2020) [[6]](#pm-ref-6) pooled human trials of labelled whey, casein and milk protein. About half of the dietary phenylalanine appeared in the circulation over five hours. Casein delivered a smaller fraction than whey or milk, a larger dose delivered more, and the older subgroup showed a lower fraction appearing within the measurement window. That pooled association does not establish uniform age-related impairment. The measured endpoint is plasma phenylalanine, not brain dopamine, noradrenaline or serotonin.

#### (Coverage across the diet)

Mariotti and Gardner (2019) [[1]](#pm-ref-1) found that adult vegetarian diets which include protein-rich plant foods can meet protein and amino-acid requirements. Moughan and Lim (2024) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) score protein quality by digestible indispensable amino acids, not by grams alone. Marsh et al. (2013) [[5]](#pm-ref-5) state that varied plant proteins across the diet can cover that set, so complementary proteins do not have to share every meal.

#### (Phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan)

Matthews (2007) [[4]](#pm-ref-4) shows that humans convert phenylalanine to tyrosine, mainly in the liver. Tyrosine is normally dispensable because of that conversion, and it can become conditionally indispensable when phenylalanine is limited. Aquili (2020) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) names tryptophan as the serotonin precursor and tyrosine as the dopamine precursor. Those names identify the amino acids in this pool. They do not show that a higher circulating supply raises brain serotonin or dopamine.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

Competitive transport of amino acids across the blood–brain barrier is [BRS1-FM1-PM2 — LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation). Dopamine synthesis and signalling are [BRS1-FM1-PM3 — Dopaminergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm3-dopaminergic-signalling-regulation). Noradrenaline signalling is [BRS1-FM1-PM4 — Noradrenergic Signalling](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm4-noradrenergic-signalling-attention-executive-modulation). Serotonin signalling is [BRS1-FM1-PM5 — Serotonergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm5-serotonergic-signalling-regulation).

#### (Integration within BRS1)

This mechanism supplies the circulating pool at the start of [BRS1(FM1)](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-monoaminergic-function). Later mechanisms in that pathway draw on it. A shared key-constraint mapping is not established.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

A protein meal releases dietary phenylalanine into the circulation. How much arrives depends on protein type, dose and age. Phenylalanine can be converted to tyrosine. Tyrosine and tryptophan are the precursors later pathways use. None of these results shows that extra protein raises brain dopamine, noradrenaline or serotonin. Timing and distribution are assessed separately; the evidence does not establish a universal schedule for optimising this precursor pool.

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS2(FM1) — Methylation Cycle Efficiency](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-methylation-cycle-efficiency) — Methylation uses methionine, another indispensable amino acid supplied by dietary protein. That is a shared amino-acid source, not a control this mechanism exerts on the methylation cycle.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS1-FM1-PM2 - LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation) — The tyrosine and tryptophan in this circulating pool still have to enter the brain. That entry is competitive transport, not this supply step.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM3 - Dopaminergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm3-dopaminergic-signalling-regulation) — Dopamine synthesis uses tyrosine after brain entry, including tyrosine made from phenylalanine. This mechanism supplies that precursor. It does not carry out the synthesis.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM4 - Noradrenergic Signalling](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm4-noradrenergic-signalling-attention-executive-modulation) — Noradrenaline synthesis uses the same tyrosine after brain entry. This mechanism supplies the circulating precursor and does not regulate noradrenaline signalling.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM5 - Serotonergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm5-serotonergic-signalling-regulation) — Serotonin synthesis uses tryptophan from this pool. This mechanism supplies that indispensable amino acid and does not carry out serotonin synthesis.

## Scientific Lesson

Dietary tracer appearance identifies amino acids arriving from the ingested food. Total plasma concentration also reflects endogenous turnover, tissue uptake and clearance. A higher tracer appearance or earlier peak is therefore not automatically a larger usable brain precursor supply, and neither establishes attention or mood benefit.
