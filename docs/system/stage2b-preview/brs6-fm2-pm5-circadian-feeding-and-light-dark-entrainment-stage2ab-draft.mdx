## BRS6-FM2-PM5 — Circadian Feeding & Light–Dark Entrainment

> **Stage 2B draft — not approved.** Independent Stage 2A/2B preview. Canonical science, mappings, ratings and review status are unchanged.

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Ensure feeding and light exposure stay aligned with circadian rhythms across the 24-hour cycle.

**Intervention Dominance:** Lifestyle-Dominant (inherited qualification, retained pending approval). Principal route displayed: System Optimisation Practices. This placement reflects the assessed process, not proven comparative superiority.

### Overview

Daily light and feeding cues help the body keep its internal clocks in step with the outside day. Light primarily informs the central timing system, while meals can shift peripheral metabolic rhythms. Keeping these cues coherent supports coordinated activity, sleep and fuel handling. Human experiments distinguish their effects: delayed meals shifted glucose and adipose-clock timing without shifting cortisol or melatonin, while evening room light suppressed the melatonin signal. Wehrens et al. (2017) [[5]](#pm-ref-5); Gooley et al. (2011) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)

- **Benefits:** Coherent cues help coordinate metabolic timing and the biological night. Wehrens et al. (2017) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)
- **Implementation Notes:** Timed meal scheduling and reduced pre-bed room light address different measured timing signals. Wehrens et al. (2017) [[5]](#pm-ref-5); Gooley et al. (2011) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)
- **Biological Relevance:** A metabolic improvement during restricted feeding does not itself demonstrate clock entrainment.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- Delaying meals shifted glucose and adipose PER2 rhythms in a controlled human protocol. Wehrens et al. (2017) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)
- Room light before bedtime suppressed melatonin onset and shortened its duration. Gooley et al. (2011) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)
