summary: >-
  NAD⁺ and NADH cycle between oxidised and reduced forms during redox reactions.
  Separate synthesis and salvage pathways replenish molecules consumed by
  signalling enzymes, so precursor supply, enzyme regulation and consumption all
  shape availability. Nicotinamide and nicotinic acid provide entry routes;
  tryptophan conversion is predominantly hepatic in the assessed mouse studies.
  These relationships support biological capacity, not a general claim that
  extra intake improves cognition.

  Nicotinamide salvage, the nicotinic-acid route and hepatic tryptophan

    finding_label: Three major pathways maintain the NAD⁺ pool
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: >-
      NAD⁺ accepts electrons to form NADH, and that NADH can be oxidised back to
      NAD⁺. Separately, nicotinamide salvage, the nicotinic-acid route and
      hepatic conversion of tryptophan are major pathways that replenish the
      pool when consuming enzymes break NAD⁺ down.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      These are major maintenance pathways, not every precursor entry.
      Identifying them does not show that eating more of a precursor, once
      intake is adequate, raises NAD⁺ or improves attention.
    finding_statement: >-
      Major pathways that maintain the mammalian NAD⁺ pool are de novo synthesis
      from tryptophan, the Preiss–Handler route from nicotinic acid, and salvage
      of nicotinamide. Most cells outside the liver depend on salvage. These are
      not the only precursor entry routes.
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: >-
      The review describes NAD⁺ as the coenzyme that accepts a hydride in redox
      reactions. It is synthesised de novo from tryptophan through the
      kynurenine pathway, from nicotinic acid by the Preiss–Handler pathway, and
      by salvage of nicotinamide. Outside the liver, most cells do not express
      the full tryptophan route. The liver can convert tryptophan to
      nicotinamide, release it, and leave other cells to salvage that
      nicotinamide. The review states that the share of the pool coming from
      tryptophan is not established. Most NAD⁺ is described as coming from
      nicotinamide salvage.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      This is a review of pathways, not a human feeding trial. It does not
      measure how much an ordinary meal changes NAD⁺, and it does not test
      attention or cognitive energy. Nicotinamide riboside and nicotinamide
      mononucleotide, as supplement doses, are outside this dietary assessment.

### Overview

NAD⁺ and NADH cycle between oxidised and reduced forms during redox reactions. Separate synthesis and salvage pathways replenish molecules consumed by signalling enzymes, so precursor supply, enzyme regulation and consumption all shape availability. Nicotinamide and nicotinic acid provide entry routes; tryptophan conversion is predominantly hepatic in the assessed mouse studies. These relationships support biological capacity, not a general claim that extra intake improves cognition.

* **Redox chemistry:** Redox cycling differs from molecular replenishment Covarrubias et al. (2021) [[1]](#pm-ref-1); Liu et al. (2018) [[12]](#pm-ref-12).
* **Route-specific support:** B6 supports tryptophan conversion; it is not a direct NAD precursor Shibata et al. (1995) [[13]](#pm-ref-13).
* **Evidence boundary:** High-dose myopathy treatment and animal depletion studies do not establish ordinary-intake enhancement or cognitive benefit Pirinen et al. (2020) [[2]](#pm-ref-2).

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

NAD⁺ accepts electrons to form NADH, which can be oxidised back to NAD⁺ [Covarrubias et al. (2021) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. Separately, synthesis and salvage replenish the pool when consuming enzymes break the molecule down [Chang and Guarente (2014) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)]. Nicotinamide salvage, the nicotinic-acid route and hepatic tryptophan conversion are major pathways [Covarrubias et al. (2021) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. In mice, the clock controls the salvage enzyme NAMPT [Ramsey et al. (2009) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)]. High-dose niacin raised NAD⁺ in mitochondrial myopathy [Pirinen et al. (2020) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)]. Training raised the muscle enzyme, not measured NAD⁺ [de Guia et al. (2019) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)].

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Introduction/Summary

NAD⁺ and NADH interconvert in dehydrogenase reactions. Separately, synthesis and salvage replenish the pool when consuming enzymes break NAD⁺ down. Nicotinamide salvage, the nicotinic-acid route and hepatic tryptophan conversion are major pathways, not every precursor entry. The findings separate that chemistry from a high-dose niacin study in mitochondrial myopathy, from a muscle-enzyme response to training, and from the absence of an NAD⁺ measurement in the ADHD papers.

## 5. BRS Pathways and Connections

### 5.1 BRS Pathways

- None listed

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS2-FM1-PM4 - Methionine Cycle Flux](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm4-methionine-cycle-flux) — One-carbon reactions use redox cofactors. No assessed study shows that this turnover changes how much NAD⁺ cells hold.
- [BRS6-FM1-PM2 - Glycaemic Variability Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/fm1/brs6-fm1-pm2-glycaemic-variability-regulation) — Carbohydrate oxidation reduces NAD⁺ to NADH. Control of glucose swings is a separate process, and no assessed study shows that those swings change NAD⁺ availability.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS4-FM1-PM1 - Electron Transport Chain Function](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/fm1/brs4-fm1-pm1-electron-transport-chain-function) — NADH made from this pool is what complex I oxidises. The respiratory complexes are a separate mechanism, and niacin precursors belong with this supply.
- [BRS4-FM1-PM3 - Creatine–Phosphocreatine Energy Buffering](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/fm1/brs4-fm1-pm3-creatine-phosphocreatine-buffer) — Oxidative energy production can supply ATP that this buffer stores and releases. No assessed study shows a serial path from this NAD⁺ pool through the respiratory complexes into that buffer.

## 7. Phenome Connections

These mappings are translational relationships, not single-mechanism outcome claims. Phenomes are emergent functional patterns supported by multiple interacting PMs across the BRAIN Framework.
