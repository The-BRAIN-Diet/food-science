## Defined mechanism

**Canonical mission:** Maintain methionine adenosyltransferase capacity to convert methionine and ATP into SAMe, sustaining the methyl-donor pool used by downstream methyltransferases.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
 M["Methionine + ATP"] -->|"MAT · Mg²⁺ and K⁺"| S["SAMe + phosphate + pyrophosphate"]
```

The diagram identifies selected relationships; it is not a complete stoichiometric or whole-PM inventory. Dashed supply arrows do not assert quantitative conversion yields. [Accepted reaction/pathway evidence](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis#pm3-f1).

## 1.1 Dietary Requirements

Nutrition maintains capacity; the body also produces and regenerates reaction participants. Insufficiency, adequate provision and therapeutic enhancement are separate propositions. This parent heading does not make every participant below a dietary intake requirement.

### 1.1.3 Key Constraints

Existing identifiers, ownership and limitations are retained. An upstream supply or pool connection is not newly classified Direct/Derived. Pools are not additional nutrient quantities.

- **One-carbon methyl-donor pool (folate, betaine, choline) — BRS2(KC1)** (supported-upstream-supply; existing stored relationship): KC1 contributes methyl groups to methionine regeneration through [PM1 — Folate/B12-dependent remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation) and [PM2 — Betaine/BHMT remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation). PM3 uses that regenerated methionine to produce SAMe.

  Limitation: Deficiency and pathway-impairment studies support this upstream connection, with responses varying by tissue and condition. KC1’s limiting effect on MAT-dependent synthesis remains unresolved; SAMe abundance also reflects consumption and turnover. Benefit from additional intake in nutrient-replete individuals is not established. This pool relationship is distinct from PM3’s biochemical reaction requirements.

  [PM3-F4: Upstream donor routes regenerate methionine for SAMe synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis#pm3-f4); [PM3-F1: MAT converts methionine and ATP into SAMe](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis#pm3-f1)

<summary>Existing individual upstream/KC input relationships</summary>

- **Vitamin B9 (folate)** — Folate supplies 5-methyltetrahydrofolate to [PM1 — Folate/B12-dependent remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation), regenerating methionine that PM3 can use to form SAMe. Limitation: This is upstream methionine supply, not a MAT substrate or cofactor. The route depends on functional vitamin B12-dependent methionine synthase; alternative methionine supply and tissue context affect its contribution. Increased MAT flux or benefit from extra folate in replete individuals is not established. [PM3-F4: Upstream donor routes regenerate methionine for SAMe synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis#pm3-f4); [PM3-F1: MAT converts methionine and ATP into SAMe](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis#pm3-f1)

- **Betaine** — Betaine donates a methyl group through [PM2 — Betaine/BHMT remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation) to regenerate methionine used by PM3. Limitation: BHMT activity is concentrated mainly in liver and kidney; this is not local BHMT activity in every tissue. Folate-dependent remethylation and dietary methionine provide alternative supply. Homocysteine lowering does not establish increased MAT flux, increased SAMe availability or benefit from extra intake in replete individuals. [PM3-F4: Upstream donor routes regenerate methionine for SAMe synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis#pm3-f4); [PM3-F1: MAT converts methionine and ATP into SAMe](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis#pm3-f1)

- **Choline** — Choline can be oxidised to betaine, supplying methyl groups through [PM2 — Betaine/BHMT remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation) for methionine regeneration upstream of PM3. Limitation: Only the choline-to-betaine methyl-supply route is admitted here, not choline membrane or neurotransmitter roles. Oxidation, predominantly hepatic/renal BHMT activity and alternative supply constrain interpretation. Increased MAT flux, increased SAMe availability or benefit from additional choline in replete individuals is not established. [PM3-F4: Upstream donor routes regenerate methionine for SAMe synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis#pm3-f4); [PM3-F1: MAT converts methionine and ATP into SAMe](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis#pm3-f1)

## SOP boundary

No structured SOP is admitted on this PM. Showing that extra methionine, a donor protocol or exogenous SAMe modifies function would require a separate intervention assessment; therapeutic SAMe evidence is not evidence of increased endogenous MAT synthesis.
