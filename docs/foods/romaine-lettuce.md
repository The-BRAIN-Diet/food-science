## Overview

Romaine (cos) lettuce is a crisp salad leaf used as a volume base in mixed salads. It contributes **vitamin K**, **folate**, and carotenoids including **beta-carotene**, **lutein** and **zeaxanthin**, at very low energy density.

Within a BRAIN-aligned pattern it is a culinary-support salad vegetable rather than a distinctive neurological food. Its practical role is to carry dressings and other leaves, and to supply those fat-soluble carotenoids when eaten with dietary fat [1,2].

## Food Context

### Synergies

- Serve with a fat-containing dressing, avocado, or olive oil so carotenoids from the salad are absorbed more effectively [1].

### Preparation

- Use raw, washed and thoroughly dried leaves so dressings coat rather than dilute.
- Do not treat romaine as interchangeable with iceberg or mixed-leaf bags; those foods differ in carotenoid and folate density.
