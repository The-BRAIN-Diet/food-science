## Overview

Chicken liver is poultry offal. Raw liver supplies **vitamin A** (3,296 µg RAE per 100 g, nearly all of it retinol), **vitamin B12** (16.6 µg), **folate** (588 µg), **vitamin B2** (1.8 mg), **iron** (9 mg) and **choline** (194.4 mg) [1].

One raw liver weighs 44 g and supplies about 1,450 µg of this vitamin A [1].

## Other Nutritional Highlights

Chicken liver supplies preformed vitamin A, which is retinol and retinyl esters. Beta-carotene from plants is the vitamin A form the body converts into retinol. That conversion is not the reason to limit liver [2].

Preformed retinol is stored. The adult upper level is 3,000 µg a day, and it applies to retinol and retinyl esters from animal foods and supplements. It does not apply to beta-carotene [2]. Raw chicken liver provides 3,296 µg vitamin A per 100 g, so a 100 g portion is already above that daily upper level. One 44 g liver, about 1,450 µg, is below it [1,2].

For liver as a food group, UK guidance is to eat liver or liver products no more than once a week, to avoid them in pregnancy, and not to combine a weekly liver meal with a vitamin A supplement [3].
