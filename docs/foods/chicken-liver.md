## Overview

Chicken liver is poultry offal. Raw liver supplies **vitamin A** (3,296 µg RAE per 100 g, nearly all of it retinol), **vitamin B12** (16.6 µg), **folate** (588 µg), **vitamin B2** (1.8 mg), **iron** (9 mg) and **choline** (194.4 mg) [1].

One raw liver weighs 44 g and supplies about 1,450 µg of this vitamin A [1].

## Other Nutritional Highlights

Chicken liver supplies preformed vitamin A, which is retinol and retinyl esters. Beta-carotene from plants is the vitamin A form the body converts into retinol. That conversion is not the reason to limit liver [2].

Preformed retinol is stored. The adult upper level is 3,000 µg a day, and it applies to retinol and retinyl esters from animal foods and supplements. It does not apply to beta-carotene [2]. Raw chicken liver provides 3,296 µg vitamin A per 100 g, so a 100 g portion is already above that daily upper level. One 44 g liver, about 1,450 µg, is below it [1,2].

For liver as a food group, UK guidance is to eat liver or liver products no more than once a week, to avoid them in pregnancy, and not to combine a weekly liver meal with a vitamin A supplement [3].

## Food Context

### Preparation

- Use it fresh and cook it through. Liver is perishable.

### Cooked liver: a 150 g comparison

Cooking can increase nutrient concentrations per 100 g through water loss, but retention and cooking method also matter. These are separate USDA cooked-food records, not a raw-to-cooked conversion or a recommended portion. Calf liver is not represented by the beef record.

| Nutrient | 150 g chicken liver, pan-fried | 150 g beef liver, braised | Adult reference guidance | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retinol (preformed vitamin A) | Approximately **6,440 µg** | Approximately **14,140 µg** | EFSA adult upper level: **3,000 µg RE/day** from preformed vitamin A [5] | Both portions exceed the daily upper level. This does not by itself establish acute toxicity, but neither portion can be certified safe weekly by averaging across seven days. |
| Copper | Approximately **0.80 mg** | Approximately **21.4 mg** | EFSA acceptable daily intake: **0.07 mg/kg body weight/day** (approximately 4.9 mg/day for a 70 kg adult) [6] | Beef liver is particularly concentrated. This daily guidance is not a weekly allowance or a single-meal toxicity threshold. |
| Purines | Not established for this exact preparation | Not established for this exact preparation | No general adult weekly safe limit established by the cited sources | Do not assign a “safe” verdict from an assumed 400 mg/day allowance. Gout guidance advises avoiding or limiting organ meats. [7] |

**Basis:** Amounts are calculated as the USDA per-100-g values × 1.5: chicken retinol 4,293 µg and copper 0.535 mg; beef retinol 9,428 µg and copper 14.283 mg. These are retinol values, not total vitamin A RAE. Composition varies between sources and preparations. [4]

**Frequency and precautions:** EFSA's 2024 assessment associates liver/offal consumption once a month or less with a low likelihood of exceeding the vitamin A upper level in European populations; it does not exempt chicken liver. NHS advice uses a different, no-more-than-weekly limit. Neither establishes that this specific 150 g portion is safe for everyone. Avoid liver and liver products during pregnancy or when planning pregnancy. Consider other dietary and supplemental preformed vitamin A. [5]
