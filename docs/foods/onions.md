## Overview

White onion contains fructooligosaccharides and raffinose-family oligosaccharides. The published 2.24 ± 0.092 g per 100 g fresh weight is not assigned to either class, and it is not a combined fructo-oligosaccharide and galacto-oligosaccharide amount [3]. Onions also provide quercetin (polyphenol) and sulfur compounds that support gut microbiome, antioxidant defenses, and glutathione synthesis.

The white-onion record names fructooligosaccharides and raffinose-family oligosaccharides. It is not an inulin amount, not conventional beta-galacto-oligosaccharides, and not a butyrate yield [3]. Quercetin is abundant in capers, apples, onions, berries, kale and soybeans.

## Food Context

### Synergies

- The white-onion oligosaccharide record is not an inulin amount and not a butyrate yield [3]
- Pair with other allium vegetables for synergistic effects

### Preparation

- Can be consumed raw or cooked; cooking may enhance some compounds
- Sulphur-containing vegetables that provide glutathione precursors (e.g., broccoli, Brussels sprouts, garlic, onions) support antioxidant defenses
