## Overview

Anchovies are a small oily fish most often eaten as canned, drained fillets. That form provides long-chain omega-3 fatty acids **EPA** and **DHA**, a complete protein, **niacin**, **selenium**, and useful **calcium**, together with a high **sodium** load from curing [1].

Within a BRAIN-aligned pattern they are a compact, shelf-stable marine omega-3 seasoning and protein rather than a large fillet portion. Typical salad or dressing amounts are small; the acids still match the drained canned record rather than an assumed fresh fish [1,2].

## Food Context

### Sourcing

- This page is canned European anchovy in oil, drained. Fresh, salted, or undrained packed fish are different foods.
- Drain the packing oil before weighing. Prefer fillets packed in olive oil over refined oils where that choice exists.
- Sodium is high. Use as a seasoning-scale ingredient unless the dish is built around a larger portion.

### Preparation

- Mash or blend drained fillets into dressings rather than frying them in additional oil.
- Taste the dish before adding salt; anchovies, capers and cheese often already season it.
