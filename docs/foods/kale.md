## Overview

Kale is a leafy green providing vitamin C, vitamin K, calcium, folate, riboflavin and manganese. Its carotenoids include beta-carotene and lutein. Zeaxanthin has been separated in supermarket kale on a dry-matter basis [2]. The 6,261 µg per 100 g on this page is lutein plus zeaxanthin together, not that zeaxanthin value [3]. Quercetin and kaempferol are also recorded as present.

Use it raw, cooked or blended into meals, with preparation chosen for texture and taste.

## Food Context

### Synergies

- Pair with fat (avocado, olive oil) for carotenoid absorption; co-consuming a small amount of unsaturated fat improves micelle formation and chylomicron packaging
- Part of diverse leafy green strategy
- Practical pairings: carrots + tahini, kale + avocado, berries + yogurt/nuts

### Preparation

- Boiling reduces oxalates and improves mineral bioavailability [Chai and Liebman 2005](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#chai_effect_2005)
