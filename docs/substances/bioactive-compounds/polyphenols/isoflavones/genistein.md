## Overview

Genistein is an isoflavone polyphenol found primarily in soybeans and soy products. In laboratory preparations, genistein and daidzein altered anandamide uptake [Thors et al. 2007](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#thors_inhibition_2007). The [FAAH-mediated endocannabinoid preservation](/docs/biological-targets/brs-x/ecs/fm1/brs-x-ecs-pm3-faah-mediated-endocannabinoid-preservation) mechanism records that finding. It does not establish that soy foods inhibit neural FAAH. Genistein also exhibits strong antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, protecting neurons from oxidative stress and neuroinflammation. It can cross the blood-brain barrier and has been studied for its potential neuroprotective effects in various neurological conditions.

## References

- Genistein and daidzein altered anandamide uptake under the tested laboratory conditions. This does not establish dietary soy inhibition of neural FAAH [Thors et al. 2007](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#thors_inhibition_2007)
- Polyphenols such as genistein may further enhance ECS tone by inhibiting fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH), the enzyme responsible for anandamide degradation [Gibellini and Smith 2010](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#gibellini_kennedy_2010)
- Genistein may amplify the ECS's ability to regulate dopamine, glutamate, and GABA signaling, all key pathways to regulate in ADHD
- Genistein has been confirmed to alleviate the deleterious effects of oxidative stress on neuronal injury, preventing neuronal death, increasing hippocampal glutathione and superoxide dismutase production, and lowering lipid peroxidation and reactive oxygen species [Fuloria et al. 2022](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#fuloria_genistein_2022)
- Genistein is believed to pass the blood–brain barrier and is extensively applied in the investigation of neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's and Huntington's [Fuloria et al. 2022](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#fuloria_genistein_2022)
