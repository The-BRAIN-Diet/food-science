## Overview

Ellagic acid is a polyphenol released when ellagitannins are hydrolysed. It is the compound some gut bacteria convert toward urolithins. [Urolithin A](/docs/substances/microbial-metabolites/secondary-plant-conversions/urolithin-a) is a downstream metabolite, not ellagic acid, and a food that supplies ellagic acid does not contain urolithin A.

Two human intestinal Gordonibacter species converted ellagic acid into intermediate urolithins in culture. They did not establish complete urolithin A production by those two species alone [1]. In healthy adults, urolithin A exposure from pomegranate juice varied, while direct urolithin A administration bypassed conversion [2]. Conversion depends on the microbial community and may stop at intermediates. It does not imply that everyone converts ellagic acid, or that ellagic acid itself increased mitochondrial function [1,2].

## Dietary Origin

Ellagic acid can occur in foods after ellagitannins hydrolyse, and it is the substrate given to the Gordonibacter cultures [1]. Pomegranate juice in the human comparison is a food matrix that can yield variable urolithin A, not a measured dose of isolated ellagic acid [2]. Direct urolithin A supplementation skips the conversion and is evidence about the metabolite, not about ellagic acid [2]. No food page is given a contains link that would treat juice, ellagic acid, and urolithin A as the same substance.
