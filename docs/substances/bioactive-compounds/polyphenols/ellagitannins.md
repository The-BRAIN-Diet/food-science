## Overview

Ellagitannins are hydrolysable tannins. Hydrolysis releases [ellagic acid](/docs/substances/bioactive-compounds/polyphenols/phenolic-acids/ellagic-acid), which some gut communities convert toward urolithins. [Urolithin A](/docs/substances/microbial-metabolites/secondary-plant-conversions/urolithin-a) is a downstream metabolite. A food that supplies ellagitannins does not contain urolithin A, and the tannin class is not the free acid.

The human comparison used pomegranate juice, a matrix that can provide ellagitannins, against direct urolithin A. Juice exposure to urolithin A varied between people, while direct administration bypassed conversion and raised exposure [1]. That juice is not proof that every ellagitannin, or every ellagitannin-containing food, converts to the same degree [1]. Final metabolite generation varies with the microbial community.

## Dietary Origin

The dietary relationship is precursor supply: ellagitannins can be hydrolysed to ellagic acid, which is then a microbial substrate. Pomegranate juice is the food matrix in the human comparison [1]. It is not an isolated ellagitannin, and it is not urolithin A. Direct urolithin A supplementation is a different exposure and does not test tannin hydrolysis [1]. No food page is tagged as containing urolithin A because of its ellagitannins.

## Research Spotlights & Evidence Checks

<EvidencePresentation
  kind="check"
  title="Does pomegranate juice show that every ellagitannin yields urolithin A?"
  exposureContext="whole-food"
  summary="No. The juice produced variable urolithin A exposure. Direct urolithin A bypassed conversion. One juice matrix is not every ellagitannin [1]."
>

Urolithin A from the juice depended on the person’s microbial conversion, and giving urolithin A itself removed that dependency [1]. Hydrolysis of ellagitannins can release ellagic acid upstream of that route. The study does not measure each ellagitannin separately, so equivalence across the class is not established [1].
