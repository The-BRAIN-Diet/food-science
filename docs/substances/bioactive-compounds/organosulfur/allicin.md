## Overview

Allicin is a reactive sulfur compound formed from alliin when garlic tissue is damaged, through the enzyme alliinase [1]. The substance page concerns allicin itself; garlic, extracts and other sulfur compounds require their own evidence attribution. Preparation therefore matters when describing exposure.

Its reactions with glutathione and protein thiols help explain activity in experimental systems [1]. Such chemistry does not independently establish a dietary benefit, a brain effect, or an effective human intake. Findings for a garlic product cannot be assigned to allicin alone without evidence that isolates its contribution.

## Dietary Context

Garlic tissue damage enables alliin → allicin formation [1]. Precursor content and formed allicin are separate measurements.
