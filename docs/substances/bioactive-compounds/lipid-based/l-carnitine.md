## Overview

L-carnitine is the naturally relevant carnitine stereoisomer. It accepts and releases fatty-acyl groups in the shuttle that moves long-chain fatty-acid substrates into mitochondria for oxidation. Human carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A (CPT1A) functional experiments support this transport machinery's role [1]. Reaction annotation independently identifies the L/(R)-carnitine substrate: [UniProt P50416](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P50416/entry), [P23786](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P23786/entry) and [Rhea 12661](https://www.rhea-db.org/rhea/12661), retrieved 23 October 2026.

This identity is distinct from D-carnitine, acetyl-L-carnitine and the L-carnitine-L-tartrate experimental formulation. It does not establish a need to increase intake, nor a brain-health or ADHD benefit.

## Dietary Context

Endogenous synthesis and dietary supply both contribute to carnitine availability. Radioisotopic food analysis measured free L-carnitine in meat samples, including beef and lamb [2]. Those qualitative composition observations support dietary supply of the same substrate; no serving quantity or effect on muscle transport is inferred. The precise cuts and preparations remain unresolved in the retrieved publisher text.

A separate 24-week human trial used L-carnitine-L-tartrate plus carbohydrate and measured muscle carnitine and exercise metabolism [3]. Its formulation and co-exposure cannot be treated as an effect of ordinary meat or dairy foods. Food-composition candidates await independent structured-relationship verification.
