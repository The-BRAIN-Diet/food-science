## Overview

Caffeine is the chemical identity recorded as [PubChem CID 2519](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/2519), with InChIKey RYYVLZVUVIJVGH-UHFFFAOYSA-N. Identity registration does not imply that caffeine is required or beneficial for a particular biological operation.

## Dietary Context

The circadian relationship below concerns oral caffeine capsules at 2.9 mg/kg, three hours before habitual bedtime, in five healthy adults after two weeks of caffeine abstinence. It does not establish equivalence to a particular coffee, tea or other food preparation. No food concentration or serving recommendation is inferred [1].

## Biological Regulatory Systems

**[BRS-X Circadian — PM1: Light–Dark Circadian Entrainment](/docs/biological-targets/brs-x/circadian/fm1/brs-x-circadian-fm1-pm1-light-dark-circadian-entrainment/): Type B Regulation — negative/disruptive direction.** The tested evening exposure delayed subsequent dim-light melatonin onset by approximately 40 minutes relative to dim-light placebo. This is a circadian phase response, not proof of reduced melatonin synthesis, universal harm or clinical benefit [1].

The small sample, single dose/time and capsule exposure limit generalisation. Caffeine plus bright light did not significantly increase the delay beyond bright light alone. Separate transformed-cell findings implicate adenosine-A1/cAMP signalling but do not establish that molecular mechanism in the participants [1].

**Timing SOP assessed separately:** limiting or timing exposure to reduce disruption remains a proposed practice, not an admitted timing protocol. Three hours before bedtime was the tested exposure, not a validated avoidance cutoff. No consumption recommendation follows from this relationship.
