## Overview

N-acetylcysteine is a supplemental form used as a cysteine precursor. It is not cysteine, and it is not the cystine measured in food tables. The [cysteine](/docs/substances/nutrients/macronutrients/amino-acids/conditionals/cysteine) page holds the amino acid and the food-composition relationship.

In older adults, two weeks of N-acetylcysteine together with glycine raised red-cell glutathione synthesis that had been lower than in younger adults [1]. The precursor was given with glycine, so the rise does not isolate N-acetylcysteine. The trial does not show an effect of N-acetylcysteine alone, of ordinary foods, or of supplementation outside that older-adult group, and it did not measure a neuronal or clinical outcome [1].

## Dietary Origin

Exposure here is a direct supplement: N-acetylcysteine plus glycine, for two weeks, in older adults [1]. Ordinary foods are not that exposure. Cystine in protein is recorded on the cysteine page and is a different chemical measurement from this precursor.

## Research Spotlights & Evidence Checks

  title="Does the glutathione rise belong to N-acetylcysteine alone?"
  exposureContext="combination-formulation"

Older adults had lower red-cell glutathione synthesis than younger adults. Two weeks of the cysteine precursor plus glycine raised synthesis [1]. Because both were given, the result cannot be assigned to N-acetylcysteine alone, to glycine alone, or to a food that contains cystine [1].
