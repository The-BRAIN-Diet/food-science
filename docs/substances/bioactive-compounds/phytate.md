## Overview

Phytic acid is myo-inositol hexakisphosphate (InsP6): an inositol ring bearing six phosphate groups. Its protonated reference formula is C6H18O24P6. “Phytate” describes deprotonated and salt forms; the structure image represents the acid reference, not every mineral salt. Lower inositol phosphates and free myo-inositol are distinct substances. The search aliases on this page do not make their measured masses interchangeable [1].

The phosphate groups can bind mineral ions. Binding chemistry is not itself a measurement of human absorption: meal composition, mineral form, phytate content and processing matter. Human cereal-porridge experiments found improved iron absorption after phytase treatment in responsive matrices, with exceptions; this does not establish universal mineral depletion or a reason to avoid all phytate-containing foods [2].

## Dietary Context

Phytic acid was measured in the amaranth-grain malting study. This supports qualitative occurrence in the studied grain, not a universal per-100-g value. The accessible original abstract reports reductions during germination but does not establish human mineral absorption [3].

Analytical reports may express phytic acid, phytate phosphorus or a particular salt; these require explicit analyte and mass-basis reconciliation before comparison. Processing reductions are protocol-specific. Phytase hydrolysis changes the inositol-phosphate distribution; household soaking cannot be assumed to reproduce a validated enzymatic process [2].

The existing Functional Property **Phytate content** describes this constituent's presence. It is not a dietary admission or proof of benefit. The existing [cereal-dephytinisation context in dopaminergic signalling](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm3-dopaminergic-signalling-regulation) concerns upstream iron provision, not phytate as an admitted dopamine-supporting input. No new substance-to-PM support relationship is asserted here.
