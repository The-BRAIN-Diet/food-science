## Overview

Glucose is the monosaccharide in free sugar, in starch after digestion, and in blood. This page records one experimental use of it: luminal glucose as a signal in a gut-to-brain nerve circuit. It is not a recommendation to increase glucose or sugar intake.

In mice, enteroendocrine neuropod cells sensed luminal glucose and used a glutamate synapse to signal rapidly to vagal neurons [1]. That shows glucose can be a sensory stimulus in the circuit that was tested. It does not set an optimal intake, an inadequacy threshold, or a benefit from eating more sugar [1].

## Dietary Origin

Dietary glucose arrives as the sugar itself, or from digestible carbohydrate that yields glucose. The nerve result used glucose applied in a mouse experiment as a luminal stimulus [1]. A food that contains starch or sugar is not that experiment. No food page is linked from this result, because composition would not carry the neural finding.

## Research Spotlights & Evidence Checks

<EvidencePresentation
  kind="check"
  title="Does the mouse glucose-sensing result mean people should eat more glucose?"
  exposureContext="isolated-substance"
  summary="No. Luminal glucose triggered a neuropod–vagal response in mice. That is a sensory experiment, not an intake target [1]."
>

Neuropod cells transduced a nutrient stimulus into a rapid vagal signal, and the transmitter of that synapse was glutamate [1]. Glucose was the luminal stimulus in the test. The paper does not estimate a human dose, and it does not show that adding sugar improves the circuit or any clinical outcome [1].

</EvidencePresentation>
