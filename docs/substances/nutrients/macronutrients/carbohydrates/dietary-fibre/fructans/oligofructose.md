## Overview

Oligofructose is a mixture of short-chain inulin-type fructans. The structure shown is one fructose trimer from that mixture, not every chain the name covers. Roberfroid places the mixture at a degree of polymerisation of about 2–8, average about 4, beside longer-chain inulin (about 10–60) and beside blends such as oligofructose-enriched inulin [1]. Niness describes the same short-chain subgroup with an upper bound of 10, so the bound is a convention rather than a second substance [2]. This page uses oligofructose for that short-chain inulin-type mixture.

FOS and fructooligosaccharides overlap with this name, and they also name sucrose-derived kestose, nystose and fructosyl-nystose [3]. Chicory, onion, garlic, leek and Jerusalem artichoke store mixed fructan lengths. That plant mix is not a standardised oligofructose ingredient, and no food page here has a measured amount. In adults with overweight, 21 g/day of oligofructose for 12 weeks lowered plasma lipopolysaccharide versus placebo. That is a small supplement result, not a vegetable effect [4].

## Dietary Origin

Commercial oligofructose is usually the partial enzymatic hydrolysate of chicory inulin: a mixture of glucose-terminated and fructose-only chains in the short degree-of-polymerisation range [1,2]. That is a direct ingredient exposure. Sucrose-derived fructooligosaccharides are a different preparation, even when a label uses the same words [3].

Native plants contain inulin-type fructans across a range of chain lengths. A food that contains fructans does not, by that fact, contain this standardised mixture, and eating the food is not the 21 g/day supplement [1,4]. No food in this ontology yet has a chain-length measurement that would support a contains relationship, so none is linked.

## Research Spotlights & Evidence Checks

<EvidencePresentation
  kind="check"
  title="Are FOS and oligofructose the same substance?"
  exposureContext="isolated-substance"
  summary="No. The names overlap, but the chain range and the manufacturing route have to be stated before the materials can be treated as the same exposure [1,3]."
>

Roberfroid’s oligofructose is the inulin hydrolysate with a degree of polymerisation of about 2–8 [1]. Kelly notes that fructooligosaccharides, oligofructose and inulin have been used inconsistently, and that commercial material may be extracted from chicory or built from sucrose, with different chain compositions [3]. A study that says only “FOS” does not identify which of those preparations was used.

</EvidencePresentation>

<EvidencePresentation
  kind="check"
  title="Does a 21 g/day oligofructose supplement show what fructan-containing vegetables do?"
  exposureContext="supplemental-formulation"
  summary="No. In adults with overweight, 21 g/day for 12 weeks lowered plasma lipopolysaccharide compared with a maltodextrin placebo. That result belongs to the supplement, not to onion, leek or chicory portions [4]."
>

Twenty participants received oligofructose and 17 received placebo, from samples available for this analysis. Plasma lipopolysaccharide fell about 40% with oligofructose and rose about 48% with placebo. Plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 also fell more with oligofructose. Interleukin-6, TNF-α, MCP-1, adiponectin and resistin did not change [4]. The dose is an added ingredient, not a food pattern, and the trial does not show that longer-chain inulin or sucrose-derived fructooligosaccharides have the same effect.

</EvidencePresentation>

## References

[1] Roberfroid (2007). [Inulin-type fructans: functional food ingredients](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#roberfroid_inulin_type_fructans_2007). Defines oligofructose as the short-chain inulin-type fraction (degree of polymerisation about 2–8), separate from longer-chain inulin and from oligofructose-enriched inulin.

[2] Niness (1999). [Inulin and oligofructose: what are they?](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#niness_inulin_oligofructose_1999). Describes oligofructose as the short-chain subgroup of inulin, with an upper bound of 10 rather than 8, so the cutoff is a convention.

[3] Kelly (2008). [Inulin-type prebiotics — a review: part 1](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#kelly_inulin_type_prebiotics_2008). Records that fructooligosaccharides, oligofructose and inulin are used inconsistently, and that chicory extraction and sucrose synthesis do not yield the same chain mixture.

[4] Parnell et al. (2017). [Oligofructose decreases serum lipopolysaccharide and plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 in adults with overweight/obesity](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#parnell_oligofructose_lps_2017). Supplemental 21 g/day result in a small overweight sample. It is not a food trial and not a finding about every fructan.
