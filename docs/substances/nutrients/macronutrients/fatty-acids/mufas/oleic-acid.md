## Overview

Oleic acid (18:1 n-9) is the principal **dietary monounsaturated fatty acid**. It is the defining fatty acid of avocado oil and a major fatty acid of avocado flesh, olive oil, and many other plant oils.

It is **not** interchangeable with the broader labels **MUFA** or **omega-9**:

- **MUFA** is a class (one double bond). Palmitoleic acid (16:1) and other monounsaturated fatty acids are MUFAs that are not oleic acid.
- **Omega-9** names the n-9 family, which also includes longer-chain n-9 fatty acids. Oleic acid is the main dietary n-9 fatty acid, not the family itself.

Oleic acid is not an essential fatty acid. The body can synthesise it, but food oils still determine how much is eaten. Food pages should admit an Oleic Acid card only where oleic is a verified identity constituent with a rendered table row—not for every food that contains some MUFA.

- Avocado oil identity ranges typically place oleic at about 60–80% of fatty acids, varying by cultivar and grade [Fernandes et al. 2018](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#fernandes_avocado_oil_2018)
