## Overview

Vitamin K in plant foods is primarily phylloquinone (vitamin K1), a fat-soluble naphthoquinone concentrated in green leaves. USDA FoodData Central reports phylloquinone, not menaquinones. Fermented foods and some animal products that supply vitamin K2 are recorded separately on the [Vitamin K2](/docs/substances/nutrients/micronutrients/vitamins/vitamin-k2) page.

Phylloquinone is the standard compositional analyte for leafy vegetables, herbs, and many plant oils. Presence in a food table means the selected USDA record reported phylloquinone — it is not a K2 claim and not a BRS mapping by itself.
