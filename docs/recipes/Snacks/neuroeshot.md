## Overview

Neuroeshot is a one- or two-mouthful appetiser that makes a small serving of salmon roe feel distinctive rather than dutiful. Chilled roe pearls bring salinity, umami and a delicate pop; bright lemon acidity follows, with a brief fermented-chilli finish.

This recipe uses a small portion of cured salmon roe. The nutrition panel uses salted chum-salmon ikura. No sodium total is published, because the hot sauce varies by brand.

## Ingredients (1 serving)

- 15 g (1 tbsp) **salmon roe** (or trout roe)
- 7.5 ml (1 1/2 tsp) **lemon juice**
- 0.6-1.2 ml (1-2 dashes) **fermented hot sauce** (Tabasco-style)

<RecipeCalculationDefault details={frontMatter} />

## Method

1. Add roe to a small shot glass or spoon.
2. Top with lemon juice.
3. Add 1-2 dashes of fermented hot sauce.
4. Serve immediately.

## Extra Guidance

- Trout roe is a direct swap at the same amount if preferred.
- Serve as a one- or two-mouthful appetiser, not as a meal or a supplement-style dose.
- Keep the roe chilled and mix immediately before serving so the pearls retain their texture against the lemon and chilli.
- This composition record does not report total choline. Phosphatidylcholine is reported separately as present in salmon roe.
- Fermented hot sauce is salted, and the salt varies by brand, so the recipe does not publish a sodium total. Roe sodium is the salted ikura figure on the salmon roe page.
- Fermented hot sauce contributes flavour at this quantity. Because products vary and may be pasteurised, this dash-sized serving is a flavouring, not a probiotic intervention.

## Brain Health Notes

- **Roe is the nutritional centre of the recipe.** It combines preformed EPA and DHA, total choline and phosphatidylcholine in a phospholipid-rich marine matrix, vitamin B12, astaxanthin and high-quality protein at unusually high density for such a small serving.
- **The evidence has clear limits.** EPA, DHA and vitamin B12 in the panel come from the salted chum-salmon ikura table. That table does not report total choline or a phospholipid-class distribution. Astaxanthin is present in salmon roe; its amount in a particular commercial product is not established without product-relevant analysis.
- **Lemon and hot sauce primarily improve flavour and acceptability** at these quantities. No cognitive, treatment or clinical outcome can be inferred from one serving.
