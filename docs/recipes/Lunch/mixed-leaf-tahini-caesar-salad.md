## Overview

A Caesar-style salad built around romaine lettuce, watercress, baby spinach and radicchio, with a creamy dressing made from extra virgin olive oil, tahini, anchovies, capers preserved in EVOO, Parmesan, garlic, lemon and balsamic vinegars.

The mixed leaves provide contrasting textures and flavours: crisp romaine, peppery watercress, tender spinach and slightly bitter radicchio. The dressing balances savoury, salty, acidic and sweet elements while supplying enough dietary fat to support the absorption of fat-soluble compounds from the vegetables.

## Ingredients (4 servings)

**Leaf base**
- 250 g romaine lettuce, washed, dried and roughly torn
- 80 g baby spinach, washed and thoroughly dried
- 60 g watercress, any coarse stems removed
- 80 g radicchio, finely sliced

**Tahini Caesar dressing**
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) extra virgin olive oil
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) extra virgin olive oil retained from the caper jar
- 2 tbsp (30 g) tahini
- 2 tbsp (30 g) good-quality mayonnaise or aioli
- 6 anchovy fillets in olive oil, drained (24 g)
- 2 tbsp (18 g) capers preserved in extra virgin olive oil, lightly drained (blend most; reserve 1 tsp to scatter)
- 25 g finely grated Parmesan
- 2 garlic cloves, crushed (6 g)
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) fresh lemon juice
- 2 tsp (10 ml) white balsamic vinegar
- 2 tsp (10 ml) dark balsamic vinegar
- 30–60 ml ice-cold water
- 2 tbsp crushed ice
- freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- salt only if required after blending

**To finish**
- 1 tsp of the capers, reserved
- 20 g Parmesan, finely shaved

**Optional wholegrain croutons**
- 100 g day-old wholegrain sourdough bread, cut into cubes
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) extra virgin olive oil (optional croutons)
- black pepper, to taste

## Method

1. Crush the garlic and leave it to stand for 5–10 minutes while preparing the remaining ingredients.
2. Lightly drain the capers. Reserve 1 tsp to scatter over the finished salad. Place the remaining capers, the anchovies, grated Parmesan, tahini, mayonnaise, garlic, lemon juice and both balsamic vinegars in a blender or small food processor.
3. Add the measured extra virgin olive oil, including the retained oil from the caper jar, and blend until the anchovies and capers are fully incorporated. The total added EVOO should remain 30 ml.
4. Add the crushed ice and enough ice-cold water to produce a smooth, glossy dressing that is thick enough to coat the leaves but loose enough to toss evenly.
5. Season generously with black pepper. Taste before adding any salt: the anchovies, capers, Parmesan and mayonnaise may already provide sufficient seasoning.
6. If making the optional croutons, toss the bread cubes with olive oil and black pepper. Bake at 190°C for approximately 8–12 minutes, turning once, until crisp and golden. Allow them to cool.
7. Combine the romaine, spinach, watercress and radicchio in a large salad bowl.
8. Add approximately two-thirds of the dressing and toss gently until the leaves are lightly and evenly coated. Add more dressing only as required.
9. Finish with the reserved capers, the shaved Parmesan and optional wholegrain croutons. Serve immediately.

## Extra Guidance

- Dry the leaves thoroughly so the dressing adheres rather than becoming diluted.
- Add the dressing gradually; the recipe may produce slightly more than required depending on the size and condition of the leaves.
- Use capers preserved in genuine extra virgin olive oil rather than capers packed in brine, vinegar or refined oil.
- Drain the capers lightly and retain some of their EVOO for the dressing.
- Blend most of the 2 tbsp capers into the dressing. Reserve 1 tsp and scatter it over the finished salad.
- Do not rinse the oil-packed capers unless they are excessively salty, because rinsing removes the EVOO and some of their flavour.
- Taste the dressing before adding salt. The sodium content depends partly on how the capers were cured before being packed in EVOO.
- The dark balsamic should provide rounded sweetness rather than dominate the dressing. Check the label because some balsamic glazes contain substantial added sugar.
- Keep the dressing and leaves separate until serving.
- Dressing made with commercial mayonnaise can be refrigerated in a sealed container for up to 2 days.
- If using homemade aioli containing raw egg, use pasteurised egg, keep refrigerated and consume promptly.

## Brain Health Notes

- Anchovies contribute marine omega-3 fatty acids, including EPA and DHA, alongside protein, vitamin B12 and selenium. The contribution is modest within a dressed salad, and quantitative values follow the drained canned-anchovy record rather than an assumed fillet.
- Capers preserved in extra virgin olive oil contribute a concentrated caper matrix containing flavonoids, including quercetin, alongside the polyphenol and monounsaturated-fat profile of EVOO. The caper serving is small, so claims stay with the mapped serving rather than concentration per 100 g, and quercetin is not given a number unless a defensible composition record supplies one.
- Extra virgin olive oil and tahini provide predominantly unsaturated fats. Dietary fat consumed with raw vegetables supports the absorption of carotenoids and other fat-soluble compounds.
- Romaine and spinach contribute folate, carotenoids and vitamin K.
- Watercress contributes vitamin C, vitamin K, carotenoids and glucosinolate-derived compounds.
- Radicchio adds bitter and red-pigmented plant compounds while increasing the diversity of the leaf matrix.
- Freshly crushed garlic provides organosulfur compounds. Allowing crushed garlic to rest briefly before blending supports their formation.
- Parmesan supplies protein and calcium but, together with the anchovies and capers, increases the sodium content.
- The dressing is energy-dense, but a measured serving can improve the palatability and nutrient absorption of a substantial mixed-leaf salad.
