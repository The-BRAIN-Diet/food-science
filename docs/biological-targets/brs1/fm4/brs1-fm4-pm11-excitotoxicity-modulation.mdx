### Overview

When glutamate receptors are driven too hard or for too long, cells can run short of energy. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) This mechanism buffers that injury-level stress, after ordinary clearance is no longer enough. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) Protecting cellular energy under that overload is the point of this buffer. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) In one laboratory model, magnesium sulfate reduced that energy harm. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)

- **Benefits:** Limiting this overload can protect cells from the energy stress of excessive receptor stimulation. The protection was shown in a laboratory model, not in people. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)
- **Biological Relevance:** This injury-level buffer is separate from network excitation–inhibition balance. [[2]](#pm-ref-2)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects
↓ excitotoxic signalling burden; ↑ inhibitory/excitatory resilience

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

When glutamate receptors are driven too hard, cells can suffer energy stress. This mechanism is the last buffer against that injury-level pressure. Sibling mechanisms hold integrative excitation–inhibition matching, GABA synthesis and upstream glutamate clearance. Network excitation–inhibition reviews and glutamate/glutamine spectroscopy are not this buffering assay.

#### (Excitotoxic pressure from receptor overload)

Chronic or excessive glutamate-receptor stimulation can produce bioenergetic stress. That injury-level pressure is what this mechanism is meant to buffer → [Clerc et al., 2013]

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

Integrative excitation–inhibition matching, GABA synthesis and glutamate clearance are coordinated sibling jobs. Network excitation–inhibition framing is cluster context, not this buffering demonstration → [Mamiya et al., 2021]

#### (Integration)

Buffering capacity can sit beside inflammatory, energetic and glycaemic context. Those cross-system links are constrained associations, not proven controllers of this layer.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

In an experimental chronic glutamate-receptor stimulation model, excessive stimulation caused bioenergetic harm and magnesium sulfate limited its consequences. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) Network-level excitation–inhibition framing supplies broader context rather than independent confirmation of that protection. [[2]](#pm-ref-2) The findings support a bounded experimental protective effect, without establishing that ordinary dietary magnesium controls excitotoxic pressure or that protection alone restores circuit balance.

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS3-FM1-PM1 — NF-kB Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-pm1-nf-kb-signalling-regulation) — Inflammatory tone can change how vulnerable synapses are to overload, but the attached studies do not show that NF-κB signalling sets excitotoxic buffering.

- [BRS6-FM1-PM1 — Glucose Appearance Kinetics](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/fm1/brs6-fm1-pm1-glucose-appearance-kinetics) — Glycaemic swings can change metabolic context for neurons, but how glucose appears in blood is not shown here as a controller of excitotoxic buffering.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS1-FM4-PM9 - GABA Synthesis Capacity](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm4/brs1-fm4-pm9-gaba-synthesis-capacity) — Makes the inhibitory transmitter from glutamate. Synthesis chemistry is coordinated with overload protection, not the same excitotoxic-buffering function.
- [BRS1-FM4-PM10 - Glutamate Clearance & Recycling](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm4/brs1-fm4-pm10-glutamate-clearance-and-recycling) — Clears and recycles glutamate after signalling. Failed or overloaded clearance is an upstream risk for this layer, not the same mechanism.
