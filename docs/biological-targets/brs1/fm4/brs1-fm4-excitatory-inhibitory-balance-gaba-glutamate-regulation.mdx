## 1. Mission & Overview

### Objective

Support coordinated excitatory and inhibitory signalling through GABA synthesis, glutamate clearance and recycling, and protection against excessive glutamate-receptor stimulation. [[4]](#fm-ref-4) [[7]](#fm-ref-7) [[8]](#fm-ref-8).

### Overview

GABA synthesis supplies inhibitory transmitter, while glutamate uptake and recycling help terminate and replenish signalling [[4]](#fm-ref-4) [[7]](#fm-ref-7). Protection against excessive glutamate-receptor stimulation is a separate, context-dependent safeguard [[8]](#fm-ref-8). These contributions support excitation–inhibition regulation, but receptor responses and synaptic adaptation remain outside the retained PM coverage; this FM does not claim comprehensive coverage [[3]](#fm-ref-3).

- **Integrated capacity:** transmitter formation, clearance/recycling and overload protection make distinct contributions rather than interchangeable measures of balance [[4]](#fm-ref-4) [[7]](#fm-ref-7) [[8]](#fm-ref-8).
- **Coverage boundary:** receptor/synaptic regulation is biologically relevant but is not owned by any retained child PM [[3]](#fm-ref-3).
- **Evidence boundary:** regional GABA/Glx concentrations are not synthesis flux, transporter activity or a direct circuit-level E/I measurement [[1]](#fm-ref-1) [[5]](#fm-ref-5) [[9]](#fm-ref-9).

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

Support GABA synthesis capacity; support glutamate signal termination/recycling; limit receptor-overload bioenergetic harm in defined contexts. These do not establish uniformly increased inhibition or comprehensive E/I regulation.

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

<span id="42-integrated-functional-narrative" />

### 4.1 Functional Rationale

GABA synthesis, glutamate clearance/recycling and protection against excessive receptor stimulation contribute to a stable excitatory–inhibitory environment. Receptor and synaptic regulation remain outside the retained PM coverage.

<span id="44-evidence-highlights" />

### 4.2 Evidence Summary

Enzyme evidence establishes PLP-dependent GABA synthesis and distinguishes basal from recruitable capacity. Reviews establish uptake/recycling as glutamate-termination steps. Chronic receptor-stimulation experiments demonstrate bioenergetic injury and bounded protection by magnesium sulfate. [[4](#fm-ref-4), [7](#fm-ref-7), [8](#fm-ref-8)]

If these capacities function adequately, they would support transmitter supply, signal termination and resistance to excessive stimulation. Their aggregation does not directly measure circuit-level excitation–inhibition matching or cover every receptor and synaptic process that determines it; transmitter concentrations cannot stand in for those processes.

### 4.3 Suboptimal Function & Its Effects

Constrained synthesis can reduce available inhibitory transmitter capacity; impaired clearance can prolong extracellular excitatory signalling [[4]](#fm-ref-4) [[7]](#fm-ref-7). Sustained excessive receptor stimulation can impose bioenergetic harm in the assessed experimental setting [[8]](#fm-ref-8). These are different consequences and need not occur together. Transmitter concentrations alone cannot identify which capacity is constrained [[9]](#fm-ref-9), and this inventory cannot diagnose unassessed receptor or synaptic regulation.
