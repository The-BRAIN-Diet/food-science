### Overview

DHA, a long-chain omega-3 fat, is built into the phospholipids of neuronal membranes. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Membranes that contain it support the structure neurons use to signal. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) The carrier matters: DHA bound as lysophosphatidylcholine can cross into the brain, and in animals phospholipid DHA accumulated in the brain more than triglyceride DHA. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[5]](#pm-ref-5) That is structural delivery, not a general omega-3 effect. [[3]](#pm-ref-3)

- **Benefits:** DHA in these membranes supports the fluidity and receptor movement that synaptic signalling depends on. [[3]](#pm-ref-3)
- **Implementation Notes:** DHA is the provision route. Phospholipid-bound DHA is a delivery form, not a second treatment, and the animal comparison does not set a human dose. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects
↑ brain DHA accretion; ↑ neuronal membrane DHA enrichment; ↑ membrane fluidity context; ↑ synaptic signalling competence

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Brain DHA replacement and supplement claims

In 14 healthy adults, intravenous tracer PET and plasma measurements yielded an estimated **3.8 ± 1.7 mg/day** of whole-brain DHA incorporation (mean ± SD). This measures replacement from circulating DHA; it is **not the amount to eat or take as a supplement** Umhau et al. (2009) [[8]](#pm-ref-8).

Using a previously reported **5 g brain DHA pool**, rather than measuring that stock in the participants, the authors estimated **0.076% daily turnover** and a **911-day half-life (about 2.5 years)**. These steady-state estimates do not establish an optimal oral dose, the proportion of a capsule reaching the brain, or clinical benefit. Absorption, peripheral metabolism and delivery form separate swallowed DHA from brain incorporation. In the 14 healthy adults studied, estimated whole-brain DHA incorporation averaged **3.8 ± 1.7 mg/day**. Under steady-state assumptions, this represents net replacement of a few milligrams daily: **3.8 − 1.7 = 2.1 mg/day** and **3.8 + 1.7 = 5.5 mg/day**, or approximately **2–6 mg/day** after rounding. This is a descriptive mean ± one standard deviation, not the observed minimum–maximum range, a recommended replacement range or a dietary intake target Umhau et al. (2009) [[8]](#pm-ref-8).

**DHA is one part of brain lipid biology.** Human PET also measures incorporation of the omega-6 fatty acid arachidonic acid Giovacchini et al. (2002) [[9]](#pm-ref-9). Developing neuronal and glial cultures demonstrate local cholesterol synthesis and uptake Genaro-Mattos et al. (2019) [[10]](#pm-ref-10). These are distinct lipid processes, not evidence that additional supplements are required. This PM remains specific to DHA incorporation; the cholesterol culture study does not establish a dietary cholesterol requirement.

### Summary

Synaptic signalling depends partly on the lipid composition of neuronal membranes. Phosphatidylcholine can carry DHA; conversion toward lysophosphatidylcholine-DHA (LPC-DHA) presents DHA to the MFSD2A transporter at the blood–brain barrier. Delivery form, not dose slogans, is the mechanistic distinction. This is not an ADHD treatment claim.

#### (Phosphatidylcholine as DHA and EPA carrier)

Phosphatidylcholine is a principal membrane phospholipid and a dietary carrier for long-chain omega-3 fatty acids. DHA in that phospholipid pool supplies neuronal-membrane architecture and the chemistry that can generate LPC carriers → [Patrick, 2019]

#### (LPC-DHA and MFSD2A blood–brain barrier transport)

DHA in phosphatidylcholine can be converted to LPC-DHA, which crosses the blood–brain barrier more efficiently than free DHA or triglyceride-bound forms via MFSD2A. That transport step precedes structural incorporation and explains why phospholipid-matrix delivery is distinct from triglyceride-oil dosing → [Patrick, 2019]

#### (Membrane incorporation and synaptic signalling)

DHA integrates into phospholipid bilayers of neuronal membranes, influencing membrane fluidity, receptor dynamics, and synaptic signalling competence. Incorporation builds over weeks to months of habitual intake rather than through single-meal bolus exposure → [McNamara & Carlson, 2006]

#### (Phospholipid-bound delivery efficacy)

Phospholipid-bound DHA showed higher gray-matter accretion than triglyceride DHA in neonatal piglets — about 1.9-fold in that comparison. The contrast is animal delivery form, not a human capsule rule and not a choline-intake test → [Liu et al., 2014]

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

This mechanism governs phospholipid-carrier delivery through neuronal-membrane incorporation over habitual intake. Lipid-mediator resolution biology belongs with inflammation-domain mechanisms. Acute amino-acid substrate supply is handled by [BRS1-FM1-PM1 - Amino-Acid Availability & Prioritisation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation). Large-neutral-amino-acid competition at the barrier belongs to [BRS1-FM1-PM2 - LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation). Choline-to-acetylcholine conversion is a different choline fate, represented by [BRS1-FM2-PM6 - Acetylcholine Synthesis Support](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm2/brs1-fm2-pm6-acetylcholine-synthesis-support). APOE4-sensitive delivery interpretation is handled by [BRS1(SM-SNP2)](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/sm/brs1-sm-snp2-apoe4-omega-3-brain-delivery-sensitivity). Membrane PUFA protection and eicosanoid/SPM balance belong downstream with lipid-peroxidation control and eicosanoid–SPM balance.

#### (Integration within BRS1)

This mechanism operationalises membrane DHA incorporation within [BRS1(FM3)](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-phospholipid-mediated-dha-delivery-and-membrane-integration). Habitual DHA provision and phospholipid/LPC delivery context remain the primary biological levers discussed here.

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS3-FM2-PM5 — Lipid Peroxidation Control](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm2/brs3-fm2-pm5-lipid-peroxidation-control) — Once DHA is in membranes it remains oxidisable; peroxidation control protects those polyunsaturated lipids but is not the incorporation step itself.
- [BRS3-FM3-PM8 — Eicosanoid / SPM Balance](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm3/brs3-fm3-pm8-eicosanoid-spm-balance) — Incorporated long-chain fats can feed eicosanoid and specialised pro-resolving mediator chemistry downstream of this membrane job.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS1-FM2-PM6 - Acetylcholine Synthesis Support](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm2/brs1-fm2-pm6-acetylcholine-synthesis-support) — Converts choline plus acetyl-CoA into acetylcholine. That choline fate is transmitter synthesis, not phosphatidylcholine carriage of DHA for membrane incorporation.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM1 - Amino-Acid Availability & Prioritisation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation) — Supplies circulating amino acids for monoamine precursors. Amino-acid pool adequacy is not DHA transport or membrane accretion.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM2 - LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation) — Governs competitive brain entry of large neutral amino acids. LAT1 amino-acid competition is not MFSD2A LPC-DHA transport.
