### 3. Evidence Base

#### Summary

This constraint concerns the availability of tryptophan, tyrosine and phenylalanine within the shared large-neutral-amino-acid (LNAA) transport pool. These monoamine precursors compete with one another and with other LNAAs for LAT1-mediated entry into the brain, so relative plasma presentation can matter alongside precursor supply. Dietary patterns can alter those ratios and downstream precursor availability, making the three amino acids one coherent transport-linked resource rather than independent performance nutrients ([Fernstrom, 2013](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#fernstrom_lnna_2013)).

- **Constraint and membership boundary:** General protein quality and indispensable-amino-acid adequacy are nutritionally important, but the cited protein-quality evidence does not establish them as this specific shared BRS1 transport constraint ([FAO, 2013](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#fao_diaas_2013); [Mariotti and Gardner, 2019](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#mariotti_dietary_2019)).
- **Evidence / measurement boundary:** The evidence links dietary patterns and plasma LNAA ratios to brain precursor transport and neurochemistry; it does not define a universal optimal meal ratio or establish downstream behavioural or clinical benefit ([Fernstrom, 2013](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#fernstrom_lnna_2013)).
- **Biological relevance and provision limitation:** Adequate precursor availability and competitive context can help avoid transport-level constraint, but that does not show that extra amino-acid intake improves monoamine function in people whose supply and balance are already sufficient ([Fernstrom, 2013](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#fernstrom_lnna_2013)).

#### Biological Importance

Tryptophan is an essential LNAA and serotonin precursor. Its brain entry depends partly on its plasma concentration relative to competing LNAAs, making its availability a constituent of the shared transport constraint rather than evidence that isolated supplementation improves serotonin-related function.

#### Biological Importance

Tyrosine is an LNAA and catecholamine precursor. Its presentation relative to competing LNAAs influences transport into the brain, so adequate availability must be interpreted within the competitive pool rather than as a standalone “more is better” target.

Phenylalanine is an essential LNAA, contributes to tyrosine availability and participates in the same competitive transport context. These roles place it within the precursor pool, while leaving any dietary dose-response or downstream functional effect unestablished.
