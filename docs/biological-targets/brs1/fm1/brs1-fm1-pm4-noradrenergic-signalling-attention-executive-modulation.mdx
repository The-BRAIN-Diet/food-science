summary: 'Noradrenaline (norepinephrine) helps regulate arousal, vigilance and executive control. Neurons form it from dopamine through dopamine β-hydroxylase, after the shared tyrosine-to-dopamine synthesis steps, and then use release, receptor signalling, reuptake and metabolism to shape its effects. Their coordination helps tune signalling across changing cognitive demand. This mechanism therefore begins after precursor supply and blood–brain transport and concerns regulated noradrenergic signalling rather than meal-level amino-acid availability.'

scientific_findings_intro: Noradrenaline (also called norepinephrine) is the brain’s main arousal and vigilance signal. The findings below separate that signalling role from shared tyrosine-to-dopamine synthesis steps and from the later dopamine β-hydroxylase conversion to noradrenaline. They establish biological relevance and important limits; they do not show that changing diet raises brain noradrenaline or improves attention or ADHD.

### Overview

Noradrenaline (norepinephrine) helps regulate arousal, vigilance and executive control O'Donnell et al. (2012) [[1]](#pm-ref-1). Neurons form it from dopamine through dopamine β-hydroxylase, after the shared tyrosine-to-dopamine synthesis steps Goldstein and Eisenhofer (2026) [[9]](#pm-ref-9); Vendelboe et al. (2016) [[10]](#pm-ref-10), and then use release, receptor signalling, reuptake and metabolism to shape its effects. Their coordination helps tune signalling across changing cognitive demand. This mechanism therefore begins after precursor supply and blood–brain transport and concerns regulated noradrenergic signalling rather than meal-level amino-acid availability.

* **Mechanism boundary:** Includes dopamine-to-noradrenaline conversion and subsequent signalling; circulating amino-acid supply, LAT1 transport and dopaminergic signalling are adjacent processes.
* **Evidence boundary:** Cofactor requirements and catecholamine-pathway measurements establish reaction biology, not that ordinary intake changes human brain noradrenaline O'Donnell et al. (2012) [[1]](#pm-ref-1); Goldstein and Eisenhofer (2026) [[9]](#pm-ref-9).
* **Biological relevance:** Noradrenergic signalling helps sustain vigilance and executive engagement, but this relevance does not establish a nutritional attention or ADHD treatment O'Donnell et al. (2012) [[1]](#pm-ref-1); MacDonald et al. (2024) [[4]](#pm-ref-4).

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Noradrenergic signalling relevant to attention, arousal and executive control depends on the tyrosine-derived catecholamine path and on cofactor context described under [BRS1(FM1) — Monoaminergic Function](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-monoaminergic-function). Physical activity, sleep and stress can also shape catecholamine tone; those relationships are not established here as dietary requirements.

#### (Noradrenergic pathways and attention)

Norepinephrine modulates attention, arousal, and executive processes frequently impaired in attention-related conditions. Alterations in noradrenergic signalling have been associated with differences in attention regulation and response inhibition → [O'Donnell et al., 2012]

#### (Precursor and cofactor dependence)

Noradrenaline is formed from dopamine on the tyrosine-derived catecholamine path. Circulating tyrosine comes from the amino-acid pool described by [BRS1-FM1-PM1 — Amino-Acid Availability & Prioritisation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation). Meal-level large-neutral-amino-acid transport is described by [BRS1-FM1-PM2 — LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation) and Fernstrom (2013) [[2]](#pm-ref-2). Iron is required by tyrosine hydroxylase, the rate-limiting step from tyrosine toward dopamine and then noradrenaline Beard et al. (2003) [[3]](#pm-ref-3). Dopamine β-hydroxylase then converts dopamine to noradrenaline and requires copper and ascorbate Goldstein and Eisenhofer (2026) [[9]](#pm-ref-9).

#### (Diet-supported rather than diet-dominant)

Meal-level substrate and cofactor supply sit beside physical activity, sleep and stress context. Those lifestyle relationships are not established here as dietary requirements.

#### (Cross-system context)

Catecholamine inactivation can involve COMT, which uses SAM from one-carbon metabolism. The attached corpus does not establish that methylation-cycle diet changes noradrenaline signalling. Glycaemic volatility can change arousal indirectly; that is not a noradrenaline dietary finding.

Together this mechanism extends catecholamine coverage from dopamine formation to noradrenergic attention and executive modulation.

- [BRS1-FM1-PM2 - LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation) — Governs competitive brain entry of large neutral amino acids, including tyrosine, after a meal. Transport bias is not noradrenaline synthesis or receptor signalling.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM3 - Dopaminergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm3-dopaminergic-signalling-regulation) — Forms dopamine, the immediate precursor converted to noradrenaline, and shares the tyrosine-hydroxylase and decarboxylase steps. Dopaminergic signalling is not noradrenergic signalling.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM5 - Serotonergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm5-serotonergic-signalling-regulation) — Parallel monoamine path from shared precursor transport. It does not feed noradrenaline synthesis.

## 7. Phenome Connections

These mappings are translational relationships, not single-mechanism outcome claims. Phenomes are emergent functional patterns supported by multiple interacting PMs across the BRAIN Framework.
