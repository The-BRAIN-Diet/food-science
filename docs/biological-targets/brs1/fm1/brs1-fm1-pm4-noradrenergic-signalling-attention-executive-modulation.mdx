title: Noradrenergic Signalling
pm_id: BRS1-FM1-PM4
mission: >-
  Support noradrenergic alertness and executive control so attention and arousal
  stay appropriately engaged.
parent_fm: BRS1(FM1)
parent_brs: BRS1
summary: >-
  Noradrenaline, also called norepinephrine, helps set arousal, vigilance and
  executive control so attention can stay engaged. Neurons make it from
  dopamine, then shape the signal through release, receptors, reuptake and
  breakdown. This coordination lets alertness follow cognitive demand. Iron,
  vitamin B6, copper and vitamin C take part in that chemistry.

scientific_findings_intro: >-
  Structural and biochemical evidence establishes copper and ascorbate
  participation in the defining dopamine-to-noradrenaline conversion.
  [[10]](#pm-ref-10) [[9]](#pm-ref-9) Animal pharmacology and neuronal
  recordings separately support receptor-dependent prefrontal regulation and
  firing-pattern responses to attended cues. [[16]](#pm-ref-16)
  [[18]](#pm-ref-18) These findings support distinct synthesis and signalling
  processes; they do not establish that changing dietary precursor or cofactor
  intake changes brain noradrenaline firing or attention.

      Noradrenaline (norepinephrine) is a neuromodulator that changes how many

      after meals, or a clinical attention outcome.
    synthesis_limitations: >-

  - id: PM4-F2
    finding_label: Noradrenaline formation shares catecholamine synthesis requirements
    finding_summary: >-
      Noradrenaline is formed from dopamine on the tyrosine-derived
      catecholamine path. Iron supports tyrosine hydroxylase, and
      pyridoxal-5′-phosphate (PLP; the active form of vitamin B6) supports
      decarboxylation toward dopamine. These shared steps are biological
      requirements, not proof that extra iron or B6 raises brain noradrenaline.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      Tyrosine remains the pathway starting material. Circulating amino-acid
      supply, competitive brain entry and dopamine formation are earlier steps.
      The later conversion of dopamine to noradrenaline is assessed in the
      dopamine β-hydroxylase finding.
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_statement: >-
      Noradrenaline formation depends on the shared tyrosine-to-dopamine
      catecholamine sequence, including iron- and PLP-dependent enzyme steps;
      cofactor or substrate necessity is not evidence of intake-responsive human
      brain noradrenaline.
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: >-
      Iron-deficiency review evidence identifies iron as required for tyrosine
      hydroxylase, the rate-limiting step from tyrosine toward dopamine and then
      noradrenaline. B-vitamin review evidence places PLP in neurochemical
      decarboxylation, and a transport study shows that B6 vitamers can enter
      the central nervous system. Together they support shared pathway
      dependence. They do not show ordinary-diet control of human brain
      noradrenaline. The later dopamine β-hydroxylase step is assessed
      separately.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      The most direct deficiency discussion is preclinical or developmental. The
      sources do not establish common inadequacy in a defined clinical
      population, responsiveness when status is already adequate, or an
      attention benefit mediated by noradrenaline.

### Overview

Noradrenaline, also called norepinephrine, helps set arousal, vigilance and executive control so attention can stay engaged. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) Neurons make it from dopamine, then shape the signal through release, receptors, reuptake and breakdown. [[9]](#pm-ref-9) [[10]](#pm-ref-10) This coordination lets alertness follow cognitive demand. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) Iron, vitamin B6, copper and vitamin C take part in that chemistry. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[7]](#pm-ref-7) [[12]](#pm-ref-12) [[13]](#pm-ref-13)

- **Benefits:** Noradrenaline helps keep arousal and vigilance matched to cognitive demand. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)
- **Implementation Notes:** Iron, vitamin B6, copper, and vitamin C are the cofactors on the path from dopamine to noradrenaline. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[7]](#pm-ref-7) [[12]](#pm-ref-12) [[13]](#pm-ref-13) Extra intake when status is already adequate is not established to raise brain noradrenaline or improve attention. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)
- **Biological Relevance:** This tunes alert engagement rather than creating the precursor pool. [[2]](#pm-ref-2)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects
↑ norepinephrine signalling; ↑ attention; ↑ executive modulation

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Noradrenergic signalling relevant to attention, arousal and executive control depends on the tyrosine-derived catecholamine path and on cofactor context described under [BRS1(FM1) — Monoaminergic Function](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-monoaminergic-function). Physical activity, sleep and stress can also shape catecholamine tone; those relationships are not established here as dietary requirements.

#### (Noradrenergic pathways and attention)

Norepinephrine modulates attention, arousal, and executive processes frequently impaired in attention-related conditions. Alterations in noradrenergic signalling have been associated with differences in attention regulation and response inhibition → [O'Donnell et al., 2012]

#### (Firing, receptor actions and regional clearance)

Noradrenergic signalling depends on when neurons fire, which receptors respond and how transmitters are cleared. Monkey pharmacology supports alpha2-adrenergic and D1 contributions to prefrontal regulation Gamo, Wang and Arnsten (2010) [[16]](#pm-ref-16). Mouse experiments identify NET-mediated dopamine uptake in frontal cortex Morón et al. (2002) [[17]](#pm-ref-17). Locus-coeruleus recordings link phasic responses to attended cues Aston-Jones et al. (1994) [[18]](#pm-ref-18). These distinct experimental links support regulated signalling beyond synthesis; they do not establish dietary control or human ADHD benefit.

#### (Precursor and cofactor dependence)

Noradrenaline is formed from dopamine on the tyrosine-derived catecholamine path. Circulating tyrosine comes from the amino-acid pool described by [BRS1-FM1-PM1 — Amino-Acid Availability & Prioritisation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation). Meal-level large-neutral-amino-acid transport is described by [BRS1-FM1-PM2 — LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation) and Fernstrom (2013) [[2]](#pm-ref-2). Iron is required by tyrosine hydroxylase, the rate-limiting step from tyrosine toward dopamine and then noradrenaline Beard et al. (2003) [[3]](#pm-ref-3). Dopamine β-hydroxylase then converts dopamine to noradrenaline and requires copper and ascorbate Goldstein and Eisenhofer (2026) [[9]](#pm-ref-9).

#### (Diet-supported rather than diet-dominant)

Meal-level substrate and cofactor supply sit beside physical activity, sleep and stress context. Those lifestyle relationships are not established here as dietary requirements.

#### (Cross-system context)

Catecholamine inactivation can involve COMT, which uses SAM from one-carbon metabolism. The attached corpus does not establish that methylation-cycle diet changes noradrenaline signalling. Glycaemic volatility can change arousal indirectly; that is not a noradrenaline dietary finding.

Together this mechanism extends catecholamine coverage from dopamine formation to noradrenergic attention and executive modulation.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

Structural and biochemical evidence establishes copper and ascorbate participation in the defining dopamine-to-noradrenaline conversion. [[10]](#pm-ref-10) [[9]](#pm-ref-9) Animal pharmacology and neuronal recordings separately support receptor-dependent prefrontal regulation and firing-pattern responses to attended cues. [[16]](#pm-ref-16) [[18]](#pm-ref-18) These findings support distinct synthesis and signalling processes; they do not establish that changing dietary precursor or cofactor intake changes brain noradrenaline firing or attention.

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS2(FM1) — Methylation Cycle Efficiency](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-methylation-cycle-efficiency) — Catecholamine inactivation can use COMT, which depends on SAM from one-carbon metabolism. The attached corpus does not establish that ordinary methylation-cycle variation controls noradrenaline signalling or attention.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS1-FM1-PM1 - Amino-Acid Availability & Prioritisation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation) — Supplies the circulating amino-acid pool, including tyrosine used on the catecholamine path to noradrenaline. Amino-acid quality and precursor-pool adequacy are assessed there. That is precursor supply, not noradrenaline signalling.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM2 - LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation) — Governs competitive brain entry of large neutral amino acids, including tyrosine, after a meal. Transport bias is not noradrenaline synthesis or receptor signalling.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM3 - Dopaminergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm3-dopaminergic-signalling-regulation) — Forms dopamine, the immediate precursor converted to noradrenaline, and shares the tyrosine-hydroxylase and decarboxylase steps. Dopaminergic signalling is not noradrenergic signalling.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM5 - Serotonergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm5-serotonergic-signalling-regulation) — Parallel monoamine path from shared precursor transport. It does not feed noradrenaline synthesis.
