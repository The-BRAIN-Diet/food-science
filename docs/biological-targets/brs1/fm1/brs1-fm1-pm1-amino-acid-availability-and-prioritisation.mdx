### Overview

Food protein supplies indispensable amino acids—the amino acids the body cannot make in sufficient amounts—including phenylalanine and tryptophan [Mariotti et al. (2019) [[1]](#pm-ref-1); Moughan and Lim (2024) [[10]](#pm-ref-10)]. Phenylalanine can also be converted to tyrosine, so this mechanism concerns dietary provision of the circulating precursor pool before brain entry [Matthews (2007) [[11]](#pm-ref-11)]. It excludes competition for transport across the blood–brain barrier and conversion into neurotransmitters [Fernstrom (2013) [[2]](#pm-ref-2); Aquili (2020) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)]. Its role is to define precursor provision before those later neural processes begin.

* **Mechanism boundary:** Covers dietary provision of indispensable amino acids and the circulating precursor pool; LAT1-mediated brain entry and downstream neurotransmitter conversion are separate mechanisms [Fernstrom (2013) [[2]](#pm-ref-2); Aquili (2020) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)].
* **Evidence boundary:** Intake adequacy and DIAAS (Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score) assess amino-acid coverage and digestibility; they do not measure brain uptake or monoamine production [Mariotti et al. (2019) [[1]](#pm-ref-1); Moughan and Lim (2024) [[10]](#pm-ref-10)].
* **Biological relevance:** Phenylalanine-derived tyrosine and indispensable tryptophan contribute to monoamine precursor biology, but that dependency is not evidence that extra protein or precursor supplementation improves ADHD [Matthews (2007) [[11]](#pm-ref-11); Aquili (2020) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)].

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Neurotransmitter-relevant biology depends first on the availability of sufficient dietary amino-acid substrate. Before transport, synthesis, signalling, or recycling can occur, meals must establish an adequate and appropriately balanced amino-acid pool capable of supporting downstream neural pathways.

#### (Amino-acid pool sufficiency)

Protein quantity and distribution influence the size and stability of the amino-acid pool available to the body throughout the day. Sparse protein intake, prolonged gaps between protein-containing meals, or chronically low amino-acid availability may reduce the substrate available to downstream neurotransmitter-related processes.

This mechanism therefore focuses on meal-level amino-acid sufficiency rather than isolated nutrient supplementation or acute precursor loading.

#### (Protein quality and amino-acid coverage)

Protein quality influences whether the dietary pattern provides adequate coverage of indispensable amino acids and neurotransmitter-relevant substrates → [Mariotti et al., 2019]

A variety of plant-protein sources can support indispensable amino-acid coverage across the diet. That is not a requirement to pair complementary plant proteins within each meal → [Marsh et al., 2013]; [Mariotti et al., 2019]

Within BRS1, tryptophan is an indispensable serotonergic precursor in this pool. Tyrosine is a catecholamine precursor normally formed from indispensable phenylalanine; it is not itself an indispensable amino acid → [Matthews, 2007]. The role of this PM is to establish adequate substrate availability rather than directly regulate neurotransmitter production.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

This PM governs amino-acid availability and prioritisation only.

Competitive transport of amino acids across the blood–brain barrier, including LNAA competition and transport bias, is handled by [BRS1-FM1-PM2 - LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation) → [Fernstrom, 2013]

Enzymatic conversion of amino acids into neurotransmitters depends on downstream cofactors and regulatory mechanisms represented elsewhere within BRS1 and [BRS2](/docs/biological-targets/methylation-one-carbon-metabolism).

#### (Integration within BRS1)

This PM establishes circulating indispensable amino-acid coverage for downstream neurotransmitter-related processes. Competitive LNAA transport is handled by [BRS1-FM1-PM2 - LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation). Conversion, signalling, and recycling are handled by sibling PMs.
