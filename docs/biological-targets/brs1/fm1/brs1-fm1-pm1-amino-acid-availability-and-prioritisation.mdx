    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: >-
      In pooled human trials, about half of the phenylalanine from labelled
      dietary protein appeared in the circulation over five hours. Casein
      delivered less than whey or milk. A larger dose delivered more. Older
      adults retained slightly less in the circulation than young adults.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      This is the measured supply step. It is plasma phenylalanine, not
      tryptophan, and not brain dopamine, noradrenaline or serotonin.
    finding_statement: >-
      Dietary protein releases phenylalanine into the human circulation. Protein
      type, dose and age change the fraction that appears. That appearance is
      not a neurotransmitter result.
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: >-
      Gorissen and colleagues pooled 18 trials in 602 people who consumed
      intrinsically labelled whey, casein or milk protein. Over five hours, 50%
      ± 14% of dietary protein-derived phenylalanine appeared in the
      circulation. Casein was 45% ± 11%, whey 57% ± 10% and milk 65% ± 13%.
      Appearance rose with dose. In the age subset, older adults showed 45% ±
      10% and young adults 51% ± 14%.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      The tracer is phenylalanine. The trials were built to study muscle protein
      handling. They do not measure tryptophan, brain entry or neurotransmitter
      synthesis, and they do not show that a higher plasma peak improves those
      later steps.

### Overview

Food protein supplies indispensable amino acids—the amino acids the body cannot make in sufficient amounts—including phenylalanine and tryptophan. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Phenylalanine can also be converted to tyrosine. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) After a protein meal, dietary phenylalanine appears in the circulation. [[6]](#pm-ref-6) Tyrosine and tryptophan are precursors later signalling can use for dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin. [[2]](#pm-ref-2) Protein that covers those amino acids is the supported dietary route. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)

- **Benefits:** These amino acids, including tyrosine made from phenylalanine, are how meal protein supplies the pool that dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin pathways draw on. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) That capacity is not evidence that extra protein improves attention or mood. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)
- **Implementation Notes:** Dietary protein, indispensable amino-acid supply, and tryptophan are the provision routes. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Tyrosine is made from phenylalanine, so it is not classed as an indispensable amino acid. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) No intake dose is established. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[6]](#pm-ref-6) Each meal does not have to supply the full set. [[5]](#pm-ref-5)
- **Biological Relevance:** Those amino acids still have to cross into the brain before they are used to make dopamine, noradrenaline or serotonin - see [PM2](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation).

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Later signalling can use tyrosine and tryptophan only after those amino acids are available in the circulation. A protein meal is what puts dietary phenylalanine there, and phenylalanine can be converted to tyrosine. Brain entry and neurotransmitter synthesis come after that.

<div class="brs-fm-hub-shell">
<button type="button" class="brs-fm-hub-summary" aria-expanded="false">

#### (Circulating supply)

Gorissen et al. (2020) [[6]](#pm-ref-6) pooled human trials of labelled whey, casein and milk protein. About half of the dietary phenylalanine appeared in the circulation over five hours. Casein delivered a smaller fraction than whey or milk, a larger dose delivered more, and older adults retained slightly less in the circulation than young adults. The measured endpoint is plasma phenylalanine, not brain dopamine, noradrenaline or serotonin.

#### (Coverage across the diet)

Mariotti and Gardner (2019) [[1]](#pm-ref-1) found that adult vegetarian diets which include protein-rich plant foods can meet protein and amino-acid requirements. Moughan and Lim (2024) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) score protein quality by digestible indispensable amino acids, not by grams alone. Marsh et al. (2013) [[5]](#pm-ref-5) state that varied plant proteins across the diet can cover that set, so complementary proteins do not have to share every meal.

#### (Phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan)

Matthews (2007) [[4]](#pm-ref-4) shows that humans convert phenylalanine to tyrosine, mainly in the liver. Tyrosine is normally dispensable because of that conversion, and it can become conditionally indispensable when phenylalanine is limited. Aquili (2020) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) names tryptophan as the serotonin precursor and tyrosine as the dopamine precursor. Those names identify the amino acids in this pool. They do not show that a higher circulating supply raises brain serotonin or dopamine.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

Competitive transport of amino acids across the blood–brain barrier is [BRS1-FM1-PM2 — LAT1 Competitive Transport Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm2-lat1-competitive-transport-modulation). Dopamine synthesis and signalling are [BRS1-FM1-PM3 — Dopaminergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm3-dopaminergic-signalling-regulation). Noradrenaline signalling is [BRS1-FM1-PM4 — Noradrenergic Signalling](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm4-noradrenergic-signalling-attention-executive-modulation). Serotonin signalling is [BRS1-FM1-PM5 — Serotonergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm5-serotonergic-signalling-regulation).

#### (Integration within BRS1)

This mechanism supplies the circulating pool at the start of [BRS1(FM1)](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-monoaminergic-function). Later mechanisms in that pathway draw on it. A shared key-constraint mapping is not established.

Tyrosine and tryptophan are the precursors later pathways use. None of these results shows that extra protein raises brain dopamine, noradrenaline or serotonin. Spreading protein across the day was not shown to set this pool.

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.
