  Influence competition for transport across the blood–brain barrier so

summary: >-
  LAT1 is a shared transporter that carries tyrosine, tryptophan and other large
  neutral amino acids into the brain. Each one competes according to its level
  in the blood relative to the others. In healthy adults, carbohydrate-rich and
  protein-rich meals shift those blood ratios in different directions. Those
  ratios decide which precursors are favoured for brain entry.

    finding_summary: >-
      Tyrosine, tryptophan and other large neutral amino acids share LAT1 at the
      blood–brain barrier. Relative plasma concentration, not the absolute
      amount of one amino acid, shapes which precursor is favoured for brain
      entry.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      This establishes competitive transport biology. It does not show that
      eating more tyrosine or tryptophan increases brain monoamines or improves
      function.
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_statement: >-
      Large neutral amino acids share LAT1-mediated blood–brain-barrier
      transport, so each precursor’s plasma concentration relative to competing
      LNAAs determines transport bias at a given meal.
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: >-
      Review evidence identifies LAT1 as a shared, saturable shuttle for
      tyrosine, tryptophan, phenylalanine and other LNAAs. Brain uptake tracks
      the plasma ratio of a given LNAA to the sum of its competitors. That
      competitive rule is the capacity this PM represents.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      The synthesis is review-level rather than a new human transport
      experiment. It does not quantify everyday meal effects, measure
      neurotransmitter synthesis in the cited meal studies, or establish a
      clinical outcome.

### Overview

LAT1 is a shared transporter that carries tyrosine, tryptophan and other large neutral amino acids into the brain. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) Each one competes according to its level in the blood relative to the others. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) In healthy adults, carbohydrate-rich and protein-rich meals shift those blood ratios in different directions. [[2]](#pm-ref-2) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Those ratios decide which precursors are favoured for brain entry. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)

- **Benefits:** The precursor favoured for entry is how a meal’s amino-acid pattern reaches the brain’s supply for dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) The meal studies measured blood ratios in healthy adults, not that formation. [[2]](#pm-ref-2) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)
- **Implementation Notes:** Meal carbohydrate-to-protein composition is the lever. Carbohydrate-rich meals raised the tryptophan ratio, and protein-rich meals raised the tyrosine ratio. [[2]](#pm-ref-2) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Tyrosine and tryptophan are cargo amino acids, not a reason to take more of either, and no best ratio is established. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)
- **Biological Relevance:** This is the brain-entry step after [dietary amino-acid supply](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation); dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin formation come later. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

After [BRS1-FM1-PM1](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation) establishes substrate availability, neurotransmitter-relevant precursors still reach the brain only through shared LAT1 transport. Relative plasma LNAA concentrations at a given meal—not protein quantity alone—determine which aromatic precursors gain competitive advantage at the barrier.

#### (LAT1 and competitive precursor transport)

Tyrosine, tryptophan, and other large neutral amino acids share the LAT1 transporter at the blood–brain barrier. Transport is competitive: each precursor's plasma concentration relative to competing LNAAs shapes transport bias at that meal → [Fernstrom, 2013]

#### (Insulin-mediated partitioning and meal macronutrient bias)

Post-prandial insulin and macronutrient structure partition amino acids between peripheral uptake and plasma availability. Carbohydrate-rich meals can increase tryptophan relative to competing LNAAs; protein-forward meals favour tyrosine and broader LNAA competition → [Wurtman et al., 2003]

Meal structure thereby biases catecholamine- versus serotonin-relevant precursor presentation at the barrier without constituting direct neurotransmitter dosing.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

Amino-acid pool sufficiency, completeness, and neurotransmitter-relevant prioritisation are handled upstream by [BRS1-FM1-PM1 - Amino-Acid Availability & Prioritisation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation).

Enzymatic conversion of amino acids into neurotransmitters, cofactor-dependent synthesis, and downstream signalling are represented elsewhere within BRS1. Competitive transport adjustments cannot compensate for chronically low substrate supply.

#### (Integration within BRS1)

Primarily supports [BRS1(FM1) - Monoaminergic Function](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-monoaminergic-function) when meal patterns shift catecholamine versus serotonin precursor bias at the blood–brain barrier.

Depends on [BRS1-FM1-PM1 - Amino-Acid Availability & Prioritisation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation): without sufficient dietary amino-acid substrate at meals, transport competition has little meaningful precursor load to partition.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

Human meal studies show that carbohydrate-versus-protein composition changes circulating tryptophan-to-competing-amino-acid ratios, with reciprocal tyrosine-ratio changes also observed. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) Reviewed transporter evidence explains why these ratios matter for competitive brain entry. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) Together, the findings support meal-responsive precursor availability, while the meal studies stop at plasma measurements and do not establish changes in brain uptake, monoamine signalling or functional outcomes.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS1-FM1-PM1 - Amino-Acid Availability & Prioritisation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm1-amino-acid-availability-and-prioritisation) — Supplies the circulating amino-acid pool whose tyrosine and tryptophan then compete at LAT1. That is substrate supply, not transport.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM3 - Dopaminergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm3-dopaminergic-signalling-regulation) — Uses tyrosine after LAT1 presentation for dopamine synthesis. Transport bias is not dopaminergic signalling.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM4 - Noradrenergic Signalling](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm4-noradrenergic-signalling-attention-executive-modulation) — Uses tyrosine after the same transport step for noradrenaline synthesis. Downstream conversion, not a LAT1 input.
- [BRS1-FM1-PM5 - Serotonergic Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm1/brs1-fm1-pm5-serotonergic-signalling-regulation) — Uses tryptophan after LAT1 presentation for serotonin synthesis. Parallel conversion, not this transporter’s control.
