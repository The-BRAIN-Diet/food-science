fm_id: BRS1(FM1)
parent_brs: BRS1
mission: >-
  Maintain reliable monoaminergic signalling so attention, motivation, arousal,
  and emotional regulation stay supported across everyday cognitive and
  emotional demands.
summary: >-
  Monoaminergic function brings together amino-acid precursor supply,
  competitive entry into the brain, and regulation of dopamine, noradrenaline
  and serotonin signalling. Supply supports synthesis, while neuronal activity,
  receptors and clearance help determine how signals operate in different brain
  regions. These processes contribute to attention, arousal, motivation, mood
  and behavioural regulation. Evidence for their biological roles does not by
  itself establish that increasing nutrient intake improves these functions.

functional_rationale: >-
  Reliable monoaminergic function depends on precursor access and regulated
  signalling, providing a biological basis for attention, arousal, motivation
  and emotional regulation without implying a uniform response across regions or
  individuals.
fm_evidence_summary: >-
  Protein-handling evidence supports flexible precursor provision; human meal
  studies show changes in circulating precursor ratios, while biochemical
  evidence establishes synthesis requirements. Human dopamine pharmacology and
  imaging show baseline- and receptor-dependent responses; noradrenergic
  recordings and serotonergic reviews add distinct signalling context. These are
  complementary measurements, not one experiment validating the whole FM.
  {{cite:trommelen_anabolic_2023,fanet_tetrahydrobioterin_2021,ashley_breakfast_1985,wurtman_effects_2003,cools_inverted-u_2011,westbrook_dopamine_2020,aston_jones_attended_cues_1994,berger_serotonin_2009}}


  If precursor provision, brain entry and the three signalling systems function
  adequately, the evidence supports a plausible capacity to sustain
  monoaminergic regulation across changing demands. It does not establish that
  raising precursor intake improves that capacity, that all branches move
  together, or that their combination produces superior cognitive outcomes.

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Maintain reliable monoaminergic signalling so attention, motivation, arousal, and emotional regulation stay supported across everyday cognitive and emotional demands.

### Overview

Monoaminergic function brings together amino-acid precursor supply, competitive entry into the brain, and regulation of dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin signalling. Supply supports synthesis, while neuronal activity, receptors and clearance help determine how signals operate in different brain regions. These processes contribute to attention, arousal, motivation, mood and behavioural regulation. Evidence for their biological roles does not by itself establish that increasing nutrient intake improves these functions. Fernstrom (2013) [\[3\]](#fm-ref-3); Cools & D’Esposito (2011) [\[12\]](#fm-ref-12); O’Donnell et al. (2012) [\[13\]](#fm-ref-13); Berger et al. (2009) [\[14\]](#fm-ref-14).

* **Biological job:** Coordinate precursor provision, brain entry and regulated signalling across the three transmitter systems.
* **Functional relevance:** Provide biological context for attention, arousal, motivation and behavioural regulation, with region- and baseline-dependent effects.
* **Dietary boundary:** Adequate substrates and cofactors support synthesis; meal-driven plasma ratios do not establish brain signalling changes or cognitive benefit Ashley et al. (1985) [\[1\]](#fm-ref-1); Wurtman et al. (2003) [\[2\]](#fm-ref-2).

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

↑ precursor availability; regulated competitive brain entry; context-dependent dopaminergic, noradrenergic and serotonergic signalling

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

<span id="42-integrated-functional-narrative" />

### 4.1 Functional Rationale

Reliable monoaminergic function depends on precursor access and regulated signalling, providing a biological basis for attention, arousal, motivation and emotional regulation without implying a uniform response across regions or individuals.

<span id="44-evidence-highlights" />

### 4.2 Evidence Summary

Protein-handling evidence supports flexible precursor provision; human meal studies show changes in circulating precursor ratios, while biochemical evidence establishes synthesis requirements. Human dopamine pharmacology and imaging show baseline- and receptor-dependent responses; noradrenergic recordings and serotonergic reviews add distinct signalling context. These are complementary measurements, not one experiment validating the whole FM. [[1](#fm-ref-1), [2](#fm-ref-2), [12](#fm-ref-12), [14](#fm-ref-14), [18](#fm-ref-18), [19](#fm-ref-19), [20](#fm-ref-20), [21](#fm-ref-21)]

If precursor provision, brain entry and the three signalling systems function adequately, the evidence supports a plausible capacity to sustain monoaminergic regulation across changing demands. It does not establish that raising precursor intake improves that capacity, that all branches move together, or that their combination produces superior cognitive outcomes.

### 4.3 Suboptimal Function & Its Effects

Insufficient precursor availability, unfavourable transport competition or altered signalling regulation can disturb different parts of monoaminergic capacity. Their consequences depend on the affected transmitter, brain region and baseline state; reduced precursor provision and altered receptor or clearance measures are not interchangeable Fernstrom (2013) [\[3\]](#fm-ref-3); Cools & D’Esposito (2011) [\[12\]](#fm-ref-12); O’Donnell et al. (2012) [\[13\]](#fm-ref-13).

Attention, arousal, motivation and emotional-regulation consequences remain separately assessed functional hypotheses. This section does not claim that all branches decline together or that dietary correction necessarily restores these outcomes.
