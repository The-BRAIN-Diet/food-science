# BRS2 → BRS1 — One-Carbon Support for Neurotransmitter Regulation

One-carbon metabolism supports selected components of [neurotransmitter regulation](/docs/biological-targets/neurotransmitter-regulation) through methyl-transfer capacity, phosphatidylcholine metabolism and a mediated connection with cellular redox regulation.

## Dependency

[BRS2](/docs/biological-targets/methylation-one-carbon-metabolism) supplies SAMe-dependent methyl-transfer capacity and contributes to phosphatidylcholine synthesis through PEMT-related metabolism. These processes can influence gene-regulatory, neurotransmitter-metabolic and membrane conditions relevant to [BRS1](/docs/biological-targets/neurotransmitter-regulation). Through [transsulfuration](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm5-transsulfuration-pathway), BRS2 also contributes cysteine to [glutathione synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm6-glutathione-synthesis), creating an indirect route through [BRS3](/docs/biological-targets/inflammation-oxidative-stress) redox regulation.

The complementary [CDP-choline/Kennedy pathway](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm3/brs2-fm3-pm7-phosphatidylcholine-formation) also forms phosphatidylcholine, but it is not a BRS2 methyl-transfer output. It is listed here only as the non-PEMT route that can supply the same membrane phospholipid.

## Network Interpretation

These routes do not form a single monoamine-synthesis pathway. They represent complementary enabling conditions: methyl-transfer capacity, membrane phospholipid metabolism and transsulfuration-linked redox support. BRS2 may therefore constrain aspects of BRS1 when these capacities are insufficient, but it does not replace amino-acid precursor availability or neurotransmitter-specific enzymatic regulation. Nucleotide synthesis and DNA synthesis/repair are not principal routes on this page; they are recognised [future-development](/docs/biological-targets/methylation-one-carbon-metabolism#future-development) areas of BRS2 rather than current PM-backed dependencies.

> **BH4 boundary:** Tetrahydrobiopterin is required for aromatic amino-acid hydroxylase activity but is not produced by one-carbon metabolism. It is therefore treated as a separate cross-system substance rather than as a BRS2 resource. See [Tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4)](/docs/substances/bioactive-compounds/endogenous-cofactors/tetrahydrobiopterin).

## Boundary and Evidence Status

The underlying one-carbon, phosphatidylcholine and transsulfuration pathways are established biochemistry. Their integration as a BRS2 → BRS1 dependency is a framework interpretation. The relationship does not establish that increasing methyl donors raises monoamine production, that one-carbon insufficiency generally causes ADHD-related neurotransmitter dysfunction, or that modifying these pathways produces clinical benefit.

## Supporting Evidence

- [Chiang et al., 1996](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#chiang_s-adenosylmethionine_1996) — Reviews S-adenosylmethionine as the principal cellular methyl donor and its roles in transmethylation pathways.
- [Vance, 2014](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#vance_phospholipid_2014) — Reviews PEMT-dependent phosphatidylethanolamine methylation to phosphatidylcholine and the contribution of that route to membrane phospholipid pools.
- [Gibellini and Smith, 2010](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#gibellini_kennedy_2010) — Reviews de novo phosphatidylcholine and phosphatidylethanolamine synthesis through the CDP-choline/Kennedy pathway.
- [Lu, 2013](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#lu_glutathione_synthesis_2013) — Reviews glutathione synthesis and the contribution of cysteine supply, including transsulfuration-linked precursor availability.
- [Kennedy, 2016](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#kennedy_b_2016) — Reviews B-vitamin involvement in one-carbon metabolism and brain function, supporting the general relevance of nutritional sufficiency without establishing a direct clinical BRS2 → BRS1 effect.
