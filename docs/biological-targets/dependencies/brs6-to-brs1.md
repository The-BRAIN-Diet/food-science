# BRS6 → BRS1 — Stress-Axis and Autonomic Shaping of Neurotransmission

Neuroendocrine, glycaemic and autonomic signals from [BRS6](/docs/biological-targets/metabolic-neuroendocrine-stress) can constrain [neurotransmitter regulation](/docs/biological-targets/neurotransmitter-regulation) directly and through mediated energetic and inflammatory routes.

## Dependency

BRS6 allocates cortisol rhythm, autonomic balance and glucose supply. Those mediators can change the receptor, energetic and inflammatory conditions in which BRS1 operates. BRS6 is not a neurotransmitter system.

## Principal Routes

<div className="markdown-table-scroll">

| Route | Contribution to BRS1 | Relationship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HPA-axis / cortisol rhythm | Alters arousal, receptor sensitivity and stress-linked transmitter tone | Direct but context-dependent |
| Autonomic (vagal/sympathetic) balance | Couples peripheral stress physiology to central signalling | Direct |
| Glucocorticoid–glucose load via mitochondria and inflammation | Changes the energetic and redox environment of synapses | Mediated (`BRS6 → BRS4/BRS3 → BRS1`) |

</div>

## Network Interpretation

Direct BRS6 → BRS1 routes are neuroendocrine and autonomic. The Picard cascade is a named mediated path through [BRS4](/docs/biological-targets/mitochondrial-function-bioenergetics) and [BRS3](/docs/biological-targets/inflammation-oxidative-stress), summarised below. Allostatic-load language is used here only for that stress-allocation sequence, not as a generic description of every BRS.

> **BH4 boundary:** Tetrahydrobiopterin is oxidation-sensitive and relevant to monoamine synthesis, but it is not a BRS6 output. See [Tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4)](/docs/substances/bioactive-compounds/endogenous-cofactors/tetrahydrobiopterin).

## Boundary and Evidence Status

HPA, autonomic and mitochondrial-stress models are established at review level. Their assembly as a BRS6 → BRS1 dependency is a framework interpretation. The cascade table maps existing BRAIN levers onto that model; it does not show that the full sequence has been demonstrated in ADHD or that those levers interrupt it.

## Supporting Evidence

- [McEwen, 1998](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#mcewen_protective_damaging_1998) — Defines allostatic load as cumulative wear when stress mediators protect or damage brain function under sustained demand.
- [McEwen, 2006](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#mcewen_stress_mediators_2006) — Reviews the brain as interpreter and target of stress-mediated regulation.
- [Thayer et al., 2012](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#thayer_2012_22178086) — Meta-analysis linking heart-rate variability to neuroimaging markers of stress and health.
- [Picard, Juster and McEwen, 2014](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#picard_mitochondrial_allostatic_load_2014) — Describes glucocorticoid and glucose effects on mitochondria as a route into bioenergetic and inflammatory strain.

## Cascade 6 — BRS6 → BRS4/BRS3 → BRS1 {#cascade-6}

[Picard, Juster and McEwen (2014)](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#picard_mitochondrial_allostatic_load_2014) describe a cascade in which chronic psychosocial or metabolic stress disrupts glucocorticoid signalling and glucose regulation within [BRS6](/docs/biological-targets/metabolic-neuroendocrine-stress), inducing mitochondrial change within [BRS4](/docs/biological-targets/mitochondrial-function-bioenergetics). The resulting bioenergetic and redox strain may increase reactive oxygen species and inflammatory signalling through [BRS3](/docs/biological-targets/inflammation-oxidative-stress), thereby altering the energetic, inflammatory and receptor environments in which neurotransmitter regulation occurs within [BRS1](/docs/biological-targets/neurotransmitter-regulation).

This table maps those cascade processes to the most relevant Primary Mechanisms and to the diet, optimisation and lifestyle levers already evidenced on those BRAIN pages. ADHD citations are the BRS-hub sources that connect each system to ADHD. They do not show that the complete glucocorticoid–glucose–mitochondrial cascade has been demonstrated in ADHD, or that modifying these levers interrupts the cascade or improves ADHD outcomes.
