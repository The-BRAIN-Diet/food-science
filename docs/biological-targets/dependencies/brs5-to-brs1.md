# BRS5 → BRS1 — Gut–Vagal Modulation of Neurotransmitter Regulation

Gut–vagal and microbial signals can reach [neurotransmitter regulation](/docs/biological-targets/neurotransmitter-regulation), but the routes are conditional and often indirect.

## Dependency

[BRS5](/docs/biological-targets/gut-brain-axis-enteric-nervous-system) can transmit vagal afferent signals and microbial metabolites that alter central transmitter receptors or precursor handling. These inputs may modify BRS1 operating conditions; they do not substitute for neuronal enzyme systems or amino-acid precursor supply.

## Network Interpretation

A direct BRS5 → BRS1 claim should be reserved for vagal and selected metabolite signals. Inflammatory or kynurenine consequences of gut immune activation belong to `BRS5 → BRS3 → BRS1`. Stress-axis consequences belong to [BRS5 → BRS6](/docs/biological-targets/dependencies/brs5-to-brs6).

## Boundary and Evidence Status

Human causal evidence that gut interventions restore BRS1 neurotransmitter function is limited. Bravo et al. is a mouse vagus-dependent study, not a human ADHD trial. The dependency is retained as a conditional framework interpretation.

## Supporting Evidence

- [Bravo et al., 2011](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#bravo_ingestion_2011) — Mouse study: a Lactobacillus strain altered emotional behaviour and central GABA receptor expression via the vagus nerve.
- [Jaggar et al., 2020](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#jaggar_sex_microbiota_gut_brain_axis_2020) — Reviews microbiota–gut–brain signalling, including sex differences; it does not establish a clinical BRS5 → BRS1 treatment effect.
