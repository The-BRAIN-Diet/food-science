## BRS5-FM1-PM1 - Gut Barrier / Tight Junction Integrity

(Maintaining the Epithelial Gate Between Gut and Body)

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Maintain epithelial tight junction integrity and selective permeability at the gut lining.

### Overview

The gut lining acts as a selective gate between the gut lumen and the body. Tight junctions (seals between epithelial cells) control paracellular passage — what moves between cells rather than through them. When junctions weaken, the physical barrier loses selectivity and inappropriate leakage may increase. Its independently adjudicated requirements include butyrate/fibre provision, retinoid signalling, cellular zinc, and glutamine substrate biology; these relationships do not form one shared nutrient pool.

* Maintains tight junction proteins and epithelial repair capacity at the gut lining.
* Preserves selective control over paracellular passage between the gut lumen and the body.
* Separates direct epithelial mechanisms from upstream microbial provision, downstream containment and unsupported food-level generalisations.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects
↑ tight-junction strength; ↓ permeability

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

BRS5-FM1-PM1 owns the epithelial tight-junction gate and its selective paracellular permeability. Tight-junction-dependent pore and leak pathways are distinct from unrestricted permeability caused by epithelial injury [Chanez-Paredes and Turner, 2023]. Butyrate can directly support junction assembly in an epithelial model, while fermentable dietary fibre lies upstream of microbial short-chain-fatty-acid production [Peng et al., 2009; Silva et al., 2020]. Retinoid signalling, cellular zinc, and glutamine substrate biology carry separate epithelial relationships and limitations [Cantorna et al., 2019; Miyoshi et al., 2016; Rao and Samak, 2012; Abbasi et al., 2024].

#### (The physical gate)

This PM owns the host epithelial barrier — tight junction proteins, mucus support, and epithelial repair that keep the gut lining a selective gate. The biological question is whether paracellular passage is appropriately controlled, not how much endotoxin or immune activation follows once exposure rises.

#### (Dietary support logic)

Microbially produced butyrate can act at the epithelium as a metabolic substrate, while fermentable dietary fibre is an upstream microbial substrate-provision relationship. Retinoic acid supports epithelial differentiation and tight-junction regulation, with vitamin A as its dietary precursor relationship [Cantorna et al., 2019]. Cellular zinc is required for occludin and claudin-3 maintenance [Miyoshi et al., 2016], and glutamine is an enterocyte substrate whose dietary addressability is condition-dependent [Rao and Samak, 2012; Abbasi et al., 2024]. Omega-3 evidence supports modulation, not a PM1 Dietary Requirement, and generic protein provision is not inferred from glutamine biology.

#### (Integration)

When selective permeability weakens, luminal contents may cross more readily — [BRS5-FM1-PM2 — LPS / Endotoxin Containment](/docs/biological-targets/brs5/fm1/brs5-fm1-pm2-lps-endotoxin-containment) owns containment of inflammatory microbial products once exposure rises; [BRS5-FM1-PM3 — Keystone Taxa Support](/docs/biological-targets/brs5/fm1/brs5-fm1-pm3-keystone-taxa-support) supports microbial ecology that reduces barrier strain.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Introduction/Summary

Intestinal tight junctions define selective paracellular permeability. Butyrate can promote junction assembly in epithelial models, but evidence for food-level, dose-responsive control in humans is limited. The LGG evidence is a pneumonia mouse intervention and should not be generalised to fermented foods.
