### 3. Evidence Base

#### Summary

Polyphenol exposure and plant-food variety are distinct dietary concepts, and the attached human evidence does not establish them as one shared nutritionally constrained pool. Wastyk et al. compared high-fibre and high-fermented-food diets rather than isolating polyphenols or plant variety, while Singh et al. showed that urolithin A exposure depends on ellagitannin exposure and variable microbial conversion and can be bypassed by direct supplementation ([Wastyk et al., 2021](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#wastyk_gut-microbiota-targeted_2021); [Singh et al., 2022](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#singh_direct_2022)).

- **Constraint and membership boundary:** Neither broad polyphenol exposure nor plant-food variety is established here as a constituent of one shared limiting resource; a dietary pattern, a compound class, and microbial conversion capacity are not interchangeable biological pools.
- **Evidence and measurement boundary:** The cited studies establish diet-associated microbiome and immune responses, plus variable production and exposure to one microbial polyphenol metabolite; they do not measure a combined polyphenol-and-diversity pool or show that its insufficiency constrains the listed mechanisms ([Wastyk et al., 2021](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#wastyk_gut-microbiota-targeted_2021); [Singh et al., 2022](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#singh_direct_2022)).
- **Biological relevance and provision limitation:** These exposures may remain relevant to particular microbial processes, but the available evidence does not justify a shared-resource requirement, an adequacy threshold, or a claim that increasing either exposure improves downstream function or clinical outcomes.
