summary: 'Methionine synthase transfers a methyl group from 5-methyltetrahydrofolate to homocysteine, forming methionine and tetrahydrofolate. Methylcobalamin, the vitamin B12 cofactor, takes part in that transfer. Regenerated methionine can later be used to form SAMe, in a separate reaction. Folic acid and vitamin B12 can lower blood homocysteine. That change is a circulating marker, not tissue remethylation flux.'

    finding_label: Methionine synthase remethylates homocysteine
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: 'Methionine synthase transfers the methyl group of 5-methyltetrahydrofolate to homocysteine, producing methionine and tetrahydrofolate. Methylcobalamin carries that methyl group. Betaine remethylation, SAMe synthesis, transsulfuration and phospholipid methylation are different reactions.'
    finding_interpretation: 'This defines the reaction and its direct participants. It does not show that eating more folate or vitamin B12 speeds the reaction in human tissues, and it does not make riboflavin a cofactor of the synthase.'
    finding_statement: 'Cobalamin-dependent methionine synthase transfers the methyl group of 5-methyltetrahydrofolate to homocysteine, producing methionine and tetrahydrofolate, and uses methylcobalamin as its cofactor. Betaine-dependent remethylation, methionine adenosyltransferase, transsulfuration and phosphatidylethanolamine methylation are separate reactions.'
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: 'A human-pathway review states the reaction directly: methionine synthase (MTR) converts homocysteine plus 5-methyltetrahydrofolate to methionine plus tetrahydrofolate and requires the methylated form of cobalamin. Methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase produces that folate substrate. Betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase is a separate, mainly liver and kidney route. Methionine adenosyltransferase then converts methionine to SAMe, and cystathionine beta-synthase starts transsulfuration. The review also traces dietary folate, including folic acid reduced by dihydrofolate reductase, into 5-methyltetrahydrofolate, and dietary cobalamin through absorption and intracellular processing onto the enzyme as methylcobalamin.'
    synthesis_limitations: 'The primary source is a biochemical review, not a new human flux study. Reaction identity does not show that ordinary dietary intake controls tissue remethylation rate, and it does not assign clinical benefit. Phosphatidylethanolamine methylation is a later SAMe-consuming reaction, not part of this step.'

### Overview

Methionine synthase transfers a methyl group from 5-methyltetrahydrofolate to homocysteine, forming methionine and tetrahydrofolate. [[9]](#pm-ref-9) Methylcobalamin, the vitamin B12 cofactor, takes part in that transfer. [[9]](#pm-ref-9) Regenerated methionine can later be used to form SAMe, in a separate reaction. [[9]](#pm-ref-9) Folic acid and vitamin B12 can lower blood homocysteine. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) That change is a circulating marker, not tissue remethylation flux. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)

- **Benefits:** Remethylating homocysteine restores methionine, which can then be converted into SAMe. [[9]](#pm-ref-9) This helps maintain the methyl-group supply used by later methylation.
- **Implementation Notes:** 5-Methyltetrahydrofolate, vitamin B9 (folate), methylcobalamin, and vitamin B12 (cobalamin) are the provision routes. [[9]](#pm-ref-9) [[1]](#pm-ref-1) Homocysteine is the substrate already in the pathway, not a dietary target. Lowering blood homocysteine does not set a dose for tissue remethylation flux. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)
- **Biological Relevance:** [Betaine-dependent remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation) and [transsulfuration](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm5-transsulfuration-pathway) are neighbouring routes, not this reaction. [[9]](#pm-ref-9)

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Maintaining a usable methionine supply depends on recycling homocysteine through the folate- and B12-dependent route. That reaction feeds methionine toward later SAMe formation, but it is not itself SAMe synthesis, betaine remethylation, or homocysteine disposal.

<div class="brs-fm-hub-item" data-brs-fm-hub>
<div class="brs-fm-hub-shell">
<button type="button" class="brs-fm-hub-summary" aria-expanded="false">
<span class="brs-fm-hub-chevron" aria-hidden="true"></span>
<strong>Folate/B12-dependent homocysteine remethylation — mechanistic detail</strong>
</button>
<div class="brs-fm-hub-panel" hidden>

#### (Methionine synthase reaction)

Methionine synthase transfers a methyl group from 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (a reduced folate carrying one methyl group) to homocysteine. The products are methionine and tetrahydrofolate. The methyl group is carried by methylcobalamin, the active vitamin B12 cofactor on the enzyme [Froese et al. (2019) [[9]](#pm-ref-9)].

Dietary folate, including folic acid after reduction by dihydrofolate reductase, can be converted to that 5-methyltetrahydrofolate substrate. Dietary vitamin B12 is absorbed and processed to methylcobalamin on the enzyme. Those are provision routes, not evidence that a higher intake speeds the reaction in tissues [Froese et al. (2019) [[9]](#pm-ref-9)].

#### (Riboflavin sits beside the reaction)

Riboflavin is the precursor of flavin adenine dinucleotide. That flavin is a cofactor of methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase, the enzyme that makes 5-methyltetrahydrofolate, and of methionine synthase reductase, which repairs inactive cobalamin. It is not the cofactor in the synthase active site. That site uses methylcobalamin [García-Minguillán et al. (2014) [[10]](#pm-ref-10)].

In adults, riboflavin status changed how an upstream reductase variant related to blood homocysteine. That interaction does not place riboflavin on methionine synthase itself [García-Minguillán et al. (2014) [[10]](#pm-ref-10)].

#### (Blood homocysteine is not the reaction rate)

Folic acid lowers blood homocysteine, and vitamin B12 lowers it further. Vitamin B6, which serves a different disposal route, did not add a clear further reduction in the same pooled trials [Collaboration (1998) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. Blood homocysteine is a circulating marker. A lower value does not measure how fast this reaction runs in a tissue.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

This reaction is folate- and B12-dependent remethylation only.

The parallel betaine route, which also makes methionine but does not use 5-methyltetrahydrofolate or vitamin B12, belongs to [BRS2-FM1-PM2 — Betaine/BHMT Remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation). Conversion of methionine to SAMe belongs to [BRS2-FM1-PM3 — SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis). Integrated cycle throughput belongs to [BRS2-FM1-PM4 — Methionine Cycle Flux](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm4-methionine-cycle-flux).

Homocysteine disposal through transsulfuration belongs to [BRS2-FM2-PM5 — Transsulfuration Pathway](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm5-transsulfuration-pathway). SAMe-dependent phospholipid methylation belongs to [BRS2-FM3-PM7 — Phosphatidylcholine Formation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm3/brs2-fm3-pm7-phosphatidylcholine-formation). Later incorporation of DHA into neuronal membranes belongs to [BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation).

B-vitamin effects on cognitive decline can depend on omega-3 status, but that interaction is a later, combined outcome, not a measurement of this reaction [Oulhaj et al. (2016) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)].

#### (Integration within methylation-cycle efficiency)

Within [BRS2(FM1) — Methylation Cycle Efficiency](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-methylation-cycle-efficiency), this is the folate- and B12-dependent way to return homocysteine to methionine. The betaine route is parallel. SAMe formation uses the methionine product later. Folate supply explains where 5-methyltetrahydrofolate comes from. It does not, by itself, show that the wider set of methyl donors limits the tissue rate of this reaction.

[[9]](#pm-ref-9) In an adult observational study, riboflavin status modified the association between an MTHFR variant and circulating homocysteine. [[10]](#pm-ref-10) Together, these findings clarify enzyme-specific dependence and a status–genotype interaction; the circulating marker does not directly measure tissue remethylation flux.

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS1-FM2-PM6 — Acetylcholine Synthesis Support](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm2/brs1-fm2-pm6-acetylcholine-synthesis-support) — This reaction remethylates homocysteine with 5-methyltetrahydrofolate rather than betaine, so it is a parallel methyl route to the path that draws on choline. It supplies methionine, not the choline used for acetylcholine.
- [BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation) — Methionine from this reaction can later become SAMe and support phospholipid methylation. That is downstream use through other reactions, not a substrate step of methionine synthase.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS2-FM1-PM2 — Betaine/BHMT Remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation) — Betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase also remethylates homocysteine, using betaine in liver and kidney and not 5-methyltetrahydrofolate or vitamin B12. Parallel route to the same methionine product.
- [BRS2-FM1-PM3 — SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis) — Methionine produced here is the amino-acid substrate for the later reaction that forms SAMe from methionine and ATP. Downstream use of the product, not part of methionine synthase.
- [BRS2-FM1-PM4 — Methionine Cycle Flux](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm4-methionine-cycle-flux) — Cycle flux combines this remethylation step with SAMe use, homocysteine return and the parallel betaine route. Shared cycle context, not the synthase reaction itself.
