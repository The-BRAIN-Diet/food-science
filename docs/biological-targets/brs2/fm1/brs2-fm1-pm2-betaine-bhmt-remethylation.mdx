summary: 'Betaine–homocysteine methyltransferase transfers a methyl group from betaine to homocysteine, forming methionine and dimethylglycine. A bound zinc ion activates homocysteine. Folate and vitamin B12 are not part of that reaction. Betaine may come from the diet or from oxidation of choline. In healthy men, controlled choline depletion lowered plasma betaine and reduced homocysteine-handling reserve, and repletion reversed that change.'

scientific_findings:
  - id: PM2-F1
    finding_label: BHMT remethylates homocysteine with betaine and catalytic zinc
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: Human betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase (BHMT) transfers one methyl group from betaine to homocysteine. The products are methionine and dimethylglycine. A zinc ion bound by three cysteines activates homocysteine. Folate and vitamin B12 are not part of this reaction.
    finding_interpretation: 'This is the reaction boundary. It does not include folate-dependent remethylation, the later conversion of methionine to SAMe, whole-cycle throughput, phosphatidylethanolamine methyltransferase, or acetylcholine formation. It also does not show that dietary zinc intake changes the reaction in people.'
    finding_statement: 'BHMT transfers a methyl group from betaine to homocysteine, forming methionine and dimethylglycine, without folate or vitamin B12 in that reaction, and uses an active-site zinc ion to activate homocysteine.'
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: 'Human crystal structures show that BHMT makes methionine from betaine and homocysteine and uses zinc to activate homocysteine. Mutating any of the three zinc-binding cysteines to alanine abolishes activity and sharply reduces zinc binding, and the same biochemical work states the products as methionine and dimethylglycine. A methyl-donor review states that methyl flow through this pathway does not depend on folate or vitamin B12. Together these sources identify the reaction and its metal. They do not test ordinary dietary control or human tissue flux.'
    synthesis_limitations: 'The enzyme evidence uses purified or recombinant human protein, not intact human metabolism. Loss of activity after removing zinc ligands does not show that dietary zinc usually limits the enzyme. The review statement that the pathway is independent of folate and vitamin B12 is not a new flux experiment. Quantitative claims in that review about choline deficiency and liver methionine were not re-checked in the primary animal papers.'

    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: 'A methyl-donor review states that betaine is produced by irreversible oxidation of choline through choline dehydrogenase and betaine-aldehyde dehydrogenase, and that BHMT then uses betaine. A choline-dehydrogenase review places the human enzyme in the inner mitochondrial membrane, mainly in kidney and liver, and separates the first oxidation to betaine aldehyde from the second oxidation to betaine. Betaine then donates a methyl group in the homocysteine-to-methionine reaction. The two steps are supply, not the BHMT reaction.'
    synthesis_limitations: 'Purified, stable human choline dehydrogenase has been difficult to obtain, so some catalytic detail is inferred from related enzymes and from tissue activity rather than from a complete human kinetic characterisation. The reviews do not show that raising choline intake increases BHMT flux in people.'

### Overview

Betaine–homocysteine methyltransferase transfers a methyl group from betaine to homocysteine, forming methionine and dimethylglycine. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) A bound zinc ion activates homocysteine. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) Folate and vitamin B12 are not part of that reaction. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Betaine may come from the diet or from oxidation of choline. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[4]](#pm-ref-4) In healthy men, controlled choline depletion lowered plasma betaine and reduced homocysteine-handling reserve, and repletion reversed that change. [[6]](#pm-ref-6)

- **Benefits:** This route replenishes methionine, and with it the capacity to handle a homocysteine load, without using folate or vitamin B12. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) In the men who became clinically depleted, that reserve fell with the betaine pool and returned after repletion. [[6]](#pm-ref-6)
- **Implementation Notes:** Betaine and choline are the provision routes. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[6]](#pm-ref-6) The bound zinc ion is part of the enzyme, not a dietary zinc target. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) Choline depletion lowered the betaine pool; that study does not set a dose for people whose supply is already adequate. [[6]](#pm-ref-6)
- **Biological Relevance:** [Folate- and vitamin B12-dependent remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation) is a neighbouring reaction, not this methyl transfer. [[3]](#pm-ref-3)

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Homocysteine (an amino acid left after methyl groups are used) can be turned back into methionine by a betaine-dependent reaction that does not use folate or vitamin B12. The methionine can later be used to make SAMe, but this reaction stops at methionine. A fall in plasma homocysteine after betaine is taken is a blood result, not a measurement of how fast the enzyme is working in tissue.

#### (The methyl transfer)

BHMT moves one methyl group from betaine (trimethylglycine) onto homocysteine. The products are methionine and dimethylglycine (what remains of betaine after that methyl group leaves) [Breksa and Garrow (1999) [[2]](#pm-ref-2); Evans et al. (2002) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. A zinc ion held by three cysteines in the active site activates the homocysteine thiol. Changing any of those cysteines to alanine abolishes activity in the recombinant human enzyme [[2]](#pm-ref-2). Human structures capture both a zinc-free oxidised form and a zinc-bound form [[1]](#pm-ref-1).

#### (What the reaction does not use)

Folate and vitamin B12 are required by the parallel methionine-synthase route. They are not substrates or cofactors of this transfer. Methyl flow through BHMT does not depend on them [Obeid (2013) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)]. The enzyme is especially active in liver and kidney rather than throughout the brain [[2]](#pm-ref-2), [[3]](#pm-ref-3).

#### (Choline is a supply route)

Choline can be oxidised to betaine aldehyde by choline dehydrogenase, then to betaine by betaine-aldehyde dehydrogenase. That oxidation is irreversible, so it can feed betaine into BHMT without being the BHMT reaction itself [Obeid (2013) [[3]](#pm-ref-3); Salvi and Gadda (2013) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)]. Human choline dehydrogenase sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane and is found mainly in kidney and liver [[4]](#pm-ref-4).

#### (Plasma homocysteine is a shared marker)

In healthy adults, 1.5–6 g/day of supplemental betaine lowered fasting plasma homocysteine over six weeks [Olthof et al. (2003) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)]. Experimental choline depletion raised homocysteine after a methionine load in mice and in a pilot of eight men, without a fall in mouse liver betaine and without an enzyme assay [da Costa et al. (2005) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)]. Vitamin B12 and fish oil also lower plasma homocysteine, so the same blood result is not specific to this enzyme [Huang et al. (2015) [[7]](#pm-ref-7)].

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

This PM is the betaine-dependent methyl transfer only.

Folate/B12-dependent remethylation belongs to [BRS2-FM1-PM1](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation). Methionine-to-SAMe conversion belongs to [BRS2-FM1-PM3](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis). Combined cycle throughput belongs to [BRS2-FM1-PM4](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm4-methionine-cycle-flux).

SAMe-dependent phosphatidylethanolamine methylation belongs to [BRS2-FM3-PM7](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm3/brs2-fm3-pm7-phosphatidylcholine-formation). Acetylcholine formation from choline belongs to [BRS1-FM2-PM6](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm2/brs1-fm2-pm6-acetylcholine-synthesis-support).

#### (Integration within BRS2)

Within [BRS2(FM1) — Methylation Cycle Efficiency](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-methylation-cycle-efficiency), this reaction is one way to regenerate methionine when the folate-dependent route is under strain [Obeid (2013) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)]. The methionine can enter SAMe formation afterwards. Whether a shortfall in the wider methyl-donor pool limits the enzyme's activity in people has not been shown, because plasma homocysteine does not measure that activity [[5]](#pm-ref-5), [[6]](#pm-ref-6).

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS1-FM2-PM6 — Acetylcholine Synthesis Support](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm2/brs1-fm2-pm6-acetylcholine-synthesis-support) — Choline that is oxidised becomes betaine for this methyl transfer, while choline that is not oxidised is the substrate used to make acetylcholine. Betaine is not converted back to choline, so this shared use of choline does not show that dietary betaine raises brain acetylcholine.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS2-FM1-PM1 — Folate/B12-Dependent Homocysteine Remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation) — This sibling regenerates methionine from homocysteine by methionine synthase, using a folate-derived methyl group and vitamin B12. BHMT uses betaine instead, so the routes are parallel and folate is not a cofactor of this reaction.
- [BRS2-FM1-PM3 — SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis) — Methionine produced here can be joined to ATP by methionine adenosyltransferase to form the later methyl donor. That conversion is a separate enzyme step and is not catalysed by BHMT.
- [BRS2-FM1-PM4 — Methionine Cycle Flux](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm4-methionine-cycle-flux) — The wider methionine cycle combines remethylation, methyl-group use and regeneration into one throughput. This PM is only the betaine-dependent remethylation step, not that combined throughput.
