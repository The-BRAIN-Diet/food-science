## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Maintain coordinated one-carbon cycling that supports homocysteine remethylation, SAMe production, and methyl-group transfer.

### Overview

Coordinates folate/B12- and betaine-dependent homocysteine remethylation with methionine-cycle flux and SAMe production. This integrated state maintains methyl-donor availability for phospholipid, neurotransmitter, and other methylation-dependent chemistry across the brain and body.

* Maintains methyl-donor context for neurotransmitter-related chemistry — Supporting BRS1.
* Connects homocysteine handling with transsulfuration and redox pathways — within BRS2 and Supporting BRS3.
* Supplies SAMe-dependent methyl-group transfer used across cellular metabolism — Supporting BRS4.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

Methionine regeneration; SAMe production; homocysteine handling; methyl-group transfer.

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

<span id="42-integrated-functional-narrative" />

### 4.1 Functional Rationale

Remethylation, SAMe formation and regulated cycle routing maintain methyl-transfer capacity while handling homocysteine. Their coordination provides a biological basis for methylation-dependent functions without defining a universal optimum for metabolite concentrations.

<span id="44-evidence-highlights" />

### 4.2 Evidence Summary

Human enzyme studies establish distinct folate/B12, betaine and MAT reaction requirements. Choline-depletion challenge evidence supports a bounded upstream dependency. Human tracer measurements found unchanged mean cycle rates during moderate vitamin B6 restriction despite altered PLP and cystathionine concentrations. [[10](#fm-ref-10), [11](#fm-ref-11), [12](#fm-ref-12), [13](#fm-ref-13), [14](#fm-ref-14)]

Together, functioning provision and routing capacities would support methionine regeneration and SAMe-dependent methyl transfer. This is a synthesis of complementary pathway evidence, not proof that lowering blood homocysteine increases tissue methylation or improves cognition.

### 4.3 Suboptimal Function & Its Effects

Effects depend on the affected route and nutritional context. In the choline-depletion study, the four clinically depleted men had reduced homocysteine-handling reserve after a methionine challenge; this does not establish the same impairment in adequately nourished people or measure human BHMT flux. [[13](#fm-ref-13)]

The [accepted child-PM connections](#key-constraints) distinguish this conditional PM2 constraint from supported supply in PM1, PM3 and PM4. Whether donor availability limits tissue MTR capacity, MAT-dependent synthesis or integrated cycle rates remains unresolved. A change in homocysteine or SAMe concentration alone cannot establish a cascade of impaired synthesis, routing and methylation across all four PMs. [[12](#fm-ref-12), [14](#fm-ref-14)]


## 5. Connected Mechanisms

- [BRS1-FM2-PM6 — Acetylcholine Synthesis Support](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm2/brs1-fm2-pm6-acetylcholine-synthesis-support) — Shares the choline resource with methyl-donor provision. Animal folate/methionine-cycle impairment studies report altered brain choline and acetylcholine; they do not establish donor-intake control of acetylcholine synthesis in adequately nourished humans. [[15](#fm-ref-15), [16](#fm-ref-16), [17](#fm-ref-17)]
- [BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation) — Choline supports phosphatidylcholine production through the Kennedy pathway, connecting nutritional provision with DHA-carrier biology. Separately, choline can contribute methyl groups through betaine and SAMe to the PEMT route. Whether inadequate choline or methyl-donor supply limits adult brain DHA incorporation remains unresolved and context-dependent; these supported pathways do not establish benefit from additional intake. [[18](#fm-ref-18), [20](#fm-ref-20), [21](#fm-ref-21), [22](#fm-ref-22)]
- [BRS2-FM2-PM5 — Transsulfuration Pathway](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm5-transsulfuration-pathway) — Uses homocysteine at the cycle's transsulfuration exit: CBS joins it with serine to form cystathionine. This shares an intermediate with remethylation, without making homocysteine concentration a measure of either branch's flux. [[19](#fm-ref-19)]
- [BRS2-FM3-PM7 — Phosphatidylcholine Formation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm3/brs2-fm3-pm7-phosphatidylcholine-formation) — Uses SAMe as the methyl donor for PEMT-mediated conversion of phosphatidylethanolamine to phosphatidylcholine. Supply of the donor does not establish increased PEMT activity or phosphatidylcholine output. [[20](#fm-ref-20)]
- [BRS2(FM3) — Methylation–Membrane Coupling](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm3/brs2-fm3-methylation-membrane-coupling) — Connects this FM's SAMe-producing metabolism with the SAMe-dependent phospholipid chemistry assessed in its PEMT child. This is shared reaction-resource context, not a demonstrated improvement in brain delivery or cognition. [[20](#fm-ref-20)]

## 7. Phenome Connections

These outcomes describe translational contexts for the FM as an integrated biological capacity. They are not single-mechanism treatment claims. FM confidence uplift: FM confidence may exceed that of any individual child PM only where multiple PMs converge on the same phenome and the integrated FM biology provides additional biological rationale (biological uplift) beyond the individual mechanisms.
