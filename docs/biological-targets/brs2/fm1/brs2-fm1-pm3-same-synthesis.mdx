## BRS2-FM1-PM3 - SAMe Synthesis

(Producing the Cell's Methyl-Donor Pool)

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Maintain methionine adenosyltransferase capacity to convert methionine and ATP into SAMe, sustaining the methyl-donor pool used by downstream methyltransferases.

### Overview

Governs S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe) formation from methionine and ATP through methionine adenosyltransferase (MAT). This PM supplies the methyl-donor pool but does not govern upstream methionine regeneration, whole-cycle flux, or downstream methyltransferase reactions. Circulating SAMe and the SAM:SAH ratio reflect pool and utilisation balance rather than synthesis flux alone. Ordinary dietary variation has not been shown to increase MAT-dependent SAMe production in adequately nourished humans.

* Converts methionine and ATP into SAMe through methionine adenosyltransferase.
* Supplies a methyl-donor pool used by downstream reactions without governing those reactions.
* Distinguishes biochemical requirements from demonstrated dietary control of synthesis.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

↑ MAT-dependent SAMe formation<br />
↑ availability of the SAMe methyl-donor pool<br />
→ permissive support for downstream methyl-transfer reactions

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

SAMe production is the point at which methionine and intracellular ATP become a usable methyl donor. The defining event is MAT catalysis; upstream regeneration of methionine and downstream consumption of SAMe determine context but are not part of the synthesis reaction itself.

#### (Methionine adenosyltransferase reaction)

MAT catalyses an ATP-dependent reaction in which methionine's sulfur attacks ATP's adenosyl carbon to form SAMe; phosphate products are subsequently released. Human MAT2A kinetics support ordered ATP-first, methionine-second binding [[1](#pm-ref-1)], while human MATα2 structures capture the catalytic states and active-site ion coordination [[2](#pm-ref-2)].

#### (Direct requirements and capacity)

Methionine and ATP are reaction substrates. Magnesium and potassium ions participate in active-site phosphate coordination under biochemical and structural conditions [[1](#pm-ref-1), [2](#pm-ref-2)]. These are biochemical requirements, not evidence that ordinary dietary magnesium, potassium or methionine intake is rate-limiting. MAT isoform abundance, enzyme stability, substrate availability and feedback all contribute to cellular capacity; the reviewed evidence does not establish a simple human basal-versus-reserve dietary threshold.

#### (Pool state is not synthesis flux)

SAM concentration reflects formation and consumption. The SAM:SAH ratio additionally incorporates SAH production and clearance and is commonly used as a methylation-potential index, not a direct measure of MAT flux. Accurate interpretation also depends on matrix and sample handling because SAM is unstable [[3](#pm-ref-3)].

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

This PM governs methionine → SAMe formation through MAT. Folate/B12-dependent remethylation belongs to [BRS2-FM1-PM1](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation); betaine/BHMT remethylation to [BRS2-FM1-PM2](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation); integrated methionine-cycle throughput to [BRS2-FM1-PM4](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm4-methionine-cycle-flux); and transsulfuration to [BRS2-FM2-PM5](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm5-transsulfuration-pathway). PEMT, DNA, neurotransmitter and other methyl-transfer reactions are downstream consumers, not SAMe synthesis.

#### (Integration within BRS2)

Within [BRS2(FM1) — Methylation Cycle Efficiency](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-methylation-cycle-efficiency), PM1 and PM2 regenerate methionine, PM3 converts it to SAMe, and PM4 represents integrated cycle flux. [BRS2(KC2) — Methionine & Transsulfuration Substrate Pool](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/kc/brs2-kc2-methionine-transsulfuration-substrate-pool) supplies substrate context without proving dietary control of PM3.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Introduction/Summary

SAMe synthesis is the MAT-catalysed conversion of methionine and ATP into SAMe. The reaction has direct biochemical requirements, but those requirements do not by themselves establish dietary control of human synthesis flux. Upstream remethylation and downstream methyl-transfer reactions remain outside this PM.
