summary: 'Cystathionine beta-synthase joins homocysteine and serine to form cystathionine. Cystathionine gamma-lyase then cleaves cystathionine and releases cysteine. Both enzymes require pyridoxal 5′-phosphate, the active cofactor made from vitamin B6. This irreversible branch disposes of homocysteine and produces cysteine. Studies of the purified enzymes establish these reactions and the cofactor requirement.'

scientific_findings_intro: 'Purified and recombinant human enzyme studies establish the two transsulfuration reaction boundaries and their PLP dependence: CBS forms cystathionine, and CGL uses it to release cysteine. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) [[4]](#pm-ref-4) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) The findings converge on biochemical pathway identity, including cysteine’s position as a product of the second step. They do not measure whole-body dietary responsiveness or glutathione synthesis, so neither increased flux from extra vitamin B6 nor downstream benefit is established by these assays.     finding_label: CBS joins homocysteine and serine
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: 'Human cystathionine beta-synthase uses pyridoxal 5''-phosphate to join L-homocysteine and L-serine, forming L-cystathionine. Pyridoxal 5''-phosphate is the active form of vitamin B6 recycled from food.'
    finding_interpretation: Homocysteine and serine are both substrates. The cofactor requirement does not show that eating more vitamin B6 increases cystathionine formation in people.
    finding_statement: 'Human cystathionine beta-synthase catalyses a pyridoxal 5''-phosphate-dependent condensation of L-serine and L-homocysteine to L-cystathionine, and pyridoxal 5''-phosphate is the active vitamin B6 cofactor recycled from food.'
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: 'Purified human enzyme kinetics and a crystal structure agree that cystathionine beta-synthase condenses L-serine and L-homocysteine to L-cystathionine and that the reaction depends on pyridoxal 5''-phosphate. A cofactor review identifies that same molecule as the active form of vitamin B6, recycled from food by the salvage pathway. Together these sources define the first reaction and its cofactor. They do not measure whole-body transsulfuration flux or an intake response.'
    synthesis_limitations: 'The kinetic and structural evidence uses recombinant or crystallised human enzyme, not intact human physiology. The salvage review does not assay cystathionine beta-synthase. Substrate inhibition constants and cofactor identity do not show that ordinary vitamin B6 intake sets the reaction rate.'

    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: 'Kinetic work on recombinant human cystathionine gamma-lyase shows gamma-lyase activity toward L-cystathionine and only marginal reactivity toward L-cysteine. A separate human-enzyme study identifies the enzyme as pyridoxal 5''-phosphate-dependent and places it in the transsulfuration route that converts homocysteine to cysteine, with cystathionine as the assayed substrate. Cysteine is therefore the pathway product of this step, not its substrate. Neither study measures glutathione synthesis.'
    synthesis_limitations: 'Both studies use recombinant human enzyme. The 2008 paper''s numeric comparisons concern disease-associated mutants and a common polymorphism, not dietary intake. The abstracts establish cystathionine cleavage and the homocysteine-to-cysteine role; they were not used as a product-table for every coproduct. Glutathione synthesis remains outside this finding.'

None of these sources reports a human tracer measurement of transsulfuration flux before and after a change in vitamin B6 intake. A cofactor requirement and a salvage route therefore cannot be read as proof that intake controls flux.
    synthesis_limitations: This is a boundary on the evidence in hand. It does not claim that a future intake study would be negative. The repository record previously used for a population association does not resolve to that study and is not part of this synthesis.

### Overview

Cystathionine beta-synthase joins homocysteine and serine to form cystathionine. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) Cystathionine gamma-lyase then cleaves cystathionine and releases cysteine. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[4]](#pm-ref-4) Both enzymes require pyridoxal 5′-phosphate, the active cofactor made from vitamin B6. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[5]](#pm-ref-5) This irreversible branch disposes of homocysteine and produces cysteine. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Studies of the purified enzymes establish these reactions and the cofactor requirement. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)

- **Benefits:** Transsulfuration directs sulfur from homocysteine toward cysteine. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Cells can later use that cysteine to make glutathione, which links this branch to antioxidant defence.
- **Implementation Notes:** Vitamin B6 is the dietary source of pyridoxal 5′-phosphate. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[5]](#pm-ref-5) Homocysteine, serine, and cystathionine are substrates already in the pathway, not dietary targets. These enzyme studies do not show that eating more vitamin B6 speeds this conversion in people. [[5]](#pm-ref-5)
- **Biological Relevance:** [Glutathione synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm6-glutathione-synthesis) is a later, separate use of the cysteine made here. [[3]](#pm-ref-3)

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Homocysteine can leave the methyl cycle when it is joined to serine and the product is cleaved to cysteine. Those two enzyme steps set the chemistry of this conversion. They do not, by themselves, show how a change in vitamin B6 intake changes the rate in people.

#### (Homocysteine and serine become cystathionine)

Cystathionine beta-synthase condenses L-homocysteine and L-serine to L-cystathionine. The reaction requires pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (PLP), the active cofactor form of vitamin B6 [Belew et al. (2009) [[1]](#pm-ref-1); Meier et al. (2001) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)]. Both amino acids are substrates. Homocysteine is the sulfur amino acid coming from the methionine cycle. Serine is the cosubstrate that CBS condenses with it.

#### (Cystathionine cleavage releases cysteine)

Cystathionine gamma-lyase (CGL; also called CSE) is PLP-dependent and cleaves L-cystathionine in the step that produces cysteine [Zhu et al. (2008) [[3]](#pm-ref-3); Steegborn et al. (1999) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)]. Recombinant enzyme work shows strong gamma-lyase activity toward L-cystathionine and only marginal activity toward L-cysteine [[4]](#pm-ref-4). Cysteine is the product. It is not a substrate of this conversion.

#### (Vitamin B6 supplies the cofactor)

Food vitamin B6 is recycled to PLP by pyridoxal kinase, pyridoxine 5'-phosphate oxidase, and phosphatases [di Salvo et al. (2012) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)]. That salvage route explains why vitamin B6 matters to both enzymes. It does not measure how fast CBS or cystathionine gamma-lyase runs, and it does not show that a larger vitamin B6 intake increases cysteine formation.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

This conversion is the irreversible route from homocysteine and serine, through cystathionine, to cysteine.

Remethylation of homocysteine belongs to [BRS2-FM1-PM1 — Folate/B12-Dependent Homocysteine Remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation) and [BRS2-FM1-PM2 — Betaine/BHMT Remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation). SAMe formation and whole-cycle throughput belong to [BRS2-FM1-PM3 — SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis) and [BRS2-FM1-PM4 — Methionine Cycle Flux](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm4-methionine-cycle-flux).

Glutathione synthesis from cysteine belongs to [BRS2-FM2-PM6 — Glutathione Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm6-glutathione-synthesis). Later antioxidant use of glutathione belongs with [BRS3 — Inflammation & Oxidative Stress](/docs/biological-targets/inflammation-oxidative-stress).

#### (Integration within BRS2)

Within [BRS2(FM2) — Transsulfuration & Redox Coupling](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-transsulfuration-redox-coupling), this conversion releases the cysteine that glutathione synthesis can use. Homocysteine that is not remethylated is the sulfur substrate of the first step. The split between remethylation and this branch is not measured on this page.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

Purified and recombinant human enzyme studies establish the two transsulfuration reaction boundaries and their PLP dependence: CBS forms cystathionine, and CGL uses it to release cysteine. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) [[4]](#pm-ref-4) [[3]](#pm-ref-3) The findings converge on biochemical pathway identity, including cysteine’s position as a product of the second step. They do not measure whole-body dietary responsiveness or glutathione synthesis, so neither increased flux from extra vitamin B6 nor downstream benefit is established by these assays. Human tracer evidence adds a physiological boundary: moderate vitamin B6 restriction changed cystathionine handling without significantly changing total transsulfuration. [[7]](#pm-ref-7)
