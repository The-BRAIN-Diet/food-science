## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Coordinate homocysteine routing into cysteine production with downstream glutathione synthesis and cellular redox defence.

### Overview

Coordinates transsulfuration with glutathione synthesis, linking homocysteine disposal to cysteine availability and cellular antioxidant capacity. This coupling connects one-carbon metabolism with redox defence without implying that pathway activity alone determines resilience or recovery.

* Routes homocysteine toward cysteine as an alternative to remethylation — within BRS2.
* Supplies substrate for glutathione-dependent antioxidant defence — Supporting BRS3.
* Contributes to the redox environment surrounding mitochondrial function — Supporting BRS4.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

↑ cysteine generation; ↑ glutathione synthesis; ↓ oxidative load

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

<span id="42-integrated-functional-narrative" />

### 4.1 Functional Rationale

Cysteine generation and glutathione assembly connect sulfur-amino-acid metabolism with provision for cellular redox defence. Glutathione use and recycling are downstream capacities beyond these two PMs.

<span id="44-evidence-highlights" />

### 4.2 Evidence Summary

Human enzyme studies establish PLP-dependent cysteine production and ATP-dependent glutathione assembly. Blood-cell tracer studies show reduced synthesis during combined methionine/cysteine withdrawal, no reduction during cysteine withdrawal with adequate methionine, and increased synthesis after combined precursors in older adults. [[3](#fm-ref-3), [5](#fm-ref-5), [6](#fm-ref-6), [7](#fm-ref-7), [8](#fm-ref-8), [9](#fm-ref-9)]

Adequate sulfur-amino-acid supply and assembly capacity would therefore support glutathione provision under the studied conditions. They do not establish universal dietary cysteine limitation, neuronal synthesis, or successful peroxide clearance simply from producing more glutathione.

### 4.3 Suboptimal Function & Its Effects

Transsulfuration and redox coupling may weaken when methionine and transsulfuration substrate pools become chronically inadequate.

Low protein quality or insufficient sulfur-amino-acid intake may reduce [BRS2(KC2) — Methionine & Transsulfuration Substrate Pool](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/kc/brs2-kc2-methionine-transsulfuration-substrate-pool). Chronic methionine substrate insufficiency, increased glutathione demand, oxidative burden driving sulfur-amino-acid utilisation, and restrictive dietary patterns reducing substrate diversity may further limit cysteine supply for glutathione synthesis.

These pressures may impair [BRS2-FM2-PM5 — Transsulfuration Pathway](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm5-transsulfuration-pathway) and weaken [BRS2-FM2-PM6 — Glutathione Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm6-glutathione-synthesis). At the FM level, this may shift BRS2(FM2) toward reduced transsulfuration–redox coupling capacity—with downstream relevance to antioxidant defence in BRS3 [Minich et al., 2019].
