summary: >-
  Cells assemble glutathione, the main intracellular thiol, in two ATP-dependent
  steps. Glutamate–cysteine ligase joins glutamate and cysteine, then
  glutathione synthetase adds glycine. A sulfur-amino-acid-free diet slowed
  whole-blood synthesis in healthy young men. Combined precursor supplements
  raised erythrocyte synthesis in older adults. These tracer studies measured
  synthesis in blood cells, not in the brain.

scientific_findings_intro: >-
  Human tracer studies show context-dependent substrate responsiveness. Removing
  methionine and cysteine together reduced whole-blood glutathione synthesis,
  whereas removing cysteine with methionine kept adequate did not reduce
  erythrocyte synthesis in young men. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[5]](#pm-ref-5) In older
  adults with lower baseline synthesis, combined N-acetylcysteine and glycine
  supplementation raised erythrocyte synthesis. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) These contrasts
  support provision under defined conditions, not universal dietary cysteine
  limitation; blood-cell measurements do not establish neuronal synthesis or
  clinical benefit.
scientific_findings:
  - id: PM6-F1
    finding_label: Two ATP-dependent enzymes assemble glutathione
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: >-
      Glutamate-cysteine ligase joins glutamate and cysteine using ATP.
      Glutathione synthetase then adds glycine, again using ATP, and binds
      magnesium. Selenium-dependent peroxidase activity and riboflavin-derived
      FAD in glutathione reductase are not part of these assembly reactions.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      Glutamate, cysteine, glycine and ATP are the assembly substrates.
      Magnesium participates in the second enzyme. ATP and magnesium are
      intracellular biochemical requirements, not dietary levers. Selenium and
      riboflavin support later use and recycling of glutathione.
    finding_statement: >-
      Glutathione synthesis requires ATP-dependent formation of
      γ-glutamylcysteine from glutamate and cysteine, followed by ATP-dependent
      addition of glycine, and does not use selenium or riboflavin as synthesis
      substrates.
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: >-
      Reviewed catalytic annotation of purified human glutamate-cysteine ligase
      records ATP-dependent ligation of L-glutamate and L-cysteine as the first
      step. Human glutathione synthetase structural work records ATP-dependent
      addition of glycine and one bound magnesium ion per subunit as the second
      step. Together they define the assembly boundary. Human glutathione
      peroxidase uses an active-site selenocysteine to reduce peroxides with
      glutathione already formed, and glutathione reductase binds FAD while
      reducing glutathione disulfide. Those enzymes consume or restore
      glutathione; they do not assemble it.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      The enzyme evidence is purified protein and structure, not intact human
      flux. A lower cysteine Km on the first enzyme does not prove that ordinary
      diets are cysteine-limited. Magnesium binding does not show that magnesium
      intake changes synthesis. The dietary review that groups selenium and
      riboflavin with glutathione support does not move those cofactors into the
      assembly reactions.

### Overview

Cells assemble glutathione, the main intracellular thiol, in two ATP-dependent steps. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) Glutamate–cysteine ligase joins glutamate and cysteine, then glutathione synthetase adds glycine. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[2]](#pm-ref-2) A sulfur-amino-acid-free diet slowed whole-blood synthesis in healthy young men. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) Combined precursor supplements raised erythrocyte synthesis in older adults. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) These tracer studies measured synthesis in blood cells, not in the brain. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)

- **Benefits:** Cells draw on this thiol to limit oxidative stress. In older adults, higher blood-cell synthesis after precursor supplements came with lower plasma oxidative-stress markers. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) That thiol is how sulfur amino acids from one-carbon metabolism stay connected to cellular antioxidant defence.
- **Implementation Notes:** Cysteine, methionine, glutamate, glycine, and N-acetylcysteine are the provision routes. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[4]](#pm-ref-4) A methionine-adequate cysteine-free diet did not lower erythrocyte synthesis in young men, so these results do not set a routine dose. [[5]](#pm-ref-5)
- **Biological Relevance:** Clearing peroxides and recycling oxidised glutathione are separate from this assembly. [[8]](#pm-ref-8) [[9]](#pm-ref-9)

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Glutathione formation is the point at which glutamate, cysteine and glycine become the cell's main low-molecular-weight thiol. The defining events are the two ATP-dependent assembly steps. Making the cysteine, and later using or recycling the glutathione, are separate processes.

#### (Two ATP-dependent assembly steps)

Glutathione (GSH; γ-glutamylcysteinylglycine) is built in two steps that each use ATP (the cell's energy-carrying molecule). Glutamate–cysteine ligase joins the side-chain carboxyl of glutamate to cysteine, forming γ-glutamylcysteine (a two-amino-acid intermediate) [[1]](#pm-ref-1). Glutathione synthetase then adds glycine, and the human enzyme binds one magnesium ion per subunit [[2]](#pm-ref-2). The reviewed constants for the purified first enzyme give cysteine a lower Km (0.1 mM) than glutamate (1.8 mM) [[1]](#pm-ref-1). That biochemical preference is not the same thing as a universal dietary cysteine shortage.

#### (When human blood-cell synthesis changes)

In seven healthy young men, removing methionine and cysteine from an otherwise adequate amino-acid formula for 10 days lowered whole-blood fractional synthesis from 0.65 ± 0.13 to 0.49 ± 0.13 per day and lowered absolute synthesis by about 23%, while glutathione concentration stayed near 1.2 mM. Glycine in that formula was increased, not reduced [[3]](#pm-ref-3). In older adults, erythrocyte synthesis was lower than in younger adults and rose after two weeks of a cysteine precursor, supplied as N-acetylcysteine, together with glycine [[4]](#pm-ref-4). When methionine was held at 14 mg/kg/day, cysteine intakes from zero to 40 mg/kg/day did not change erythrocyte synthesis in four young men [[5]](#pm-ref-5).

#### (What is outside the assembly reactions)

Selenium is part of glutathione peroxidase: human glutathione peroxidase uses an active-site selenocysteine and glutathione to reduce peroxides [[8]](#pm-ref-8). Riboflavin is relevant because glutathione reductase binds FAD, a riboflavin-derived cofactor, while it reduces oxidised glutathione back to the reduced form [[9]](#pm-ref-9). A dietary review groups these nutrients with glutathione support [[6]](#pm-ref-6). They act after glutathione has been made.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

This reaction is glutathione assembly from glutamate, cysteine and glycine. Conversion of homocysteine to cysteine belongs to [BRS2-FM2-PM5 — Transsulfuration Pathway](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-pm5-transsulfuration-pathway). Remethylation of homocysteine belongs to [BRS2-FM1-PM1](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation) and [BRS2-FM1-PM2](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation). Using glutathione to limit lipid peroxidation belongs to [BRS3-FM2-PM5 — Lipid Peroxidation Control](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm2/brs3-fm2-pm5-lipid-peroxidation-control).

#### (Integration within BRS2)

Within [BRS2(FM2) — Transsulfuration & Redox Coupling](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm2/brs2-fm2-transsulfuration-redox-coupling), transsulfuration can supply cysteine and this reaction consumes it. Inadequacy of the shared methionine–cysteine pool can slow synthesis: whole-blood synthesis fell when both amino acids were removed [[3]](#pm-ref-3). That does not make selenium, riboflavin, or a clinical treatment part of the assembly step.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

Human tracer studies show context-dependent substrate responsiveness. Removing methionine and cysteine together reduced whole-blood glutathione synthesis, whereas removing cysteine with methionine kept adequate did not reduce erythrocyte synthesis in young men. [[3]](#pm-ref-3) [[5]](#pm-ref-5) In older adults with lower baseline synthesis, combined N-acetylcysteine and glycine supplementation raised erythrocyte synthesis. [[4]](#pm-ref-4) These contrasts support provision under defined conditions, not universal dietary cysteine limitation; blood-cell measurements do not establish neuronal synthesis or clinical benefit.
