summary: 'In the liver, phosphatidylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (PEMT) converts phosphatidylethanolamine, a membrane phospholipid, into phosphatidylcholine by three methyl transfers from S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe, the methyl-group donor). SAMe and phosphatidylethanolamine are the substrates, and the product S-adenosylhomocysteine can regulate activity. The CDP-choline route uses choline and does not perform these methylations. This hepatic pool is formed before blood–brain DHA transport and neuronal membrane uptake, which this reaction does not carry out.'

scientific_findings:
  - id: PM7-F1
    finding_label: PEMT methylates phosphatidylethanolamine to phosphatidylcholine
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: 'In the liver, phosphatidylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (PEMT) adds three methyl groups from S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe) to phosphatidylethanolamine and releases phosphatidylcholine. Magnesium is not a cofactor of this reaction. The CDP-choline route is a separate way to make phosphatidylcholine.'
    finding_interpretation: 'Phosphatidylethanolamine and SAMe are the substrates of the methylation. Choline feeds the parallel route, and animals that lack PEMT need dietary choline. That does not make choline a substrate of this methylation, and it does not make supplemental SAMe or magnesium a requirement of it.'
    finding_statement: 'Hepatic PEMT converts phosphatidylethanolamine to phosphatidylcholine by three sequential SAMe-dependent methyl transfers. The reaction substrates are phosphatidylethanolamine and SAMe. Magnesium is not an established cofactor of this reaction, and the CDP-choline pathway is a parallel route outside it.'
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: 'Human liver enzyme localisation, SAMe-binding mutagenesis and a physiological review agree on the reaction boundary. PEMT in the endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria-associated membranes of liver uses SAMe to methylate phosphatidylethanolamine in three steps, through phosphatidylmonomethylethanolamine and phosphatidyldimethylethanolamine, to phosphatidylcholine. Conserved residues are required for SAMe binding and activity. Mice that lack PEMT tolerate a normal diet, but a choline-deficient diet causes liver failure within days because the CDP-choline route is then unavailable as well. None of these sources names magnesium as a PEMT cofactor.'
    synthesis_limitations: 'The enzyme evidence is recombinant protein and a review of knockout mice, not a human dietary flux study. Absence of magnesium from the reaction description rejects the claim that magnesium is a PEMT cofactor; it does not test every buffer condition in the older assay literature. The choline-deficient knockout result shows that choline supports a parallel route. It does not show that choline is a PEMT substrate or that choline intake increases this methylation.'

### Overview

In the liver, phosphatidylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (PEMT) converts phosphatidylethanolamine, a membrane phospholipid, into phosphatidylcholine by three methyl transfers from S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe, the methyl-group donor) [Vance (2014) [[1]](#pm-ref-1); Shields et al. (2003) [[7]](#pm-ref-7)]. SAMe and phosphatidylethanolamine are the substrates, and the product S-adenosylhomocysteine can regulate activity [Vance (2014) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. The CDP-choline route uses choline and does not perform these methylations. This hepatic pool is formed before blood–brain DHA transport and neuronal membrane uptake, which this reaction does not carry out.

* **Mechanism boundary:** Includes the three SAMe-dependent methylations of phosphatidylethanolamine. The CDP-choline route, blood–brain DHA transport and neuronal DHA uptake are separate [Vance (2014) [[1]](#pm-ref-1); Shields et al. (2003) [[7]](#pm-ref-7)].
* **Evidence boundary:** Piglet brain DHA accretion, human cerebrospinal-fluid DHA and a B-vitamin cognitive interaction do not measure this methylation rate [Liu et al. (2014) [[3]](#pm-ref-3); Arellanes et al. (2020) [[2]](#pm-ref-2); Oulhaj et al. (2016) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)].
* **Biological relevance:** The liver uses this reaction when the choline-based route is unavailable [Vance (2014) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. That role does not show that choline, magnesium, B vitamins or omega-3 fats improve attention or cognition by speeding the methylation [Oulhaj et al. (2016) [[4]](#pm-ref-4); Millichap and Yee (2012) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)].

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Liver cells can build phosphatidylcholine in more than one way. The route that matters here is methylation of an existing membrane phospholipid. That chemistry uses a methyl donor and stops before DHA is carried into the brain.

<div class="brs-fm-hub-shell">
<button type="button" class="brs-fm-hub-summary" aria-expanded="false">

#### (Three methyl transfers)

Phosphatidylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (PEMT) in human liver sits mainly in the endoplasmic reticulum and in the membranes where mitochondria meet that reticulum. It methylates phosphatidylethanolamine (a phospholipid already in the membrane) three times. The methyl groups come from S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe, the methyl-group donor). The intermediates are phosphatidylmonomethylethanolamine and phosphatidyldimethylethanolamine. The phospholipid product is phosphatidylcholine, and each transfer releases S-adenosylhomocysteine [Shields et al. (2003) [[7]](#pm-ref-7); Shields et al. (2003) [[8]](#pm-ref-8)].

#### (What the reaction uses)

The substrates are phosphatidylethanolamine and SAMe. Conserved amino acids in the human enzyme are required for SAMe binding and for activity [Shields et al. (2003) [[8]](#pm-ref-8)]. Purified rat-liver enzyme follows an ordered sequence: the phospholipid binds, SAMe binds, the methyl group moves, and S-adenosylhomocysteine leaves [Ridgway and Vance (1988) [[9]](#pm-ref-9)]. The supply of those substrates, and of S-adenosylhomocysteine, can regulate activity [Vance (2014) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. Magnesium is not part of this reaction. It helps the upstream enzyme that makes SAMe. It is not a cofactor shown for PEMT.

SAMe used here is produced by methionine adenosyltransferase in [BRS2-FM1-PM3 — SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis). Needing that methyl donor does not make a SAMe supplement the substrate, and it does not show that more SAMe intake speeds these methylations.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

The CDP-choline pathway, also called the Kennedy pathway, builds phosphatidylcholine from choline by different reactions. Mice that lack PEMT cope on an ordinary diet. The same mice develop liver failure within three days if the diet also lacks choline, because both routes are then missing. The liver phosphatidylcholine to phosphatidylethanolamine ratio falls and hepatocyte membranes leak [Vance (2014) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. Choline therefore supports the parallel route. It is not the molecule PEMT methylates.

Blood–brain transport of DHA and its uptake into neuronal membranes are later steps, described under [BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation). A piglet comparison of phospholipid-bound and triglyceride-bound DHA measured brain accretion, and the liver was labelled most heavily [Liu et al. (2014) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)]. A human trial measured DHA in cerebrospinal fluid after supplements, while both groups also took B vitamins, and cognitive scores did not differ [Arellanes et al. (2020) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)]. Neither study measured how fast PEMT works.

In mild cognitive impairment, B vitamins were linked with slower cognitive decline when omega-3 status was higher [Oulhaj et al. (2016) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)]. That is a clinical interaction. It is not an assay of this methylation. Diet reviews in ADHD and choline reviews in neurodevelopment likewise do not measure the enzyme [Millichap and Yee (2012) [[5]](#pm-ref-5); Derbyshire and Maes (2023) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)].

#### (Integration within BRS2)

Within [BRS2(FM3) — Methylation–Membrane Coupling](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm3/brs2-fm3-methylation-membrane-coupling), this is the methylation that turns phosphatidylethanolamine into phosphatidylcholine. [BRS2-FM1-PM1 — Folate/B12-Dependent Homocysteine Remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation) can regenerate methionine that later becomes SAMe. That is earlier than the methyl transfers. [BRS2-FM1-PM3 — SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis) is the reaction that supplies the SAMe used here. Methyl-donor supply and the methionine pool sit upstream of that SAMe. Their shortage has not been shown to limit this methylation itself, as distinct from limiting SAMe production. Neuronal DHA incorporation remains the downstream step in [BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation).

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Introduction/Summary

Hepatic phosphatidylcholine formation here is three methyl transfers from S-adenosylmethionine onto phosphatidylethanolamine. The findings separate that reaction from the parallel CDP-choline route, from magnesium, and from brain DHA or cognitive measurements that do not record the methylation rate.

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation) — Hepatic methylation produces phosphatidylcholine from phosphatidylethanolamine. Later blood–brain transport of DHA and its uptake into neuronal membranes are a downstream delivery step, not this methyl transfer.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS2-FM1-PM3 - SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis) — Methionine adenosyltransferase produces the S-adenosylmethionine consumed in each phosphatidylethanolamine methylation. That is upstream methyl-donor supply, not the methylation reaction itself.

## 7. Phenome Connections

These mappings are translational relationships, not single-mechanism outcome claims. Phenomes are emergent functional patterns supported by multiple interacting PMs across the BRAIN Framework.
