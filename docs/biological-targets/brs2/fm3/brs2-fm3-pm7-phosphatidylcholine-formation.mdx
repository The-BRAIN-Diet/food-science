summary: >-
  Cells form phosphatidylcholine through distinct routes: hepatic PEMT
  methylates phosphatidylethanolamine using SAMe, while the Kennedy pathway
  combines choline-derived phosphocholine with CTP and then diacylglycerol.
  Uridine can contribute upstream to nucleotide supply. These routes support
  membrane phospholipid provision; precursor availability does not establish
  that extra intake increases human membrane synthesis or improves cognition.

    finding_label: PEMT methylates phosphatidylethanolamine to phosphatidylcholine
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: >-
      In the liver, phosphatidylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (PEMT) adds
      three methyl groups from S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe) to
      phosphatidylethanolamine and releases phosphatidylcholine. Magnesium is
      not a cofactor of this reaction. The CDP-choline route is a separate way
      to make phosphatidylcholine.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      Phosphatidylethanolamine and SAMe are the substrates of the methylation.
      Choline feeds the parallel route, and animals that lack PEMT need dietary
      choline. That does not make choline a substrate of this methylation, and
      it does not make supplemental SAMe or magnesium a requirement of it.
    finding_statement: >-
      Hepatic PEMT converts phosphatidylethanolamine to phosphatidylcholine by
      three sequential SAMe-dependent methyl transfers. The reaction substrates
      are phosphatidylethanolamine and SAMe. Magnesium is not an established
      cofactor of this reaction, and the CDP-choline pathway is a parallel route
      outside it.
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: >-
      Human liver enzyme localisation, SAMe-binding mutagenesis and a
      physiological review agree on the reaction boundary. PEMT in the
      endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria-associated membranes of liver uses
      SAMe to methylate phosphatidylethanolamine in three steps, through
      phosphatidylmonomethylethanolamine and phosphatidyldimethylethanolamine,
      to phosphatidylcholine. Conserved residues are required for SAMe binding
      and activity. Mice that lack PEMT tolerate a normal diet, but a
      choline-deficient diet causes liver failure within days because the
      CDP-choline route is then unavailable as well. None of these sources names
      magnesium as a PEMT cofactor.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      The enzyme evidence is recombinant protein and a review of knockout mice,
      not a human dietary flux study. Absence of magnesium from the reaction
      description rejects the claim that magnesium is a PEMT cofactor; it does
      not test every buffer condition in the older assay literature. The
      choline-deficient knockout result shows that choline supports a parallel
      route. It does not show that choline is a PEMT substrate or that choline
      intake increases this methylation.

## BRS2-FM3-PM7 - Phosphatidylcholine Formation

(PEMT methylation and Kennedy-pathway phosphatidylcholine synthesis)

## 1. Mission, Objective & Biological Context

### Mission

Maintain phosphatidylcholine formation through PEMT methylation and the choline-based Kennedy pathway.



### Overview

Cells form phosphatidylcholine through distinct routes: hepatic PEMT methylates phosphatidylethanolamine using SAMe, while the Kennedy pathway combines choline-derived phosphocholine with CTP and then diacylglycerol. Uridine can contribute upstream to nucleotide supply. These routes support membrane phospholipid provision; precursor availability does not establish that extra intake increases human membrane synthesis or improves cognition. [[19]](#pm-ref-19) [[20]](#pm-ref-20) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)

- **Benefits:** PC formation supplies a major membrane phospholipid. PEMT/choline-route loss in mice demonstrates the importance of maintaining hepatic PC provision; the new uridine evidence measures precursor formation, not a clinical benefit. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[19]](#pm-ref-19)
- **Implementation Notes:** Choline and phosphatidylcholine can supply the existing methyl-donor provision route. Uridine adds a supported nucleotide-precursor route, with no established ordinary-food dose or human PC-flux benefit. [[20]](#pm-ref-20) [[21]](#pm-ref-21)
- **Biological Relevance:** Only the PEMT branch directly couples methylation to membrane chemistry in the parent FM. Kennedy synthesis is a parallel PC-forming route; brain DHA delivery and incorporation remain separate downstream jobs. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[19]](#pm-ref-19)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

Phosphatidylcholine formation through distinct PEMT and Kennedy routes; S-adenosylhomocysteine production confined to PEMT methyl transfer. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[19]](#pm-ref-19)

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

PC formation has two distinct routes. Hepatic PEMT uses SAMe to methylate phosphatidylethanolamine. The Kennedy pathway builds PC using choline, CTP and diacylglycerol; uridine can contribute to its nucleotide supply. Neither route alone establishes brain DHA uptake or cognitive benefit. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[19]](#pm-ref-19)

<div class="brs-fm-hub-item" data-brs-fm-hub>
<div class="brs-fm-hub-shell">
<button type="button" class="brs-fm-hub-summary" aria-expanded="false">
<span class="brs-fm-hub-chevron" aria-hidden="true"></span>
<strong>Phosphatidylcholine formation — mechanistic detail</strong>
</button>
<div class="brs-fm-hub-panel" hidden>

#### (Three methyl transfers)

Phosphatidylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (PEMT) in human liver sits mainly in the endoplasmic reticulum and in the membranes where mitochondria meet that reticulum. It methylates phosphatidylethanolamine (a phospholipid already in the membrane) three times. The methyl groups come from S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe, the methyl-group donor). The intermediates are phosphatidylmonomethylethanolamine and phosphatidyldimethylethanolamine. The phospholipid product is phosphatidylcholine, and each transfer releases S-adenosylhomocysteine [Shields et al. (2003) [[7]](#pm-ref-7); Shields et al. (2003) [[8]](#pm-ref-8)].

#### (What the reaction uses)

The substrates are phosphatidylethanolamine and SAMe. Conserved amino acids in the human enzyme are required for SAMe binding and for activity [Shields et al. (2003) [[8]](#pm-ref-8)]. Purified rat-liver enzyme follows an ordered sequence: the phospholipid binds, SAMe binds, the methyl group moves, and S-adenosylhomocysteine leaves [Ridgway and Vance (1988) [[9]](#pm-ref-9)]. The supply of those substrates, and of S-adenosylhomocysteine, can regulate activity [Vance (2014) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. Magnesium is not part of this reaction. It helps the upstream enzyme that makes SAMe. It is not a cofactor shown for PEMT.

SAMe used here is produced by methionine adenosyltransferase in [BRS2-FM1-PM3 — SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis). Needing that methyl donor does not make a SAMe supplement the substrate, and it does not show that more SAMe intake speeds these methylations.

#### (Kennedy-pathway PC formation)

Choline is phosphorylated to phosphocholine. CTP then supplies the nucleotide used to form CDP-choline; transfer of its phosphocholine group to diacylglycerol forms PC. This route does not consume SAMe methyl groups. Uridine can enter nucleotide metabolism upstream of CTP. [[19]](#pm-ref-19)

In rat brain slices, uridine increased CDP-choline formation without reducing measured acetylcholine release. Oral UMP increased brain uridine, UTP, CTP and CDP-choline in gerbils. Those are provision and precursor endpoints, not measurements of absolute human PC synthesis. [[19]](#pm-ref-19) [[20]](#pm-ref-20) In a four-week gerbil study, dietary UMP also increased measured PC abundance on a choline-containing diet (Table 1A); this was not a synthesis-flux measurement. [[22]](#pm-ref-22) Human uridine administration changed phosphomonoester signals on phosphorus MRS; these signals do not establish membrane accretion or cognition. [[21]](#pm-ref-21)

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

The CDP-choline pathway, also called the Kennedy pathway, builds phosphatidylcholine from choline by different reactions. Mice that lack PEMT cope on an ordinary diet. The same mice develop liver failure within three days if the diet also lacks choline, because both routes are then missing. The liver phosphatidylcholine to phosphatidylethanolamine ratio falls and hepatocyte membranes leak [Vance (2014) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. Choline therefore supports the parallel route. It is not the molecule PEMT methylates.

Blood–brain transport of DHA and its uptake into neuronal membranes are later steps, described under [BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation). A piglet comparison of phospholipid-bound and triglyceride-bound DHA measured brain accretion, and the liver was labelled most heavily [Liu et al. (2014) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)]. A human trial measured DHA in cerebrospinal fluid after supplements, while both groups also took B vitamins, and cognitive scores did not differ [Arellanes et al. (2020) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)]. Neither study measured how fast PEMT works.

In mild cognitive impairment, B vitamins were linked with slower cognitive decline when omega-3 status was higher [Oulhaj et al. (2016) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)]. That is a clinical interaction. It is not an assay of this methylation. Diet reviews in ADHD and choline reviews in neurodevelopment likewise do not measure the enzyme [Millichap and Yee (2012) [[5]](#pm-ref-5); Derbyshire and Maes (2023) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)].

#### (Integration within BRS2)

Within [BRS2(FM3) — Methylation–Membrane Coupling](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm3/brs2-fm3-methylation-membrane-coupling), this is the methylation that turns phosphatidylethanolamine into phosphatidylcholine. [BRS2-FM1-PM1 — Folate/B12-Dependent Homocysteine Remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation) can regenerate methionine that later becomes SAMe. That is earlier than the methyl transfers. [BRS2-FM1-PM3 — SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis) is the reaction that supplies the SAMe used here. Methionine supplies that SAMe upstream. Neonatal piglet tracing shows dietary methionine-dependent methyl incorporation into hepatic PC [[18]](#pm-ref-18). This supports provision; a distinct methionine–cysteine pool constraint remains unresolved (PM7-F6). Neuronal DHA incorporation remains the downstream step in [BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation).

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

Enzyme localisation, mutagenesis and kinetic evidence establishes PEMT-mediated phosphatidylcholine formation and regulation by its substrates and methyl-transfer product. [[7]](#pm-ref-7) [[8]](#pm-ref-8) [[9]](#pm-ref-9) Donor restriction/rescue and upstream perturbation studies support bounded dependence, with experimental contexts limiting extrapolation. [[11]](#pm-ref-11) [[12]](#pm-ref-12) [[15]](#pm-ref-15) Human tracing identifies methyl-derived phosphatidylcholine separately from intact-headgroup incorporation, supporting provision without establishing indispensable dependence, increased absolute hepatic flux or brain benefit. [[10]](#pm-ref-10) Kennedy-pathway studies add a separate nucleotide-provision chain: uridine increased CDP-choline formation in rat slices, and oral UMP increased brain precursor pools in gerbils. [[19]](#pm-ref-19) [[20]](#pm-ref-20) Human MRS provides connected precursor-pool evidence rather than proof of PC synthesis or benefit. [[21]](#pm-ref-21) A longer gerbil study measured PC abundance after dietary UMP, a stronger lipid endpoint than precursor pools, but not a human flux or benefit result. [[22]](#pm-ref-22)

### 5.1 BRS Pathways

[BRS2-FM1-PM1 — Folate/B12-Dependent Homocysteine Remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm1-folate-b12-dependent-homocysteine-remethylation)

↓
[BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation)

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS1-FM3-PM7 — Neuronal Membrane DHA Incorporation](/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation) — Hepatic methylation produces phosphatidylcholine from phosphatidylethanolamine. Later blood–brain transport of DHA and its uptake into neuronal membranes are a downstream delivery step, not this methyl transfer.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS2-FM1-PM2 — Betaine/BHMT Remethylation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm2-betaine-bhmt-remethylation) — Choline-derived betaine contributes methyl groups through homocysteine remethylation to methionine and subsequent SAMe synthesis. This upstream chain supports methyl-group provision to PEMT (PM7-F7).

- [BRS2-FM1-PM3 - SAMe Synthesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm3-same-synthesis) — Methionine adenosyltransferase produces the S-adenosylmethionine consumed in each phosphatidylethanolamine methylation. That is upstream methyl-donor supply, not the methylation reaction itself.

## 7. Phenome Connections

These mappings are translational relationships, not single-mechanism outcome claims. Phenomes are emergent functional patterns supported by multiple interacting PMs across the BRAIN Framework.
