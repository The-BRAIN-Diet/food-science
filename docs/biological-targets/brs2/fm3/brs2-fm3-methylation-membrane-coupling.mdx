## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Maintain the methylation-dependent phosphatidylcholine pathway that links one-carbon metabolism with membrane phospholipid chemistry.

### Overview

Links one-carbon metabolism with membrane phospholipid chemistry through SAMe-dependent phosphatidylcholine formation. This pathway contributes to phospholipid composition and may influence the context in which long-chain omega-3 fatty acids are transported and incorporated, while direct effects on brain membrane function remain context-dependent.

* Provides a methylation-dependent route for phosphatidylcholine formation — within BRS2.
* Contributes phospholipid context relevant to neuronal membrane composition — Supporting BRS1.
* Connects upstream methyl-donor availability with downstream membrane-lipid handling — Supporting BRS1.

The child PC-formation mechanism also describes the parallel Kennedy pathway. This FM covers its PEMT methylation branch; Kennedy nucleotide-provision findings do not establish methylation-dependent effects and are not automatically included in this FM’s evidence or outcome ratings.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

↑ phosphatidylcholine formation; ↑ membrane fluidity; ↑ receptor signaling stability

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

<span id="42-integrated-functional-narrative" />

### 4.1 Functional Rationale

PEMT-mediated phosphatidylcholine formation connects methyl-transfer capacity with hepatic lipid provision. Current coverage supports that formation pathway, rather than all membrane composition or brain-delivery processes.

<span id="44-evidence-highlights" />

### 4.2 Evidence Summary

Enzyme and kinetic studies establish PEMT formation and product regulation. Donor restriction/rescue and upstream perturbations support bounded dependence; human methyl-derived labelling distinguishes methyl provision from intact phosphatidylcholine headgroup incorporation. [[7](#fm-ref-7), [8](#fm-ref-8), [9](#fm-ref-9), [10](#fm-ref-10), [11](#fm-ref-11)]

When the supported provision and enzymatic capacities are maintained, they would sustain this phosphatidylcholine-formation route. The evidence does not establish extra-intake enhancement, absolute human hepatic flux, neuronal membrane delivery or cognitive benefit from that route alone.

### 4.3 Suboptimal Function & Its Effects

Reduced capacity in the hepatic methylation route can reduce its contribution to phosphatidylcholine formation. Parallel synthesis and downstream lipid handling remain distinct processes; impaired methylation alone does not establish a particular brain or cognitive outcome.
