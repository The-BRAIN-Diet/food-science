## BRS2(FM3) - Methylation–Membrane Coupling

(Methylation Support for Brain Membrane Building)

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Maintain the methylation-dependent phosphatidylcholine pathway that links one-carbon metabolism with membrane phospholipid chemistry.

### Overview

Links one-carbon metabolism with membrane phospholipid chemistry through SAMe-dependent phosphatidylcholine formation. This pathway contributes to phospholipid composition and may influence the context in which long-chain omega-3 fatty acids are transported and incorporated, while direct effects on brain membrane function remain context-dependent.

* Provides a methylation-dependent route for phosphatidylcholine formation — within BRS2.
* Contributes phospholipid context relevant to neuronal membrane composition — Supporting BRS1.
* Connects upstream methyl-donor availability with downstream membrane-lipid handling — Supporting BRS1.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

↑ phosphatidylcholine formation; ↑ membrane fluidity; ↑ receptor signaling stability

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

Methylation–membrane coupling represents a framework-relevant biological state anchored principally by its sole primary mechanism.

### 4.1 Core Primary Mechanisms

- [BRS2-FM3-PM7 — Phosphatidylcholine Formation](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm3/brs2-fm3-pm7-phosphatidylcholine-formation) — Converts phosphatidylethanolamine into phosphatidylcholine using three SAMe-dependent methyl transfers.

### 4.2 Integrated Functional Narrative

This single-PM FM places hepatic phosphatidylcholine methylation within the wider connection between one-carbon metabolism and membrane phospholipid chemistry. Upstream SAMe synthesis supplies the methyl donor; the child PM governs its use in phospholipid formation.

One-carbon donor availability can constrain this pathway in demonstrated animal contexts. That conditional relationship does not establish universal nutrient limitation, an intake optimisation target or a cognitive effect.

### 4.3 Suboptimal Function & Its Effects

Reduced capacity in the hepatic methylation route can reduce its contribution to phosphatidylcholine formation. Parallel synthesis and downstream lipid handling remain distinct processes; impaired methylation alone does not establish a particular brain or cognitive outcome.

- **Synthesised Evidence Confidence:** Not yet scored
- **Synthesis:** Human liver enzyme localisation, SAMe-binding mutagenesis and a physiological review agree on the reaction boundary. PEMT in the endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria-associated membranes of liver uses SAMe to methylate phosphatidylethanolamine in three steps, through phosphatidylmonomethylethanolamine and phosphatidyldimethylethanolamine, to phosphatidylcholine. Conserved residues are required for SAMe binding and activity. Mice that lack PEMT tolerate a normal diet, but a choline-deficient diet causes liver failure within days because the CDP-choline route is then unavailable as well. None of these sources names magnesium as a PEMT cofactor.
