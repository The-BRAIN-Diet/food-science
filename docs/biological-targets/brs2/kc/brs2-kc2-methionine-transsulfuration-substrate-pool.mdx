### 3. Evidence Base

#### Summary

Methionine and cysteine form a shared nutritional sulfur-amino-acid resource. Methionine is dietarily indispensable, enters the methionine cycle and supplies sulfur to the irreversible transsulfuration route. Dietary cysteine can spare part of the methionine requirement and contribute directly to downstream sulfur-amino-acid availability. Together they constitute a coherent requirement spanning methionine conservation and transsulfuration-linked substrate capacity, rather than an inventory of every compound used by those pathways ([Ball et al., 2006](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#ball_methionine_cysteine_2006); [Kumar and Yadav, 2017](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#kumar_transsulfuration_2017)).

- **Constraint and membership boundary:** Serine is a transsulfuration cosubstrate, while glycine can co-limit glutathione synthesis under some conditions; neither is established as part of the shared methionine–cysteine nutritional requirement ([Kumar and Yadav, 2017](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#kumar_transsulfuration_2017); [Sekhar et al., 2011](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sekhar_glutathione_2011)).
- **Evidence and measurement boundary:** Human requirement studies establish methionine indispensability and partial cysteine sparing, but the size of that sparing is method-dependent and does not define one threshold for every population ([Ball et al., 2006](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#ball_methionine_cysteine_2006)).
- **Biological relevance and provision limitation:** Adequate sulfur-amino-acid availability prevents substrate constraint; the evidence does not show that higher methionine or cysteine intake improves function in protein-sufficient people or that every connected mechanism is intake-responsive ([Ball et al., 2006](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#ball_methionine_cysteine_2006)).

#### Biological Importance

Methionine is the dietarily indispensable member of the shared sulfur-amino-acid requirement. It enters the methionine cycle and supplies sulfur to the irreversible transsulfuration route, so inadequate availability can constrain both methionine-cycle and downstream sulfur-amino-acid capacity.

#### Biological Importance

Cysteine is nutritionally linked to methionine because it can spare part of the dietary methionine requirement. Its availability also contributes to downstream sulfur-amino-acid capacity, including glutathione synthesis under conditions in which cysteine and glycine availability are limiting.

<h4 class="brs-hub-integration-section-title">Supporting Evidence</h4>
<ul class="brs-hub-integration-evidence-list">
<li class="brs-hub-integration-evidence-item"><p><a href="/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#ball_methionine_cysteine_2006">Ball et al., 2006</a> — Reviewed evidence that cysteine can substitute for part of the adult-human methionine requirement, while documenting methodological uncertainty in the magnitude of sparing.</p></li>
<li class="brs-hub-integration-evidence-item"><p><a href="/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sekhar_glutathione_2011">Sekhar et al., 2011</a> — Showed conditional cysteine-plus-glycine limitation of glutathione synthesis in older adults. This supports cysteine availability at the downstream edge of the pool but does not establish isolated cysteine effects or universal limitation.</p></li>
</ul>
