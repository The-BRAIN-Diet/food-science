## BRS4(FM4) - Mitochondrial Capacity Expansion & Adaptation

(Building More Cellular Energy Capacity)

<span id="41-core-primary-mechanisms" />

### Primary Mechanisms

- [BRS4-FM4-PM9 — Mitochondrial Biogenesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/fm4/brs4-fm4-pm9-mitochondrial-biogenesis)

### Key Constraints

- [BRS4(KC1) — Macronutrient Substrate Availability](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/kc/brs4-kc1-macronutrient-substrate-availability)
- [BRS4(KC2) — Mitochondrial Cofactor Sufficiency](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/kc/brs4-kc2-mitochondrial-cofactor-sufficiency)

## 1. Definition

Supports integrated regulation of exercise-driven mitochondrial biogenesis (formation of new mitochondria) and adaptive density expansion, with diet providing permissive substrate and cofactor support for long-term energetic capacity.

* Drives formation of new mitochondria through PGC-1α and related pathways.
* Links physical activity signals to mitochondrial density expansion over time.
* Depends on macronutrient substrate and cofactor sufficiency for adaptive support.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

↑ mitochondrial density; ↑ long-term energy capacity; ↑ adaptive energetic reserve

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

<span id="42-integrated-functional-narrative" />

### 4.1 Functional Rationale

Adaptive mitochondrial capacity provides a longer-term energetic reserve. Current coverage has one biogenesis-focused PM and must distinguish new capacity from removal of damaged organelles.

<span id="44-evidence-highlights" />

### 4.2 Evidence Summary

The child corpus combines exercise/adaptation framing with direct urolithin A intervention evidence concerning mitochondrial-health and mitophagy-related measures. These address related but different aspects of mitochondrial maintenance; microbial precursor exposure is not equivalent to direct metabolite administration. [[1](#fm-ref-1), [7](#fm-ref-7), [8](#fm-ref-8)]

Appropriate adaptation and quality control would support maintained energetic capacity over time. The assembled evidence does not establish that a mitophagy response alone measures mitochondrial biogenesis or that a single dietary exposure expands brain mitochondrial capacity.

### 4.3 Suboptimal Function & Its Effects

Mitochondrial capacity expansion and adaptation may weaken when macronutrient substrate availability or mitochondrial cofactor sufficiency become inadequate, or when supporting biological pools are chronically strained.

Chronic energy deficit or under-fuelling may reduce [BRS4(KC1) — Macronutrient Substrate Availability](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/kc/brs4-kc1-macronutrient-substrate-availability). Erratic meal patterns reducing substrate continuity may further strain pool availability, ultra-processed food patterns with poor fuel quality, low protein intake where amino-acid support is needed, while metabolic or inflammatory burden increasing energetic demand.

Low micronutrient density across the diet may reduce [BRS4(KC2) — Mitochondrial Cofactor Sufficiency](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/kc/brs4-kc2-mitochondrial-cofactor-sufficiency). Restrictive or low-variety dietary patterns may further strain pool availability, chronic oxidative or inflammatory burden increasing cofactor demand, impaired absorption or depletion states, while high energy intake with poor micronutrient quality.

Insufficient training stimulus, poor recovery, or chronic under-recovery may impair [BRS4-FM4-PM9 — Mitochondrial Biogenesis](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/fm4/brs4-fm4-pm9-mitochondrial-biogenesis). At the FM level, this may shift BRS4(FM4) toward reduced mitochondrial capacity expansion and adaptation performance.
