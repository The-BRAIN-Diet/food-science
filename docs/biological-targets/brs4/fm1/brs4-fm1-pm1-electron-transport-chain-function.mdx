### Overview

The mitochondrial electron transport chain passes electrons from NADH and from succinate through five enzyme complexes [Vercellino and Sazanov (2022) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. Ubiquinone and cytochrome c carry electrons between the complexes, and the proton gradient drives ATP synthase. Iron–sulfur clusters, haem and copper participate in the transfers, and complex I accepts NADH electrons at its flavin cofactor, FMN [Curtabbi and Enríquez (2022) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)]. Defined sites in the chain can also leak electrons to oxygen and form superoxide [Brand (2016) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)].

* **Mechanism boundary:** Includes the five respiratory complexes, ubiquinone, cytochrome c and ATP synthase. NAD synthesis, fuel routing, creatine buffering and peroxide disposal sit outside this chain [Vercellino and Sazanov (2022) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)].
* **Evidence boundary:** Enzyme and structural reviews identify the participants. Cybrid cells from three boys with ADHD respired less, and that study did not test food or attention [Verma et al. (2016) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)].
* **Biological relevance:** The chain supplies ATP. These studies do not show that eating more iron, riboflavin or coenzyme Q10 improves attention or cognitive energy [Brand (2016) [[2]](#pm-ref-2); Verma et al. (2016) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)].

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

↑ ATP synthesis coupled to electron transfer to oxygen<br />
↑ proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane<br />
→ electron leak to superoxide at defined sites, separate from ATP yield

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Five enzyme complexes pass electrons from NADH and succinate to oxygen. Ubiquinone and cytochrome c connect the complexes, and ATP synthase uses the proton gradient [Vercellino and Sazanov (2022) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)]. Complex I starts that transfer at FMN [Curtabbi and Enríquez (2022) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)]. Separate sites can leak electrons to oxygen [Brand (2016) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)].

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

Electron transport is the transfer of electrons from NADH and succinate to oxygen, coupled to ATP synthesis. The findings separate that chemistry, the sites that can leak electrons to oxygen, and the small ADHD cybrid result. They do not show that ordinary meals or extra cofactors change attention or cognitive energy.

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

- [BRS2-FM1-PM4 - Methionine Cycle Flux](/docs/biological-targets/brs2/fm1/brs2-fm1-pm4-methionine-cycle-flux) — Both systems use redox cofactors, but no assessed study shows that one-carbon turnover changes the rate of these respiratory complexes.
- [BRS6-FM1-PM2 - Glycaemic Variability Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/fm1/brs6-fm1-pm2-glycaemic-variability-regulation) — Carbohydrate metabolism can supply the NADH this chain oxidises. Control of glucose swings is a separate process, and no assessed study shows that those swings change electron-transport rate.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

- [BRS4-FM1-PM2 - NAD⁺ Metabolism](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/fm1/brs4-fm1-pm2-nad-metabolism) — That pathway supplies the NADH that complex I oxidises. Niacin precursors belong with that supply, not as a separate carrier in this chain.
- [BRS4-FM1-PM3 - Creatine–Phosphocreatine Energy Buffering](/docs/biological-targets/brs4/fm1/brs4-fm1-pm3-creatine-phosphocreatine-buffer) — ATP made by this chain is the high-energy phosphate the creatine buffer can store and release. The buffer reaction is not itself electron transport.
