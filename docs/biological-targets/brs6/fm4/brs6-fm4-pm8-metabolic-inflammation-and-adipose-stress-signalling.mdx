    finding_label: Adipokine responses depend on hormone, depot and exposure
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: false
    finding_summary: >-
      Insulin and glucocorticoid exposures regulate leptin synthesis or release and adiponectin
      expression or release in human adipose preparations, with context-dependent responses.
    finding_statement: >-
      Insulin and glucocorticoid exposures regulate leptin synthesis or release and adiponectin
      expression or release in human adipose preparations, with context-dependent responses.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      Human adipose pulse-labelling separates leptin biosynthesis from release of stored protein.
      Dexamethasone can increase leptin synthesis while suppressing adiponectin release in other
      preparations; these are distinct hormone-specific responses. Circulating adiponectin responses
      to systemic glucocorticoids are not uniformly reproduced.
    synthesis: >-
      Human adipose pulse-labelling separates leptin biosynthesis from release of stored protein.
      Dexamethasone can increase leptin synthesis while suppressing adiponectin release in other
      preparations; these are distinct hormone-specific responses. Circulating adiponectin responses
      to systemic glucocorticoids are not uniformly reproduced.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      Ex vivo hormone exposures and pharmacological glucocorticoids do not establish ordinary
      dietary effects. Donor adiposity, depot, exposure duration and concentration matter;
      circulating concentrations do not measure secretion rates or receptor sensitivity.

      Human adipose cortisol regeneration is tissue- and metabolic-context dependent; whole-body
      dietary changes in cortisol metabolism do not necessarily alter subcutaneous adipose 11β-HSD1.

      Human adipose cortisol regeneration is tissue- and metabolic-context dependent; whole-body
      dietary changes in cortisol metabolism do not necessarily alter subcutaneous adipose 11β-HSD1.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      Microdialysis and tracer measurements distinguish local cortisone-to-cortisol conversion from
      systemic turnover. Insulin effects differed by adiposity. A low-carbohydrate crossover altered
      systemic cortisol metabolism without a measured adipose 11β-HSD1 response; weight loss in
      another small study increased adipocyte transcript expression rather than demonstrating
      universal suppression.
    synthesis: >-
      Microdialysis and tracer measurements distinguish local cortisone-to-cortisol conversion from
      systemic turnover. Insulin effects differed by adiposity. A low-carbohydrate crossover altered
      systemic cortisol metabolism without a measured adipose 11β-HSD1 response; weight loss in
      another small study increased adipocyte transcript expression rather than demonstrating
      universal suppression.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      Small selected samples and differing assays; transcript abundance is not in vivo regeneration
      flux. Systemic or hepatic effects cannot be assigned to adipose, and less local cortisol
      activity is not assumed universally beneficial.

## BRS6-4-8 - Adipose Endocrine Regulation

(Communicating Metabolic State Through Adipose Hormones)

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Regulate adipose-derived endocrine signalling and local glucocorticoid responsiveness to communicate metabolic state and coordinate hormonal adaptation.

**Intervention Dominance:** Not established — No principal intervention route established for the defined endocrine operation.

### Overview

Adipose tissue communicates metabolic state through endocrine signals, including leptin and adiponectin. Hormone synthesis, release and target responsiveness respond to metabolic and hormonal conditions. Local cortisone-to-cortisol regeneration and glucocorticoid receptors provide another level of regulation. These operations interact, but they do not establish one universal causal chain: circulating hormone levels, tissue secretion and stress feedback require different measurements. [Scheja & Heeren (2019)](#pm-ref-27); [Tsuji & Tseng (2026)](#pm-ref-28)

- **Biological function:** Distinct adipokines communicate with other tissues through their receptors; production and circulating concentration are not interchangeable. [Scheja & Heeren (2019)](#pm-ref-27)
- **Local regulation:** Human tissue studies distinguish hormone synthesis and secretion, while local cortisol conversion differs from systemic metabolism. [Lee et al. (2007)](#pm-ref-30); [Sandeep et al. (2005)](#pm-ref-29)
- **Brain relevance:** Mouse adipocyte-GR experiments support stress-feedback involvement; the mediator and corresponding human pathway remain unresolved. [de Kloet et al. (2015)](#pm-ref-25)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- Context-dependent leptin and adiponectin output rather than universal increases or suppression. [Lee et al. (2007)](#pm-ref-30); [Degawa-Yamauchi et al. (2005)](#pm-ref-31)
- Local glucocorticoid availability and cellular responsiveness, distinct from systemic cortisol rhythm. [Sandeep et al. (2005)](#pm-ref-29)
- A qualified peripheral contribution to stress feedback demonstrated in a genetic mouse model. [de Kloet et al. (2015)](#pm-ref-25)

## 4. Mechanistic Basis + Scientific Findings

### Summary

Adipokine production and release, local cortisol regeneration and glucocorticoid responsiveness provide distinguishable endocrine control points. A genetic mouse experiment supports peripheral involvement in stress feedback without identifying an adipokine mediator. Diet-related hormone concentrations and tissue transcripts provide relevant but different evidence. [Lee et al. (2007)](#pm-ref-30); [Sandeep et al. (2005)](#pm-ref-29); [de Kloet et al. (2015)](#pm-ref-25)

Established molecular relationships: [11β-HSD1 reaction provenance](?review=1#pm8-sp1) and [leptin/adiponectin identities](?review=1#pm8-sp2).

#### PM8-F12

Human adipose pulse-labelling separates leptin biosynthesis from release of stored protein. Dexamethasone can increase leptin synthesis while suppressing adiponectin release in other preparations; these are distinct hormone-specific responses. Circulating adiponectin responses to systemic glucocorticoids are not uniformly reproduced. [Lee et al. (2007)](#pm-ref-30); [Degawa-Yamauchi et al. (2005)](#pm-ref-31); [Fallo et al. (2004)](#pm-ref-36); [Patel et al. (2006)](#pm-ref-38)

Supporting evidence: [PM8-F12](#pm8-f12).

<span id="pm8-mechanism-f13" />

#### PM8-F13

Microdialysis and tracer measurements distinguish local cortisone-to-cortisol conversion from systemic turnover. Insulin effects differed by adiposity. A low-carbohydrate crossover altered systemic cortisol metabolism without a measured adipose 11β-HSD1 response; weight loss in another small study increased adipocyte transcript expression rather than demonstrating universal suppression. [Sandeep et al. (2005)](#pm-ref-29); [Stimson et al. (2007)](#pm-ref-32); [Tomlinson et al. (2004)](#pm-ref-33)

Supporting evidence: [PM8-F13](#pm8-f13).

<span id="pm8-mechanism-f14" />

#### PM8-F14

The genetic experiment supports a qualified contribution of adipocyte-enriched GR signalling to mouse stress feedback. The communicating signal was not identified; unchanged baseline adipokines do not establish an adipokine-mediated pathway. Human stress feedback through the same route remains unresolved. [de Kloet et al. (2015)](#pm-ref-25)

Supporting evidence: [PM8-F14](#pm8-f14).

<span id="pm8-mechanism-f15" />

#### PM8-F15

Alternate-day and daily energy restriction reduced circulating leptin without an adiponectin change in one randomised trial. POUNDS Lost reported increased circulating adiponectin over two years across diet groups. A small walking trial increased adipose adiponectin mRNA without a significant plasma increase. Duration, tissue measurement and circulating exposure must remain separate. [Trepanowski et al. (2018; online 2017)](#pm-ref-34); [Ma et al. (2016)](#pm-ref-35); [Moghadasi et al. (2013; online 2011)](#pm-ref-37)

Supporting evidence: [PM8-F15](#pm8-f15).

## 5. BRS Pathways and Connections

### 5.1 BRS Pathways

Peripheral endocrine communication contributes to metabolic–endocrine adaptation. No whole-body resource-allocation mechanism is inferred.

### 5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

BRS3 inflammatory regulation can influence adipose endocrine conditions, but inflammation is not this PM’s defining operation. No new causal graph or brain-outcome relationship is admitted.

### 5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

[Insulin Sensitivity & Glucose Disposal](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/fm1/brs6-fm1-pm3-insulin-sensitivity-and-glucose-disposal) addresses insulin-responsive disposal; [Cortisol Rhythm Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/fm2/brs6-fm2-pm4-cortisol-rhythm-regulation) addresses systemic timing. [Stress-Modulated Appetite & Reward Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/fm4/brs6-fm4-pm9-stress-induced-appetite-reward-drive-modulation) addresses central appetite/reward integration, not adipokine production.
