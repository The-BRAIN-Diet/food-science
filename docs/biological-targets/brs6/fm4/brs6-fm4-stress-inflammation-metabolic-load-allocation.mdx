## 1. Definition

Coordinates peripheral metabolic–endocrine signalling and stress-responsive central regulation to communicate energy availability, metabolic state and physiological demand.

- Regulates adipose-derived endocrine output, including adipokine secretion and local glucocorticoid responsiveness.
- Integrates metabolic and stress-related hormonal signals with central appetite and reward regulation.
- Supports communication between peripheral metabolic state and central neuroendocrine responses to changing physiological demands.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

Context-appropriate endocrine communication and stress-responsive appetite/reward regulation; no universal direction of adipokine change or eating response is assumed.

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

### 4.1 Functional Rationale

Coordinates peripheral metabolic–endocrine signalling and stress-responsive central regulation to communicate energy availability, metabolic state and physiological demand.

### 4.2 Evidence Summary

Human adipose experiments distinguish endocrine synthesis, secretion and local glucocorticoid conversion. Mouse adipocyte-GR experiments support qualified stress-feedback involvement; they do not establish a corresponding human dietary pathway. Central stress-eating evidence is assessed separately in PM9. See the linked PM evidence and source assessments.

### 4.3 Functional Boundaries

FM1 retains glycaemic–insulin regulation and glucose disposal. FM2 retains systemic HPA-axis rhythm and cortisol timing. FM3 retains autonomic activation and recovery. BRS3 retains inflammatory activation, redox regulation and resolution. This FM does not own whole-body allostasis or resource allocation across the six BRSs. Inflammatory signalling is a possible Cross-BRS relationship rather than the defining function.
