### 3. Evidence Base

#### Summary

Brain tissue depends on a continuous circulating supply of glucose, and inadequate availability can constrain cerebral energy metabolism ([Mergenthaler et al., 2013](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#mergenthaler_sugar_brain_2013)). This supports glucose availability as the resource represented here, but it does not make slow-release carbohydrate, protein, fat, or soluble-viscous fibre constituents of one limiting pool. Carbohydrate quality and glucose variability remain biologically relevant dietary or metabolic questions, distinct from membership in the glucose resource itself ([Reynolds et al., 2019](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#reynolds_2019_30638909); [Monnier et al., 2006](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#monnier_2006_16638903)).

- **Constraint and membership boundary:** The shared resource is circulating glucose availability; meal composition and fibre may influence glycaemic handling but are not themselves glucose-pool constituents ([Reynolds et al., 2019](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#reynolds_2019_30638909)).
- **Evidence and measurement boundary:** Cerebral glucose dependence and harmful glucose fluctuations are supported, but the sources do not show that ordinary dietary carbohydrate is indispensable or that this resource constrains every listed mechanism ([Mergenthaler et al., 2013](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#mergenthaler_sugar_brain_2013); [Monnier et al., 2006](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#monnier_2006_16638903)).
- **Biological relevance and provision limitation:** Preventing inadequate glucose availability matters for brain energy metabolism; that dependency does not show that adding carbohydrate, fibre, protein, or fat improves neuroendocrine function in adequately nourished people ([Mergenthaler et al., 2013](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#mergenthaler_sugar_brain_2013)).

#### Biological Importance

Glucose is a foundational cerebral energy substrate, and the brain depends on regulated delivery from the circulation. The evidence supports biological dependence on glucose availability. It does not establish dietary carbohydrate as indispensable, identify an ordinary-diet limiting threshold, or show that meal protein, fat, and fibre are members of the same resource.
