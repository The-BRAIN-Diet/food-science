## 1. Definition

Supports integrated regulation of glucose appearance, glycaemic stability, and insulin-supported glucose disposal across the post-prandial period — influencing metabolic continuity, reactive neuroendocrine demand, and cognitive energy availability.

* Regulates the rate and profile of glucose appearance after meals.
* Limits glycaemic variability and post-prandial glucose oscillation.
* Supports insulin-sensitive glucose clearance and utilisation.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

↑ post-prandial metabolic stability; ↓ glycaemic volatility; ↓ reactive catecholamine demand; ↑ continuity of cognitive energy availability

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

<span id="42-integrated-functional-narrative" />

### 4.1 Functional Rationale

Glucose appearance, excursion regulation and disposal jointly shape the timing and stability of available metabolic energy after meals.

<span id="44-evidence-highlights" />

### 4.2 Evidence Summary

The child evidence reports meal-structure and sequencing effects on postprandial glucose, exercise-related improvements in excursion profiles, and oxidative-stress associations with glucose variability. These findings distinguish dynamic exposure from mean glucose. [[1](#fm-ref-1), [2](#fm-ref-2), [4](#fm-ref-4), [5](#fm-ref-5), [14](#fm-ref-14)]

When appearance and disposal are appropriately matched, they would support more stable postprandial glucose availability. A lower glucose excursion does not identify every contributing mechanism or demonstrate improved cognitive energy from the combined system.

### 4.3 Suboptimal Function & Its Effects

Glycaemic–insulin stability & cognitive energy availability may weaken when glucose / energy substrate availability declines or when refined high-glycaemic carbohydrate loads without buffering macronutrients.

Refined high-glycaemic carbohydrate loads without buffering macronutrients may reduce [BRS6(KC1) — Glucose / Energy Substrate Availability](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/kc/brs6-kc1-glucose-energy-substrate-availability). Acute glucose fluctuations that amplify oxidative and metabolic stress relative to sustained hyperglycaemia alone may further strain pool availability, erratic meal timing and skipped meals, ultra-processed low-fibre meal patterns, chronic energy deficit or prolonged underfeeding, while inflammatory and oxidative load increasing metabolic demand.

These pressures may impair [BRS6-FM1-PM1 — Glucose Appearance Kinetics](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/fm1/brs6-fm1-pm1-glucose-appearance-kinetics), weaken [BRS6-FM1-PM2 — Glycaemic Variability Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/fm1/brs6-fm1-pm2-glycaemic-variability-regulation), and reduce the effectiveness of [BRS6-FM1-PM3 — Insulin Sensitivity & Glucose Disposal](/docs/biological-targets/brs6/fm1/brs6-fm1-pm3-insulin-sensitivity-and-glucose-disposal). At the FM level, this may shift BRS6(FM1) toward reduced glycaemic–insulin stability & cognitive energy availability performance.
