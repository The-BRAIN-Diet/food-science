## BRS-X-Hormones-1-7 - Circulating Sex-Hormone Availability & Regulation

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Regulate measured circulating sex-hormone availability. Production, transport, metabolism and clearance are separate parts of that availability. A measurement of one part does not establish the others. The operation stops before receptor binding and downstream signalling. It does not judge a higher or lower concentration as benefit or harm.

### Overview

The initial human record is Rose et al. (1991). Sixty-two premenopausal women raised estimated fibre intake from about 15 g/d to 30 g/d for two months with wheat, oat or corn bran. In the wheat-bran group, serum estrone and estradiol fell. Oat bran and corn bran did not show that change. Progesterone and sex-hormone-binding globulin did not change in the wheat-bran group. The paper does not show a microbial step, a receptor effect or a clinical benefit. A lower concentration is not presented as beneficial. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)

This is the same comparison already cited on [Oestrogen Signalling Stability](/docs/biological-targets/brs-x/hormones/fm1/brs-x-hormones-pm1-oestrogen-signalling-stability) and [Estrobolome Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs-x/hormones/fm1/brs-x-hormones-pm2-estrobolome-regulation). Those Findings stay in place. They are not a second study.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- Wheat bran lowered serum estrone and estradiol in the reported premenopausal group. Oat bran and corn bran did not. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)
- Progesterone and sex-hormone-binding globulin did not change in the wheat-bran group. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)
- The measurement stops at circulating concentration. Receptor signalling remains on PM1. Microbial oestrogen recycling remains on PM2.

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Circulating sex-hormone availability is the measured concentration in blood. Rose et al. (1991) shows a wheat-bran-specific reduction in serum estrone and estradiol. That result does not identify production, binding, metabolism or clearance, and it does not show a receptor or microbial step. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)

**Mechanism boundary:** PM1 owns oestrogen receptor signalling. PM2 owns microbial oestrogen recycling. PM5 and PM6 own androgen signalling and androgen–microbiome relationships. This PM does not take those missions.
