## BRS-X(Hormones-PM1) - Oestrogen Signalling Stability

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Maintain stable oestrogen-linked signalling so dopamine tone and emotional regulation remain steady across hormonal fluctuation.

### Overview

Oestrogen signalling interacts with neural systems involved in working memory and emotional processing. Human evidence indicates that responses vary with dopamine-related baseline context, so more hormone or less fluctuation is not automatically better. This mechanism concerns that interaction, rather than intestinal hormone recycling. Dietary exposures may change measured hormone concentrations, but those changes alone do not show steadier neural signalling. The practical boundary is to keep receptor biology separate from claims for fibre, soy or generic micronutrient supplementation.  Jacobs and D'Esposito (2011) [[4]](#pm-ref-4); Rose et al. (1991) [[7]](#pm-ref-7)

- **Biological Relevance:** Estradiol-related cognitive responses varied with dopamine-related baseline context.  Jacobs and D'Esposito (2011) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- Estradiol-related cognitive responses varied with dopamine-related baseline context.  Jacobs and D'Esposito (2011) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)
- Wheat bran reduced measured estrogens; oat and corn bran did not show the same result.  Rose et al. (1991) [[7]](#pm-ref-7)
