## BRS-X(Hormones-PM4) - Metabolic-Reproductive Hormone Integration

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Enable coordinated integration of insulin and metabolic signalling with reproductive hormone balance.

### Overview

Metabolic signals can influence reproductive hormone production, rather than merely accompanying it. Insulin experiments in ovarian cells identify one direct connection. In women with PCOS, a low-glycaemic-index dietary pattern improved selected insulin-sensitivity and menstrual-cycle readouts compared with a healthy comparison diet. This supports a contextual dietary-practice route, not a requirement for one isolated food or nutrient. The endocrine response depends on the population and treatment context; improved cyclicity does not establish a uniform change in every reproductive hormone.  Nestler et al. (1998) [[3]](#pm-ref-3); Marsh et al. (2010) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)

- **Biological Relevance:** Insulin stimulated testosterone production through its receptor and an inositolglycan-sensitive signalling route.  Nestler et al. (1998) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)
- **Implementation Notes:** Use the bounded dietary-pattern relationship below; its context and limitations travel with the disclosure.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- Insulin stimulated testosterone production through its receptor and an inositolglycan-sensitive signalling route.  Nestler et al. (1998) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)
- Completers showed better oral-test insulin sensitivity and menstrual cyclicity with low GI; reproductive hormone superiority was not established.  Marsh et al. (2010) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)
