## BRS-X(Hormones-PM2) - Estrobolome Regulation

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Shape systemic oestrogen exposure through microbiome-mediated regulation of oestrogen metabolism and recycling.

### Overview

Some gut bacterial enzymes remove glucuronide groups from conjugated oestrogens, making the free hormones available for further handling. This is a specific recycling step, not a claim that every microbiome change improves hormone balance. Enzyme studies identify which reactions can occur, while dietary studies provide only partial evidence about systemic exposure. Supporting the gut generally therefore cannot be equated with controlling these enzymes. The key next link is whether an exposure changes this conversion and its contribution to circulating hormone availability.  Ervin et al. (2019) [[3]](#pm-ref-3); Rose et al. (1991) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)

- **Biological Relevance:** A subset of enzymes cleaved estrogen glucuronides; selective inhibition reduced ex-vivo reactivation.  Ervin et al. (2019) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- A subset of enzymes cleaved estrogen glucuronides; selective inhibition reduced ex-vivo reactivation.  Ervin et al. (2019) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)
- Wheat bran reduced measured estrogens; oat and corn bran did not show the same result.  Rose et al. (1991) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)
