### 3. Evidence Base

#### Summary

The attached evidence does not establish phospholipid or NAPE precursor availability as a shared nutritional bottleneck across distinct endocannabinoidome mechanisms. Garani et al. review altered endocannabinoid-system measures in human psychiatric disorders but do not test dietary phospholipid supply, NAPE synthesis, or nutrient limitation ([Garani et al., 2021](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#garani_endocannabinoid_2021)). The Davies record describes a proposed oatmeal feeding experiment and hypothesised changes in circulating NAPE/NAE; it reports neither completed results nor a second mechanism sharing that resource ([Davies, 2018](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sean_davies_oatmeal_2018)).

- **Constraint and membership boundary:** Dietary NAPE may be relevant to NAPE/NAE biology, but these sources do not establish generic phospholipid matrices or choline-linked precursors as one shared limiting pool ([Davies, 2018](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sean_davies_oatmeal_2018)).
- **Evidence and measurement boundary:** The human review measures disease-associated endocannabinoid-system alterations, while the oatmeal record states a study hypothesis; neither demonstrates dietary precursor insufficiency across distinct mechanisms ([Garani et al., 2021](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#garani_endocannabinoid_2021); [Davies, 2018](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sean_davies_oatmeal_2018)).
- **Biological relevance and provision limitation:** NAPE and NAE biology is biologically relevant, but the available records do not justify food prescriptions, supplementation, or claims that additional phospholipid or choline intake improves signalling or clinical outcomes ([Garani et al., 2021](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#garani_endocannabinoid_2021); [Davies, 2018](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sean_davies_oatmeal_2018)).
