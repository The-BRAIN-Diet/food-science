## BRS-X(ECS-PM4) - Endocannabinoid–Dopamine Neuromodulation

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Shape dopaminergic reward and motivation pathways through endocannabinoid neuromodulatory interaction.

### Overview

Endocannabinoid signalling can influence dopamine responses to reward-predicting cues. The relevant job is local circuit modulation, rather than producing lipid precursors or slowing their degradation. Rat experiments identify a VTA CB1-dependent route and distinguish 2-AG augmentation from anandamide administration. Those distinctions matter because two signalling lipids need not produce the same response. The evidence explains a mechanism contributing to cue-driven behaviour; it does not establish that a nutrient or food improves human motivation through this circuit.  Solinas et al. (2006) [[3]](#pm-ref-3); Oleson et al. (2012) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)

- **Biological Relevance:** CB1 disruption reduced cue responses; augmenting 2-AG, rather than anandamide, enhanced the tested cue-linked response.  Oleson et al. (2012) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- CB1 disruption reduced cue responses; augmenting 2-AG, rather than anandamide, enhanced the tested cue-linked response.  Oleson et al. (2012) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)
- FAAH inhibition prolonged the rapid CB1-sensitive dopamine response; a later component differed pharmacologically.  Solinas et al. (2006) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)
