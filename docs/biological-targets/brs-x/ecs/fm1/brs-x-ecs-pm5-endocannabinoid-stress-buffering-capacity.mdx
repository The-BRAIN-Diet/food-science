## BRS-X(ECS-PM5) - Endocannabinoid Stress-Buffering Capacity

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Sustain endocannabinoid-mediated stress buffering so HPA-axis and neuroinflammatory reactivity stay proportionate.

### Overview

Endocannabinoid signals participate in stress regulation through region-specific brain circuits. In rats, stress-related anandamide clearance in the amygdala contributes to HPA-axis activation. Human exercise after a fear-learning protocol changes circulating anandamide and later threat responses, providing a bounded practice candidate. These are complementary findings, not a single measured gut-to-brain or diet-to-stress chain. The evidence supports investigating stress recovery while keeping peripheral lipid measurements, neural mechanisms and neuroinflammatory outcomes separate.  Hill et al. (2009) [[5]](#pm-ref-5); Crombie et al. (2021) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)

- **Biological Relevance:** Restraint increased FAAH and reduced AEA; basolateral-amygdala FAAH inhibition attenuated corticosterone through a CB1-dependent route.  Hill et al. (2009) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)
- **Implementation Notes:** Use the bounded lifestyle relationship below; its context and limitations travel with the disclosure.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- Restraint increased FAAH and reduced AEA; basolateral-amygdala FAAH inhibition attenuated corticosterone through a CB1-dependent route.  Hill et al. (2009) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)
- Moderate exercise increased circulating AEA; the full-sample threat response was lower at reinstatement.  Crombie et al. (2021) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)
