## BRS-X(ECS-PM3) - FAAH-Mediated Endocannabinoid Preservation

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Preserve endocannabinoid signalling duration by limiting FAAH-mediated degradation of anandamide and related NAEs.

### Overview

FAAH is an enzyme that breaks down anandamide and related signalling lipids. Its activity helps determine how long a locally produced signal remains available. Rat pharmacology and laboratory assays establish that changing this clearance step can alter anandamide effects. They do not establish that eating soy, polyphenol-rich foods or a general phospholipid diet inhibits brain FAAH. The useful distinction is between a verified clearance mechanism and a dietary intervention that still needs exposure-specific evidence.  Thors et al. (2007) [[1]](#pm-ref-1); Solinas et al. (2006) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)

- **Biological Relevance:** FAAH inhibition prolonged the rapid CB1-sensitive dopamine response; a later component differed pharmacologically.  Solinas et al. (2006) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- FAAH inhibition prolonged the rapid CB1-sensitive dopamine response; a later component differed pharmacologically.  Solinas et al. (2006) [[2]](#pm-ref-2)
- Genistein and daidzein altered anandamide uptake under the tested laboratory conditions.  Thors et al. (2007) [[1]](#pm-ref-1)
