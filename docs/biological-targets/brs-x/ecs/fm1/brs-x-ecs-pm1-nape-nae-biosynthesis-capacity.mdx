## BRS-X(ECS-PM1) - NAPE → NAE Biosynthesis Capacity

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Enable NAPE-to-NAE biosynthesis so endocannabinoidome signalling lipids remain available for stress and motivation regulation.

### Overview

N-acylethanolamines are signalling lipids made from membrane phospholipid precursors. This mechanism covers the formation step that supplies these molecules, rather than the later receptor responses or their degradation. Brain and enzyme experiments identify the precursor and catalytic machinery. That makes the synthesis pathway biologically meaningful, while leaving open whether particular foods increase neural production. A tested phosphatidylethanolamine meal did not improve satiety; that outcome does not negate its intracellular precursor role.  Cadas et al. (1997) [[3]](#pm-ref-3); Magotti et al. (2015) [[4]](#pm-ref-4); Lithander et al. (2008) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)

- **Biological Relevance:** N-arachidonoyl phosphatidylethanolamine and its biosynthetic pathway were identified in brain preparations.  Cadas et al. (1997) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

- N-arachidonoyl phosphatidylethanolamine and its biosynthetic pathway were identified in brain preparations.  Cadas et al. (1997) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)
- The membrane substrate enters a binuclear zinc active centre; bile acids influence enzyme organisation and activity.  Magotti et al. (2015) [[4]](#pm-ref-4)
- PE-containing meals did not improve the tested satiety or energy-intake endpoints.  Lithander et al. (2008) [[5]](#pm-ref-5)

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

N-arachidonoyl phosphatidylethanolamine and its biosynthetic pathway were identified in brain preparations.  Cadas et al. (1997) [[3]](#pm-ref-3)

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

Precursor identification and human enzyme structure support the biosynthetic machinery. The meal experiment addresses satiety, not synthesis flux; a food-responsive neural supply requirement remains unestablished.
