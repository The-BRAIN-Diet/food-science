scientific_findings:
  - id: PM2-F1
    finding_label: Barrier translocation can raise systemic inflammatory signalling
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: >-
      The gut lining normally keeps lipopolysaccharide out of the blood. Dietary
      change can alter that lining. Lipopolysaccharide that gets through
      activates Toll-like receptor 4 and is reviewed as a route to low-grade
      systemic inflammation.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      This is the exposure route. It is a review, not a measurement of how much
      lipopolysaccharide crosses after a fibre intake.
    finding_statement: >-
      Metabolic endotoxemia is lipopolysaccharide reaching the blood after a
      change in the gut barrier, with downstream inflammatory signalling through
      Toll-like receptor 4.
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: >-
      Mohammad and Thiemermann review diet-linked metabolic endotoxemia. The gut
      epithelium normally limits lipopolysaccharide absorption. Structural
      change in that lining lets lipopolysaccharide into the bloodstream.
      Lipopolysaccharide then activates Toll-like receptor 4 and
      pro-inflammatory cytokines. The review suggests dietary interventions
      might reduce that inflammation. It does not test one.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      A narrative review of the route is not a human translocation-flux trial
      and not a fibre-dose result.
    evidence_considered:
      - label: Mohammad and Thiemermann (2021)
        citation_key: mohammad_role_2021
        href: /docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#mohammad_role_2021
        data_level: Mechanistic
        exposure_context: narrative review of diet-linked endotoxemia
        evidence_source: repository-inherited
        directional_finding: >-
          Barrier change can raise plasma lipopolysaccharide, and that
          lipopolysaccharide activates Toll-like receptor 4.
        assessment:
          study: >-
            Narrative review of metabolic endotoxemia, barrier change, and
            inflammatory signalling.
          population: Animal and human literature. No new cohort.
          result: >-
            Lipopolysaccharide that crosses a changed gut lining is reviewed as
            a contributor to low-grade systemic inflammation.
          effect_magnitude: No single translocation rate or intake effect is pooled.
          evidence_summary: >-
            Endotoxin spillover is a barrier-to-blood route, not a nutrient
            reaction.
          limitations: >-
            The review does not measure translocation after fermentable fibre,
            and it does not test butyrate as a requirement.

## BRS3-FM1-PM2 - Gut-Derived Inflammatory Signalling

(Endotoxin Spillover and Gut Interface Management)

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Limit gut-derived endotoxin spillover so barrier dysfunction does not drive systemic inflammatory load.


**Intervention Dominance:** Diet-Supported in an obese-mouse experiment; no principal intervention route established

A defined oligofructose exposure changed permeability and circulating LPS in obese mice. Human implementation and comparative route prominence remain unestablished. [[14]](#pm-ref-14)

### Overview

The gut barrier helps contain microbial products within the intestine. When that containment changes, endotoxin can contribute to systemic inflammatory signalling. This PM concerns the gut-derived load reaching the circulation, connecting intestinal barrier function with inflammatory regulation elsewhere. Experimental evidence identifies epithelial redox protection and a prebiotic-sensitive barrier route, while their context determines what can be inferred about dietary action. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) [[12]](#pm-ref-12) [[14]](#pm-ref-14)

- **Benefits:** Limiting inappropriate microbial-product entry helps contain inflammatory pressure; the reviewed experiments do not establish a clinical treatment benefit. [[1]](#pm-ref-1)
- **Implementation Notes:** Cysteine and glycine provide upstream glutathione resources. Oligofructose altered barrier readouts in obese mice, but a human intake protocol is not established. [[11]](#pm-ref-11) [[14]](#pm-ref-14)
- **Biological Relevance:** Gut-barrier processes connect BRS5 to this gut-derived load; [PM1](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-pm1-nf-kb-signalling-regulation) concerns the downstream NF-κB response.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects
Gut-to-blood microbial-product handling; barrier-related systemic inflammatory signalling.

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Systemic inflammatory tone can be driven partly by gut-derived signals. When barrier integrity weakens, endotoxin and related microbial inputs enter circulation more readily, increasing low-grade inflammatory signalling within [BRS3(FM1) - Anti-Inflammatory Signalling Tone](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-anti-inflammatory-signalling-tone).

#### (Endotoxin-linked inflammatory load)

Mohammad and Thiemermann (2021) review lipopolysaccharide reaching the blood after a change in the gut lining, with inflammatory signalling through Toll-like receptor 4. [[1]](#pm-ref-1) That is the spillover this mechanism governs.

#### (Microbial and barrier modulation logic)

Koh et al. (2016) describe fermentation of dietary fibre to short-chain fatty acids. [[10]](#pm-ref-10) Cavaliere et al. (2022) gave butyrate to obese mice and saw a partial fall in cortical neuroinflammation. [[6]](#pm-ref-6) Neither result measures lipopolysaccharide crossing the barrier, so neither is a dietary requirement of this spillover.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

Downstream NF-κB transcriptional control is handled by [BRS3-FM1-PM1 - NF-kB Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-pm1-nf-kb-signalling-regulation). Downstream cytokine-network expression belongs to [BRS3-FM3-PM7 - Cytokine Network Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm3/brs3-fm3-pm7-cytokine-network-modulation). Native gut-barrier biology is represented within [BRS5](/docs/biological-targets/gut-brain-axis-enteric-nervous-system); this PM captures the BRS3 inflammatory-tone output of gut-derived signals.

#### (Integration within BRS3)

This PM links gut-derived inputs to inflammatory tone within [BRS3(FM1)](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-anti-inflammatory-signalling-tone). Barrier and endotoxin-containment context imported through section 4.3 connected mechanisms conditions translocation load.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

The evidence supports gut-derived inflammatory load as a barrier-related process. Glutathione participates in oxidant-sensitive epithelial protection, but is not a universal barrier constraint. A defined prebiotic intervention changed permeability and circulating LPS in obese mice; this does not establish a generic fibre requirement or a human intake target.
