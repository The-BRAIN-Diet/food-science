summary: >-
  NF-κB regulates inflammatory gene programmes. IKK-dependent inhibitor
  phosphorylation permits its activation; glutathione-dependent redox changes
  also regulate kinase activity and DNA binding. These distinct cellular
  readouts support the mechanism, but do not establish a dietary dose that
  improves human inflammatory regulation.

scientific_findings:
  - id: PM1-F1
    finding_label: IKK phosphorylation is required for NF-κB activation
    presentation: mechanistic-basis
    fm_rollup: true
    finding_summary: >-
      A purified complex from HeLa cells contains two related kinases, IKK-1 and
      IKK-2. They phosphorylate the inhibitor proteins that hold NF-κB. Mutant
      IKK-2 changes NF-κB movement into the nucleus and NF-κB-dependent reporter
      activity.
    finding_interpretation: >-
      This identifies the kinase step. It does not show that dietary magnesium,
      polyphenols, fibre, or omega-3 fats change how fast those genes are
      transcribed.
    finding_statement: >-
      IKK-1 and IKK-2 phosphorylate the NF-κB inhibitor proteins, and IKK-2 is
      required for NF-κB activation in the cell systems tested. This is an
      upstream activation step, rather than transcription itself.
    synthesised_evidence_confidence: not-yet-scored
    synthesis: >-
      Mercurio and colleagues purified a large IκB kinase complex from
      cytokine-stimulated HeLa cells. It phosphorylated IκB-alpha and IκB-beta.
      The two kinase components, IKK-1 and IKK-2, are related serine kinases.
      Mutant IKK-2 had pronounced effects on RelA nuclear translocation and on
      an NF-κB-dependent reporter. The paper does not test diet.
    synthesis_limitations: >-
      The result is a cell-biochemistry identification of the kinase. It is not
      a human intake trial, and it does not measure dietary magnesium.

### Overview

NF-κB helps cells translate immune signals into inflammatory gene programmes. Its activation and DNA binding are regulated at several steps, including inhibitor phosphorylation and glutathione-dependent redox control. [[7]](#pm-ref-7) [[11]](#pm-ref-11) [[12]](#pm-ref-12) Keeping this response appropriate is the biological aim; suppressing every response is not an established health benefit.

- **Benefits:** This mechanism governs inflammatory signalling rather than a proven nutrient treatment or clinical outcome. The reviewed experiments establish regulation of activation and DNA binding. [[7]](#pm-ref-7) [[11]](#pm-ref-11)
- **Implementation Notes:** Cysteine and glycine have supported upstream roles through glutathione. Combined supplementation increased red-cell glutathione synthesis in older adults, but did not measure NF-κB or establish a food dose. [[13]](#pm-ref-13)
- **Biological Relevance:** Glutathione provision connects this PM to redox biology; NF-κB regulation remains distinct from glutathione synthesis and downstream cytokine-network outputs. [[11]](#pm-ref-11) [[12]](#pm-ref-12)

## 2. Primary Biological Effects
NF-κB activation, DNA binding and redox-sensitive inflammatory gene regulation

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

When inflammatory transcriptional programmes remain engaged, downstream cytokine and immune-signalling tone escalates. NF-κB is a principal upstream node through which those programmes are expressed within [BRS3(FM1) - Anti-Inflammatory Signalling Tone](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-anti-inflammatory-signalling-tone).

#### (NF-κB as an inflammatory transcriptional node)

Mercurio et al. (1997) identified IKK-1 and IKK-2 as the kinases that phosphorylate the inhibitor proteins holding NF-κB, and showed that IKK-2 is required for NF-κB activation in the cell systems tested. [[7]](#pm-ref-7) That kinase step permits activation; it is not transcription itself. The cytokine messengers that follow it are a later step — see PM7.

#### (Signalling pressure and pattern context)

Glutathione-dependent redox regulation acts on IKK activity and NF-κB DNA binding. [[11]](#pm-ref-11) [[12]](#pm-ref-12) Redox state can inhibit as well as permit activation, so more precursor supply cannot be assumed to suppress inflammation. The attached dietary-pattern studies do not independently establish a PM-specific intervention. Adams (2001) reviews ATP-dependent protein-kinase chemistry, including the usual metal partner of that reaction. [[8]](#pm-ref-8) That chemistry is not a dietary magnesium result.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

Downstream cytokine-network expression is handled by [BRS3-FM3-PM7 - Cytokine Network Modulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm3/brs3-fm3-pm7-cytokine-network-modulation). Gut-derived endotoxin load is represented by [BRS3-FM1-PM2 - Gut-Derived Inflammatory Signalling](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-pm2-gut-derived-inflammatory-signalling). EPA/DHA as specialised pro-resolving mediator substrates, and review claims that SPMs downregulate NF-κB, belong to [BRS3-FM3-PM8 - Eicosanoid / SPM Balance](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm3/brs3-fm3-pm8-eicosanoid-spm-balance). This PM does not currently carry an evidence-qualified EPA/DHA dietary requirement or System Optimisation Practice for NF-κB transcriptional tone. Endogenous antioxidant induction and net redox balance belong to [BRS3-FM2-PM3 - Nrf2-Mediated Cellular Defence Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm2/brs3-fm2-pm3-nrf2-are-antioxidant-activation) and [BRS3-FM2-PM4 - ROS Generation vs Clearance Balance](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm2/brs3-fm2-pm4-ros-generation-vs-clearance-balance).

#### (Integration within BRS3)

This PM anchors transcriptional inflammatory tone within [BRS3(FM1)](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-anti-inflammatory-signalling-tone). Gut-barrier context imported through [BRS5-FM1-PM1 - Gut Barrier / Tight Junction Integrity](/docs/biological-targets/brs5/fm1/brs5-fm1-pm1-gut-barrier-tight-junction-integrity) conditions endotoxin-linked pressure on this pathway.

### 4.1 Scientific Findings

#### Summary

IKK experiments establish an activation gate, while redox studies demonstrate glutathione-dependent regulation of kinase activity and DNA binding. Human precursor tracing supports glutathione provision, but does not measure NF-κB. Together these findings support a bounded upstream supply relationship, not a proven dietary anti-inflammatory effect.
