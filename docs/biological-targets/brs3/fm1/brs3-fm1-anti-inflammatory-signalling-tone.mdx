(Calming Excessive Immune Signalling)

## 1. Mission & Overview

### Mission

Keep inflammatory signalling intensity in check so pro-inflammatory transcription, cytokines, and gut-derived immune load do not chronically dominate.

### Overview

A diet-actionable control point that helps regulate how intensely inflammatory signals fire across cytokine networks, NF-κB transcription (a master switch for pro-inflammatory gene expression), gut-derived inputs, and lipid-mediator pathways.

* Helps keep pro-inflammatory transcriptional tone in check through NF-κB regulation.
* Limits gut-derived endotoxin spillover into systemic inflammatory signalling — Supporting BRS5.
* Connects dietary antioxidant and fatty-acid context to inflammatory intensity.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects

↓ NF-kB tone; ↓ pro-inflammatory cytokines; ↑ immune signalling balance

## 4. Mechanistic Basis (Integrated FM Narrative)

<span id="42-integrated-functional-narrative" />

### 4.1 Functional Rationale

Inflammatory transcriptional regulation and containment of gut-derived endotoxin contribute to managing inflammatory signalling pressure. They act at distinct stages, so a change in one readout does not establish control of the entire system.

<span id="44-evidence-highlights" />

### 4.2 Evidence Summary

Kinase studies establish the NF-κB activation step, while metabolic-endotoxaemia reviews connect barrier translocation with systemic inflammatory exposure. Fermentation and SCFA sources supply relevant gut–host context, but do not directly measure reduced endotoxin translocation following fibre intake. [[5](#fm-ref-5), [8](#fm-ref-8), [9](#fm-ref-9), [10](#fm-ref-10), [11](#fm-ref-11)]

Effective containment and regulated transcription would be expected to limit excessive inflammatory signalling. The aggregated evidence does not demonstrate dietary control of both steps or show that a circulating inflammatory marker identifies which step changed.

### 4.3 Suboptimal Function & Its Effects

Anti-inflammatory signalling tone may weaken when antioxidant substrate availability becomes chronically inadequate or when habitual EPA/DHA intake fails to support resolution-competent lipid-mediator context.

Low fruit and vegetable intake may reduce [BRS3(KC1) — Antioxidant Substrate Availability](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/kc/brs3-kc1-antioxidant-substrate-availability). Low polyphenol density, poor sulfur-amino-acid and glutathione-building substrate availability, chronic oxidative burden, and ultra-processed patterns displacing antioxidant-rich foods may further amplify inflammatory signalling pressure.

Low omega-3 intake may limit EPA/DHA substrate availability for lipid-mediator pathways. Excessive omega-6 dominance, low oily-fish consumption, poor fatty-acid diversity, and chronic inflammatory load may skew lipid-mediator context away from resolution-competent signalling.

Gut barrier compromise and metabolic endotoxemia may amplify gut-derived inflammatory inputs—see [BRS5(FM1) — Gut Barrier Integrity and Immune Interface](/docs/biological-targets/brs5/fm1/brs5-fm1-gut-barrier-integrity-and-immune-interface).

These pressures may impair [BRS3-FM1-PM1 — NF-kB Signalling Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-pm1-nf-kb-signalling-regulation) and weaken [BRS3-FM1-PM2 — Gut-Derived Inflammatory Signalling](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm1/brs3-fm1-pm2-gut-derived-inflammatory-signalling). At the FM level, this may shift BRS3(FM1) toward chronically elevated pro-inflammatory tone.
