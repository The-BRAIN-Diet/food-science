### Overview

Peroxyl radicals can keep oxidising polyunsaturated fats in membranes. Niki (2014) shows that vitamin E is the main lipophilic antioxidant that breaks that chain, and that it does so for peroxyl radicals rather than for every oxidant. [[5]](#pm-ref-5) Traber and Atkinson (2007) place that job in protecting those fats in membranes. [[6]](#pm-ref-6) Extra vitamin E was not shown to lower peroxidation.

- **Benefits:** Breaking this chain helps keep polyunsaturated fats intact in membranes. [[5]](#pm-ref-5) [[6]](#pm-ref-6)
- **Implementation Notes:** Vitamin E is the nutrient in that reaction. [[5]](#pm-ref-5) No intake was shown to lower peroxidation. A prevention trial found more prostate cancers with 400 IU a day. [[7]](#pm-ref-7)
- **Biological Relevance:** Generation versus clearance of reactive oxygen is the upstream pressure — see PM4.

## 2. Primary Biological Effects
↓ lipid peroxidation; ↑ membrane stability

## 4. Mechanistic Basis

### Summary

Polyunsaturated fatty acids in membranes are especially vulnerable to oxidative attack. When peroxidation outpaces lipid-phase antioxidant protection, structural and signalling lipid pools destabilise within [BRS3(FM2) - Antioxidant Defense Capacity](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm2/brs3-fm2-antioxidant-defense-capacity).

#### (Lipid peroxidation burden)

Niki (2014) shows that peroxyl radicals propagate through polyunsaturated fats and that vitamin E breaks that chain. [[5]](#pm-ref-5) Malondialdehyde comparisons in clinical cohorts are not this reaction.

#### (Lipid-phase antioxidant protection)

Traber and Atkinson (2007) place the vitamin function of vitamin E in protecting polyunsaturated fats in membranes. [[6]](#pm-ref-6) Lutein and lycopene absorption are not this chain-breaking job. Vitamin E's later regeneration by vitamin C is a different job — see PM6.

#### (Boundaries of the mechanism)

Net ROS generation versus clearance is handled by [BRS3-FM2-PM4 - ROS Generation vs Clearance Balance](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm2/brs3-fm2-pm4-ros-generation-vs-clearance-balance). Transcriptional Nrf2 induction belongs to [BRS3-FM2-PM3 - Nrf2-Mediated Cellular Defence Regulation](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm2/brs3-fm2-pm3-nrf2-are-antioxidant-activation). Eicosanoid and SPM substrate quality downstream is represented by [BRS3-FM3-PM8 - Eicosanoid / SPM Balance](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm3/brs3-fm3-pm8-eicosanoid-spm-balance).

#### (Integration within BRS3)

This PM protects membrane lipid integrity within [BRS3(FM2)](/docs/biological-targets/brs3/fm2/brs3-fm2-antioxidant-defense-capacity). No shared-pool mapping is established. Mitochondrial reactive-oxygen generation is a neighbouring pressure — see BRS4.
