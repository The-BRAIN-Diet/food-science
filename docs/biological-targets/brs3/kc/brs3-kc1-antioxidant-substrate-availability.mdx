### 3. Evidence Base

#### Summary

This constraint concerns cysteine and glycine availability for glutathione synthesis. Both amino acids are substrates of glutathione, and a human study in older adults with deficient synthesis found that glycine plus N-acetylcysteine (a cysteine source) restored red-cell synthesis and glutathione concentrations. Their availability can therefore form a coherent precursor bottleneck under relevant deficiency states. The evidence is conditional, however, and does not establish ordinary dietary limitation, a universal intake threshold or isolated effects of either amino acid in the general population ([Sekhar et al., 2011](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sekhar_glutathione_2011)).

- **Constraint and membership boundary:** Glutamate is required for glutathione structure but is not shown to be nutritionally limiting; polyphenols and vitamin C belong to wider redox biology rather than this precursor-availability constraint ([Packer et al., 1997](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#packer_vitamin_1997)).
- **Evidence and measurement boundary:** Eight older adults were restudied after 14 days of glycine plus N-acetylcysteine, alongside eight younger controls. Isotope tracing measured red-cell glutathione synthesis; there was no placebo-controlled treatment arm, no isolated constituent comparison and no brain measurement ([Sekhar et al., 2011](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sekhar_glutathione_2011)).
- **Biological relevance and provision limitation:** Adequate precursor availability can prevent glutathione-synthesis constraint under the studied conditions, but this does not show that extra intake improves redox function or clinical outcomes in glutathione-replete people ([Sekhar et al., 2011](/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sekhar_glutathione_2011)).

#### Biological Importance

Cysteine is the sulfur-amino-acid substrate in glutathione. In older adults with deficient glutathione synthesis, combined glycine and N-acetylcysteine provision restored red-cell synthesis, supporting conditional cysteine availability as one part of the precursor constraint.

#### Biological Importance

Glycine contributes the terminal amino-acid residue of glutathione. In the same older-adult study, glycine given with N-acetylcysteine corrected deficient red-cell synthesis, supporting glycine as a conditional co-limiting precursor rather than a universally limiting dietary input.

<h4 class="brs-hub-integration-section-title">Supporting Evidence</h4>

<li class="brs-hub-integration-evidence-item"><p><a href="/docs/papers/BRAIN-Diet-References#sekhar_glutathione_2011">Sekhar et al., 2011</a> — Glycine plus N-acetylcysteine restored deficient red-cell glutathione synthesis and concentrations in eight older adults after 14 days. The before/after study does not isolate glycine or establish ordinary dietary glycine shortage, brain synthesis or long-term clinical benefit.</p></li>
</ul>
