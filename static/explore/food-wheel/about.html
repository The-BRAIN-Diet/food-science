Scope and evidence

This reviewed snapshot, top-ten experiment dated 9 November 2026, shows 65 selected foods, 890 food–substance–PM connections and 100 covered targets out of 151 existing dietary PM–substance targets. 51 targets remain without a qualifying food ranking. These are not necessarily missing foods.

Composition comes exclusively from the BRAIN canonical nutritional records used for this reviewed snapshot. Source references, preparation, species and limitations are retained. Both PM dropdowns and this wheel use one generated ranked snapshot from publication-approved canonical food records. Unpublished research backfills are excluded. Scientific admissions remain unchanged.

Relative Source Power equals a food's verified concentration divided by the highest verified comparable food concentration, multiplied by 100. The provisional eligibility threshold is 10%. Up to ten qualifying foods are selected per admitted PM–substance target, with at least two distinct comparable foods. Lists are not padded.

Raw, cooked, salted and other ordinary edible preparations can compete when chemical identity, analytical definition, units and measurement basis are compatible. Dry-weight and edible-weight values, incompatible chemical forms and unsupported conversions remain separate. Preparation, sodium content and serving considerations remain important metadata; source ranking is not recipe formulation.

Diagram inclusion is not dietary admission. Target coverage counts distinct substance–PM pairs, not clinical efficacy, nutrient density, serving adequacy or tissue delivery. Food positions and colours reflect their qualified connections. The existing substance–system network retains contextual and candidate relationships; it is not a set of proven food interventions. No additional food source is admitted because a biological connection exists.

Presence-only, unverified, single-source and incompatible records remain in diagnostic reporting, not in the ranked food ring. Trout roe has no verified quantitative ranking. MEXT salted chum-salmon roe is a measured source of DHA and EPA; this does not establish ADHD benefit.
