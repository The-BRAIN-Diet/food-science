## C1 — Baseline energetic reserve under sustained demand

**Recovered proposition and gate.** PM10-EG-5 links PM10-F3/F5. It asks whether baseline energetic state predicts response during sleep restriction, illness, ageing or sustained demand; these are several different experiments. PM10 already admits bounded capacity provision by tyrosine, methionine, B2 and iron. Those admissions do not establish additional-intake benefit, tissue CoQ delivery or clinical efficacy.

**Evidence.** Deshwal's compartmental CoQ work supplies cellular rationale, not a human reserve-response trial ([primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9928583/), STARD7/CoQ compartmentation experiments; full methods not independently reverified here because PMC access was challenged). Dohlmann studied 37 statin-treated adults, oral CoQ10 400 mg/day for eight weeks versus placebo: plasma exposure rose without corresponding muscle CoQ/function improvement ([Methods, supplementation and muscle biopsies; Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9495827/)). Schmücker's older-adult study also reports a plasma/tissue-function disconnect; publisher full text was inaccessible in this pass, so exact protocol details are not verified ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-025-02068-9)). Gamal's six-month adjunctive atomoxetine/CoQ10 trial in children supplies clinical context, not mitochondrial mediation or baseline-reserve testing; exact dose was not independently verified ([primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34819012/)).

**Chain and alternatives.** CoQ participation in electron transfer is established; baseline tissue concentration as a useful reserve metric is inferred; reserve → preservation/recovery under a specified demand is untested by this evidence. Training, oxygen delivery, mitochondrial mass, substrate supply, illness and medication could explain response. Plasma change is not evidence of tissue target engagement.

**Proposed rewrite.** “In adults undertaking a standardised repeated muscle-work challenge, lower baseline mitochondrial-normalised muscle CoQ availability predicts impaired energetic recovery; a manipulation that demonstrably raises that pool improves recovery preferentially in the low-pool group.” This replaces an all-purpose resilience claim with a tissue-specific hypothesis. Do not claim it covers brain or sleep restriction.

**Decisive test.** First a mechanistic cohort with biopsy CoQ/redox assessment, respirometry, exercise workload and phosphorus-MRS PCr recovery, controlling fitness and perfusion. Then a placebo-controlled intervention only after tissue target engagement is demonstrated. Support: prespecified interaction and improved energetic recovery after pool correction. Against: a precise null despite verified tissue change, or disappearance of the relationship after mitochondrial mass/perfusion adjustment. Failed uptake tests delivery, not the causal pool hypothesis. No particular supplement dose is newly recommended.

**Ontology/status.** Destination PM10/BRS4 only; `coq10` and the existing precursor IDs remain separate. Beef's canonical qualified CoQ occurrence and amino-acid foods are not proof of a muscle dose. Proposed wording: **“Unestablished tissue-specific reserve-response relationship; dietary capacity admissions unchanged.”** Resolve the chosen tissue/demand definition before registration.

## C2 — Microbial diversity and substrate processing

**Recovered proposition and gate.** PM4-EG-RECOVERY-1, PM4-F1/F2. The unresolved claim is generic causal improvement from diversity, not whether organisms and glycans interact. Existing substrate-specific admissions and exclusions remain intact.

**Evidence.** Patnode used male gnotobiotic mice with defined human-derived communities and controlled fibre diets, including ingredient mixtures and community-member omissions. The study measured competitive fitness, proteins and glycan degradation, including recoverable glycan-coated beads; community composition altered processing ([STAR Methods: gnotobiotic mice/defined communities; Figs 3–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6760872/)). This is stronger mechanistic evidence than a richness association, but it does not randomise “diversity” independently of organism identity. Wastyk randomised 36 generally healthy adults to high-fibre or fermented-food diets in a ten-week intervention within a seventeen-week protocol. Functional-potential, diversity and inflammatory endpoints diverged; CAZyme abundance is not measured substrate flux ([Fig 1, intervention and metagenomic results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9020749/); indexed primary text inspected, full-page access challenged).

**Chain.** Community membership → processing of particular glycans is experimentally supported. Richness → greater functional breadth is plausible but not guaranteed; richness → greater processing at fixed functional composition is unresolved. Competition can reduce a degrader's access; redundancy can leave flux unchanged.

**Disposition and falsification.** Merge as C3's mediator question, while retaining a separable mechanistic experiment: factorial defined communities varying richness and glycan-utilisation capability at matched biomass, substrate and incubation time. Measure substrate disappearance, enzyme activity and labelled-carbon products. Support for the richness-specific proposition requires effects beyond the presence/abundance of the relevant degraders; a null after functional matching counts against it. A productive species replacement without greater richness supports a composition mechanism instead. No generic “higher diversity is better” claim should be registered.

**Ontology/status.** Existing `inulin`, `pectin`, `resistant-starch` and other individually admitted PM4 substrates may be experimental inputs, with exact forms retained. Community diversity is a community property, not a new canonical nutrient. PM4/BRS5 owns the question; no forced cross-BRS edge. **“Proposed mediator within substrate-variety hypothesis; diversity is not a validated processing-capacity surrogate.”**

## C3 — Plant variety and microbial substrate processing

**Recovered proposition.** PM4-EG-PLANT-VARIETY-1 explicitly rejects interpreting 30 plants as a demonstrated optimum or threshold. It preserves the distinction between a useful tested diet and proof of a universal count rule.

**Evidence.** McDonald reports self-reported plant-variety associations with microbiome/metabolome measures; no allocated intervention, fixed dose or processing-flux experiment ([American Gut, plant-consumption comparisons/Fig 5](https://journals.asm.org/doi/full/10.1128/msystems.00031-18)). Stanford's 25-person stage 3–4 CKD crossover compared ≥30 with ≤15 plants weekly for six weeks each, separated by ≥4 weeks. Its abstract reports no consistent overall uremic-toxin reduction ([primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12097182/)). Supplement Item S1, pp2–3, confirms individualised counselling and weekly food boxes; count was not isolated from dietary composition ([methods supplement](https://cdn-links.lww.com/permalink/cjn/c/cjn_2025_03_13_stanford_2024-000689_sdc2.pdf)). Creedon's BIOME randomised 399 adults, analysed 349: 30 g/day mixed plant product versus 28 g croutons or probiotic for six weeks. Species composition and symptoms changed, but between-group microbiome health-rank differences were not significant; the acute substudy glucose endpoint was null ([primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42423005/)). The canonical review also records decreased richness; this detail was not independently reverified from the full paper in this pass.

**Proposed combined hypothesis.** “At matched total fermentable-substrate intake, a defined increase in plant-substrate variety increases the breadth of microbial processing of a prespecified glycan panel in the tested population; changes in functional community composition, including but not necessarily greater richness, may mediate the response.”

**Decisive test.** Controlled six-week feeding comparison in adults with stable medications, with energy and total fermentable carbohydrate matched, chemically characterised preparations, baseline stratification and a prespecified glycan-utilisation panel. Collect repeated stool for standardised ex-vivo utilisation and enzyme assays; use an appropriate tracer/mass-balance substudy to test in-vivo relevance. Analyse richness, taxa and functional activities separately. Support: improved prespecified processing breadth with reproducible mediator evidence. Against: a precise null at matched exposure; functional changes without richness change count against the richness mediator, not against the diet's effect. Ex-vivo performance alone is not proof of in-vivo flux. Transit, biomass, substrate chemistry and adherence are alternatives. Plant count cannot be perfectly isolated from all chemical diversity: name that limitation rather than claiming a pure count effect.

**Ontology/status.** One receiving record in PM4/BRS5, two linked sub-questions. Example canonical source anchors: `asparagus` → `inulin` (dry-matter spear measurement); `bananas`/`potatoes` → `resistant-starch` (preparation/subtype limits). Apple pectin occurrence does not override PM4's citrus-specific restrictions. No food is newly admitted by an experimental menu. **“Unestablished substrate-variety effect on processing; microbial diversity is a candidate mediator; no universal weekly plant-count threshold.”**

## C4 — Vitamin D and epithelial VDR capacity

**Recovered proposition.** PM1-EG-RECOVERY-VDR, PM1-F12, asks for a Type A supply assessment distinct from Type B additional-intake regulation. The local record does not contain a newly admitted vitamin-D lever. This is partly an unfinished reconciliation, not simply an untested biological mechanism.

**Evidence.** Kong compared VDR-null and wild-type mice under DSS injury and exposed Caco-2 monolayers to calcitriol. Outcomes include junction proteins and transepithelial resistance, with receptor perturbation informing dependence ([primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17962355/); [publisher source, Methods and DSS figure](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpgi.00398.2007)). Indexed primary text specifies 2.5% DSS days1–7 then water days7–15. The full publisher methods were blocked; cell dose, exposure duration, mouse rescue-diet/calcium matching and complete controls remain unverified here. No dietary vitamin-D dose can be inferred from that calcitriol exposure.

**Chain.** Receptor/active-ligand dependence is supported in the studied models. Dietary D2/D3 → activation → local epithelial ligand concentration → receptor-dependent barrier function needs source/form and receiving-tissue reconciliation. Higher junction-gene expression alone is not functional barrier improvement. Whole-body knockout effects may include mineral, developmental and immune effects.

**Disposition.** Retain the existing supply gap; do not relabel VDR biology itself as speculative. If a new experimental hypothesis is desired, narrow it to: “Correction of a defined low-D3 state restores epithelial barrier function through epithelial VDR-dependent signalling under a specified injury context.”

**Possible decisive test.** Adult inducible epithelial-specific VDR deletion versus littermate controls under matched calcium/phosphate and controlled D3 intake, with local ligand assays, permeability flux and junction localisation, plus calcitriol rescue where interpretable. Support: restoration in VDR-intact tissue with target engagement and a genotype interaction. Against: no functional response despite adequate ligand exposure, or an effect independent of epithelial VDR. A human deficiency-correction study is separate and must not deliberately maintain harmful deficiency. This is a proposed protocol, not an admission criterion requiring clinical benefit for Type A provision.

**Ontology/status.** Existing `vitamin-d`; salmon/egg food observations support composition only, not calcitriol delivery or a PM outcome. Destination PM1/BRS5; no necessary cross-BRS source. **“Experimental VDR dependence supported; dietary-form-to-local-ligand supply assessment unresolved.”** No new hypothesis recommended until that reconciliation separates established capacity from a genuinely additional effect claim.

## C5 — Additional creatine-precursor supply and cerebral availability

**Recovered proposition.** PM3-EG-RECOVERY-1, PM3-F1/F7. Arginine, glycine and methionine have existing Derived capacity admissions; SAM remains a separate intracellular participant. The unresolved claim is increased cerebral availability from extra supply, not precursor participation.

**Evidence.** Edison measured human renal GAA exchange and used acute rat infusions; the rat protocol included 200 mM arginine, 100 mM citrulline or 200 mM glycine, 20 minutes at 0.037 mL/min after priming. Arginine/citrulline increased GAA; glycine did not ([primary Methods: amino-acid infusion; Results](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajprenal.00356.2007); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17928413/)). These are not oral human brain exposures. Asiriwardhana's 20 neonatal Yucatan piglets received variable methionine diets after five days on complete diets; the primary abstract specifies 20–220% requirement, 0.32–3.55 g/100 g total amino acids ([Methods/Results abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40998128/)). Hepatic methyl incorporation is the relevant canonical result; full tracer timing and feeding route were not independently verified here. Solis found similar posterior-cingulate total creatine in vegetarians and omnivores despite intake differences ([primary report](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114513003802)); cross-sectional, not a precursor trial.

**Proposed statement.** “In an adult model with adequate background glycine and methionine, additional oral arginine increases labelled precursor contribution to the cerebral creatine pool compared with an isonitrogenous control.” Separate experiments would test low-status correction, glycine and methionine; no mixture result can identify one precursor's effect.

**Decisive test.** Adult rodent dose-ranging oral arginine study with labelled substrate, equalised tracer enrichment or precursor-enrichment correction, pair feeding, brain-region LC–MS isotopologues and total creatine/PCr, plasma/renal/hepatic GAA and creatine, and transport/feedback measurements. Include a direct-creatine comparator. Support requires brain incorporation beyond control with a prespecified pool/turnover result, not simply higher plasma GAA. Against: verified exposure and peripheral response with a precise cerebral null. Distinguish local synthesis from imported newly synthesised creatine; isotope appearance alone does not identify the organ of synthesis. A subsequent controlled human oral trial with validated brain MRS/tracer endpoints tests human relevance separately. Peripheral renal function, methyl allocation and SLC6A8 transport are alternatives/constraints.

**Ontology/status.** `arginine`, `glycine`, `methionine` → existing GAA/SAM/creatine pathway, receiving PM3/BRS4. Eggs, beef and pumpkin seeds have canonical amino-acid observations; their constituent supply is not a cerebral creatine dose. No cross-BRS edge is needed merely because SAM is involved. **“Unestablished additional-precursor effect on cerebral creatine; existing synthesis-capacity admissions retained.”**

## C6 — Dietary PE and local NAPE/NAE pools

**Recovered proposition/gate.** ECS PM1-EG-RECOVERY-1 and pending scope review. Intracellular PE is resolved as `phosphatidylethanolamine`; the NAPE class is recorded as an identity gap. Zinc's capacity admission is independent. No oral PE/NAPE admission follows from these facts.

**Evidence.** Cadas studied brain tissue and embryonic rat cortical neurons, including radiolabelled ethanolamine culture experiments and short drug challenges; it supports endogenous N-arachidonoyl-PE formation, not oral food delivery ([Materials and Methods: neuronal cultures; enzyme/substrate experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6793739/)). Lithander's 18 healthy men consumed acute breakfasts in a four-condition crossover: high/medium/control PE approximately 0.68/0.57/0.04 g, with lunch at3.5h. PE did not improve subjective satiety relative to matched lipid control; lunch energy was higher in the PE conditions ([Table2; Methods; Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2600636/)). NAPE incorporation was not measured. This is a relevant null behavioural result, not proof that no molecular incorporation occurs.

**Proposed statement.** “Oral PE(16:0/18:1) contributes ethanolamine-headgroup atoms to cortical N-arachidonoyl-PE and anandamide in adult rodents under the tested exposure conditions.” PE(16:0/18:1) is a **proposed experimental material**, not a newly verified food observation. Its glycerol-linked acyl chains do not specify the separate N-acyl chain. Do not equate all NAEs with anandamide or infer an intact dietary molecule survives digestion.

**Decisive test.** Ethanolamine-headgroup stable-isotope-labelled PE(16:0/18:1) in a characterised food-compatible matrix in adult rodents, versus matched unlabelled PE and labelled free-headgroup/fatty-acid controls. Time-course targeted lipidomics in gut, plasma and a prespecified brain region; perfuse tissue to minimise vascular contamination. Use labels that distinguish headgroup and acyl contribution, assay endogenous remodelling and appropriate pathway perturbation. Support: molecular-species-resolved incorporation and product formation. Against: adequate systemic absorption but no brain-pool/product labelling within validated detection limits. Gut incorporation alone cannot establish the neural PM claim. A human food experiment would separately establish exposure; appetite is not a substitute for the synthesis endpoint.

**Ontology/status.** PE → specific NAPE → specific NAE, ECS PM1 only. The PE page names eggs, roe, liver and oat bran, but this targeted check did not verify a matching structured PE observation in those named food records; do not label them source-verified neural-precursor foods. The oats record expressly treats the cited oatmeal trial registration as a proposal, not an observed effect. No new food mapping is recommended. Do not use PC/choline as an equivalent PE measurement. **“Unestablished oral phospholipid contribution to the specified neural precursor pool; no dietary efficacy inference.”** The identity gap blocks reliable graph projection, not discussion of an explicit biochemical hypothesis.

## C7 — Dietary lipid status and cue-related dopamine

**Recovered proposition/gate.** ECS PM4-EG-RECOVERY-1/F1 and pending scope review. 2-AG is a biochemical participant with an unresolved canonical identity; there is no independently accepted dietary lipid intervention for this circuit in the inspected record.

**Evidence.** Oleson manipulated CB1/2-AG pharmacologically in rats, measured nucleus-accumbens cue dopamine and reward behaviour, and used local VTA manipulations. JZL184 was tested intravenously at3–10 mg/kg and locally at6 µg/0.5 µL; FAAH inhibition did not reproduce the relevant task effect. Local DMSO itself affected responding, an important vehicle limitation ([Experimental Procedures; Figs2–4,7–8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3269037/)). Lafourcade's developmental n-3 imbalance model concerns receptor coupling/plasticity, not a joined adult dietary-to-cue-dopamine experiment ([primary paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.2736), Results/Methods; full diet formulation and exposure schedule not reverified here).

**Proposed rewrite.** “In adult animals with a defined low n-3 status, correction with a specified lipid formulation changes cue-evoked accumbens dopamine through VTA 2-AG/CB1 signalling.” This remains provisional until the exact nutrient species, background n-6 intake and adult-repletion rationale are appraised. DHA, EPA and ALA are not interchangeable interventions.

**Decisive test.** Adult-onset matched diets, tissue fatty-acid and regional 2-AG measurements, cue-evoked dopamine recording, locomotor/reward controls and regional CB1 perturbation. Support: exposure effect plus loss of the effect under specific mediation perturbation. Against: lipid correction without dopamine change, or dopamine change persisting independently of the proposed mediator. Developmental rescue and adult modulation are distinct. Direct effects on membranes, learning, reward valuation or dopamine metabolism are alternatives.

**Ontology/status.** No food-to-2-AG occurrence edge. A specified canonical fatty acid may eventually supply the exposure: the existing IDs are `dha-docosahexaenoic-acid`, `epa-eicosapentaenoic-acid` and `ala-alpha-linolenic-acid`, not interchangeable aliases. Fish or seed food identities cannot stand for an unspecified “lipid status” intervention. ECS PM4 is the receiving owner; no automatic BRS1 dopamine edge. **“Unestablished exposure-specific dietary modulation of cue dopamine; local endocannabinoid pharmacology does not establish a food effect.”** Distinct from existing genistein/FAAH hypothesis.

## C8 — Dietary substrates and microbial estrogen recycling

**Recovered proposition/gate.** Hormones PM2-EG-RECOVERY-1/F1, pending scope review. Estrogen glucuronides are biochemical substrates with an unresolved class identity. Generic fibre/probiotic supply has not been admitted as selective enzyme control.

**Evidence.** Ervin tested purified microbial glucuronidases and fecal preparations. The coupled assay used30 nM enzyme and500 µM estrone/estradiol glucuronide at37°C for30min with no-enzyme controls; HPLC and inhibition assessed conversion ([Methods: estrogen-processing assays; enzyme-panel results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6901331/)). This is substrate-specific activity, not gene abundance or systemic exposure. Rose's 62 premenopausal women increased estimated fibre from about15 to30g/day with wheat/oat/corn bran over two months. Wheat bran reduced serum estrogens; oat/corn did not show the same result; progesterone/SHBG were unchanged ([primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1652197/)). Detailed allocation, cycle handling and bran processing require full-text confirmation; no microbial mediation can be assigned from these outcomes.

**Proposed rewrite.** “A characterised wheat-bran intervention changes intestinal estrogen-glucuronide deconjugation and subsequent host exposure through a defined microbial glucuronidase activity, independently of its effects on transit and binding.” Direction must be prespecified from enzyme/ex-vivo pilot evidence; do not imply that increased re-exposure is beneficial.

**Decisive test.** Begin with donor fecal cultures, labelled estrogen glucuronide and characterised bran versus substrate control, measuring enzyme-specific activity and liberated product, with selective inhibition and binding-recovery controls. If this establishes a direction, test a defined community in a host model with luminal, portal and systemic isotope kinetics, transit and hormone synthesis/clearance controls. Support: altered labelled re-entry dependent on the microbial enzyme. Against: serum effects without enzyme/labelled-route change, or effects explained by binding/transit. Human menstrual stage and endogenous secretion require separate control.

**Ontology/status.** Host estrogen conjugates are not food constituents in this proposition. No named ordinary food has an established diet → enzyme → systemic chain in this review. Do not substitute wheat grain for the tested bran preparation. Hormones PM2 owns the proposed relationship, with no required cross-BRS edge. **“Unestablished substrate-specific microbial mediation of estrogen re-exposure; known enzyme chemistry retained.”** Define and verify the intervention before registration.

## C9 — Dietary substrates, hydrogen and microbial progestins

**Recovered proposition/gate.** Hormones PM3-EG-RECOVERY-1/F1. The canonical scope review explicitly distinguishes progestin production from progesterone stability; THDOC and H₂ remain identity gaps, and fermentable fibre is not an admitted completed route to the hormonal endpoint.

**Evidence.** McCurry tested microbial corticoid21-dehydroxylation, including E. lenta14A, co-cultured E. coli Nissle and hydrogen perturbation. Experiments used100 µM3α5αTHDOC; culture/co-culture and gas-atmosphere tests ran on different schedules, including48h and72h. EcN alone did not perform the conversion; lowering redox potential alone was insufficient. Pregnant-person fecal differences do not establish systemic exposure or a dietary effect ([Figs2–4; STAR Methods: defined atmosphere and bacterial culture](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11179439/)). **Source discrepancy:** Figure2/text identify G. pamelaeae DSM19738, whereas the resource table lists DSM19743. Resolve stock/genome provenance before using that strain as a precise experimental identity; do not silently “correct” the PM.

**Proposed rewrite.** “A chemically specified fermentable substrate increases THDOC-derived progestin production in a defined producer/cross-feeder community by increasing converter-accessible H₂.” The substrate is currently **not selected by sufficient evidence**; generic fibre is not an acceptable final exposure label. This is a narrow in-vitro hypothesis, not systemic progesterone support.

**Decisive test.** Anaerobic factorial substrate/no-substrate cultures with a verified converter and H₂-producing partner, a matched H₂-deficient partner, and controlled H₂ rescue. Match growth and pH; measure H₂ time-course, substrate utilisation and steroid mass balance with labelled THDOC. Support: substrate-dependent product formation lost on H₂ suppression and restored by rescue. Against: no substrate effect despite consumption, or production independent of H₂ mediation. Hydrogen consumption by other organisms and changes in biomass are alternatives. A later host experiment must separately measure absorption, metabolism and circulating product. Hydrogen water and probiotic food equivalence are not inferred.

**Ontology/status.** Dietary substrate → microbial H₂ resource → host-steroid conversion in Hormones PM3. THDOC is host-derived; food is not supplying the steroid. Keep source class, resource and product distinct; no ordinary-food association is currently justified for this complete route. **“Unestablished dietary control of a demonstrated microbial reaction; systemic progestin exposure and progesterone stability not established.”** Narrow before registration; preserve the existing gate.

## C10 — Microbial androgen deconjugation and systemic re-entry

**Recovered proposition/gate.** Hormones PM6-EG-RECOVERY-1/F1. Androgen glucuronides are biochemical substrates, not a dietary admission. The gate requires deconjugation, uptake and disposition to be distinguished; reverse hormone-to-microbiota observations do not resolve direction.

**Evidence.** Colldén compared8-week germ-free and conventional C57BL/6 mice and measured free/conjugated steroids by GC–MS/MS; eight young men supplied serum/stool. This was not a food intervention. Luminal free DHT depended strongly on microbiota. Crucially, serum testosterone/DHT did not differ between mouse groups, while liver and seminal-vesicle DHT were higher in germ-free mice; endocrine compensation was discussed ([Methods: animals/clinical samples; Figs5–10 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6962501/)). Thus high fecal DHT is not proof of systemic microbial re-exposure. The canonical Leão hormone-manipulation finding is reverse-direction context; its full protocol was not independently verified and is not counted as support for re-entry.

**Proposed statement.** “In a defined host–microbiota model, microbial deglucuronidation of a specified intestinal androgen conjugate contributes a measurable fraction of circulating labelled unconjugated androgen.” A contribution need not increase steady-state serum concentration because clearance and feedback can compensate.

**Decisive test.** Deliver a trace dose of isotopically labelled androgen glucuronide to the intestinal compartment in matched gnotobiotic hosts with a deconjugating strain, an enzyme-deficient counterpart and complementation/rescue. Measure luminal substrate/product, portal and peripheral isotope kinetics, tissue distribution and clearance; exclude free-steroid contamination and quantify nonmicrobial hydrolysis.
