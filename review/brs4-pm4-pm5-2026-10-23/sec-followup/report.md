## PM4-F2 — completed with model boundaries

Bell et al. (2007) full primary XML was recovered and appraised. Figures 1–8 include control RNAi, respiratory-defective cybrids, preserved DMOG responses, Qo/Qi inhibitor comparisons and peroxide/catalase controls. Quantitative experiments report independent repeats and SEM. This supports the existing tested-cell proposition; probe specificity, model dependence and contrasting rho0-cell reports prevent stronger factors. Guzy's full original paper remains access-limited; Bell supplies the pivotal complete primary appraisal. The earlier blanket requirement to recover every Guzy method is therefore no longer an essential scoring blocker.

Srinivas et al. (2001) and Vaux et al. (2001) report preserved hypoxic HIF responses in respiratory-chain-defective cells. They remain explicit contrary evidence against a universal necessity claim. Distinct groups are not automatically given full replication credit where models and assays overlap or credible disagreement remains. Raw and adjusted SEC are both 5/8; no cap is triggered.

## PM5-F2 — completed for protective chemistry, not dietary delivery

Additional original indexed Forsmark Results, Table I and descriptions of Figures 1–3 establish the extraction/reconstitution contrasts and HPLC verification that tocopherol was not detectable in the extracted particles. These qualitative controls distinguish protective chemistry from its absence, although batch-level precision, dispersion and detection limits remain incomplete. That supports P1, not P2: lack of a clinical-style interval does not automatically make a qualitative biochemical contrast unassessable.

Independent Yamamoto et al. (1990) and Frei et al. (1990) experiments provide partial confirmation in defined lipid models. They are not independent repetitions of the mitochondrial-particle protocol. Kagan et al. (1990), which emphasizes tocopherol recycling, remains a mechanistic counterpoint; direct scavenging and recycling can depend on the preparation. None of this establishes oral CoQ10 delivery, food effectiveness or a clinical outcome. Raw and adjusted SEC are both 5/8.

Access qualification: Forsmark original complete PDF was not downloaded; primary indexed Methods/Results/Table passages were recovered. Frei's primary Introduction/Methods and primary abstract were appraised. Scite labelled a Yamamoto body response fulltext, but it contained only a 683-character proof note: it was not treated as full Methods/Results. The Yamamoto primary abstract supports only its stated model comparison.

## PM4-F5 — precision remains unresolved; wording concern for review

Herbst Figure 5 (not Figure 6) reports ROS emission alongside 4HNE/protein-carbonyl measurements in whole-muscle homogenates. These damage measurements are not isolated mitochondrial membrane assays. The publication reports means/SEM and paired testing; the paired change estimates, covariance and a defensible no-increase/equivalence boundary were not recovered. A nonsignificant change does not establish absence of damage.

The existing phrase “without increasing the measured oxidative-damage products” could be read as a descriptive statistical result or a stronger absence claim. Recommended correction for review: **“with no statistically detected increase in the measured whole-muscle 4HNE and protein-carbonyl markers.”** This proposal has not been implemented in the canonical Finding. Obtain paired data/uncertainty or resolve the intended claim before assigning P; do not fabricate zero, a default one or an equivalence margin.
