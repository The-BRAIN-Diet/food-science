BRS4-FM4-PM9 — Mitochondrial Biogenesis

4. Mechanistic Basis

Scientific Hypotheses

Hypothesis-1 — Glucose Appearance and Mitochondrial Biogenesis Unestablished

Scientific hypothesis. Scientific admission is required before any proposed mechanism relationship can be approved, including when a hypothesis is marked Supported.

Proposition: Postprandial glucose-appearance kinetics may influence mitochondrial biogenesis signalling.

Proposed direction: BRS6-FM1-PM1 → BRS4-FM4-PM9

Rationale: Nutrient availability and metabolic signalling can influence mitochondrial adaptation pathways. Whether the rate of post-meal glucose appearance itself influences this signalling remains a testable question.

Supporting Findings

None identified as directly testing this proposition.

Contradictory or null Findings

None identified as directly testing this proposition.

Contextual Findings
  • BRS4-FM4-PM9 / SF1 — Interval training increases muscle mitochondrial-content markers — The training study records muscle mitochondrial-content and regulatory markers, with unchanged total PGC-1alpha. It provides exercise/endpoint context, not a test of glucose-appearance mediation.
  • BRS6-FM1-PM1 / SF4 — Defined fibre preparations affect appearance differently — Defined fibre studies measured changes in glucose appearance, including a null primary endpoint in one preparation. They did not measure mitochondrial biogenesis; this is measurement context, not direct support or contradiction of the hypothesis.
Source evidence and its role
  • Hill et al. (2013) — Carbohydrate and whey protein co-ingestion (contextual) — The carbohydrate–whey exercise study reported altered post-exercise PGC-1alpha mRNA expression. The study did not establish measured glucose-appearance kinetics as the mediator or directly demonstrate mitochondrial formation.

References to the same study or cohort are not independent evidence. Finding scores are not inherited.

Principal limitations
  • Measured postprandial glucose appearance was not established as the mediator.
  • Acute PGC-1alpha transcription is not direct evidence of mitochondrial formation.
  • No causal dietary-to-brain or ADHD outcome is established.
  • The linked Findings address different studies and endpoints; their combination does not establish the proposed causal chain.
Alternative or interacting explanations
  • Exercise stimulus and training history.
  • Insulin responses, glycogen availability and total energy availability.
  • Protein/amino-acid exposure and nutrient composition independent of glucose-appearance rate.
Evidence required
  • Measure postprandial glucose-appearance kinetics using an appropriate tracer approach and prespecified rate/time-course endpoints.
  • Link those measured kinetics to mitochondrial-biogenesis signalling and longitudinal mitochondrial-content/formation endpoints, with tissue and assay limitations explicit.
  • Control or separately assess exercise, insulin, glycogen, protein exposure and energy availability; test mediation rather than equating carbohydrate exposure with glucose appearance.
  • Replicate in the relevant population; brain or ADHD claims would require their own direct evidence.
