# Manuscript-ready dietary synthesis

The current mechanism-based abstraction supports a candidate BRAIN Diet built around varied plant foods, appropriately qualified protein and choline sources, and foods supplying long-chain omega-3 fatty acids. It provides a traceable rationale for food selection; it does not establish the clinical efficacy of the complete pattern or an ADHD treatment. The retained relationships are drawn from five BRSs, while independently assessed meal practices contribute BRS6 coverage; peripheral meal entrainment is retained separately as Cross-BRS evidence.

Established biochemical relationships support maintaining substrate and cofactor supply rather than assuming that more intake improves function. Leafy vegetables and legumes recur as folate sources; eggs and soy foods as choline sources; beetroot, spinach, quinoa and wheat germ as betaine sources; nuts and seeds as vitamin E or copper sources; and fish and seafood as EPA/DHA, B12 or other nutrient sources. Vitamin B6 and riboflavin require activation before their coenzyme roles. These are composition and functional-capacity relationships, with absorption, status and tissue delivery limitations. Food-composition presence alone does not establish an effective serving, additional-intake benefit or a clinical outcome. The biochemical scope is supported by the source-specific records in the accompanying roll-up; for example, [NAD pathways](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7963035/) remain distinct from [pharmacological niacin treatment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32386566/).

Microbial substrate diversity is a defensible candidate principle. The retained resistant-starch class has preparation-specific source records for green/unripe bananas, cooked-and-cooled potato material, black beans and chickpeas. Apple pectin remains a research-supported provisional source. Starch structure, ripeness, processing, degraders and cross-feeding partners affect fermentation. These foods have not been shown to reproduce Baxter’s extracted potato-RS2 butyrate response; purified citrus-pectin cultures do not establish a whole-apple intervention effect. Inulin remains an unadmitted candidate in the controlled PM5 assessment. This distinction is preserved in the approved PM5 source assessments and manuscript records.

Preparation and meal structure warrant separate treatment from biochemical inputs. A [two-week crossover trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7372063/) supports less-milled whole-grain foods over a finely milled, nutrient-matched package for selected glycaemic measures in adults with type 2 diabetes. Smaller acute studies support the tested fish-before-rice sequence and beta-glucan meal formulation, without validating all protein-first meals, whole oats or chronic brain outcomes. Broccoli-sprout findings concern a defined myrosinase-active homogenate and airway gene expression; [a COPD capsule trial was null](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27832073/). Ordinary broccoli preparations must not inherit those formulation effects.

Timing remains context dependent. [Bruno’s short prediabetes crossover](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39759025/) supports an early, calorie-front-loaded eating protocol for selected glucose-variability outcomes; [a different longer calorie-restriction trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38264001/) did not show added average-glucose or insulin-sensitivity benefit. [Meal-delay experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28578930/) demonstrate peripheral phase changes, not an optimal meal schedule or brain benefit. Supervised interval walking is retained separately as a lifestyle lever.

Walnuts supply a verified ellagitannin precursor relationship, while the assayed commercial pomegranate-juice relationships remain provisional. Neither food is a direct urolithin A source or a predictable urolithin exposure; [human conversion varies](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41430-021-00950-1). Clinical testing is needed for claims that combining these foods improves cognition, ADHD symptoms, adipose regulation or the full BRAIN Diet’s intended outcomes. BRS6-PM8 contributes no approved dietary claim while its mission remains under review.
