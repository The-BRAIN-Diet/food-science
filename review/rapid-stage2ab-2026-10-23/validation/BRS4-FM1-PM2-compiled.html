BRS4-1-2 - NAD⁺ Metabolism

(Keeping Cellular Energy Chemistry Charged)

1. Mission & Overview + Dietary Levers

Mission

Maintain NAD⁺ availability so redox reactions and mitochondrial signalling stay adequately powered.

Intervention Dominance: Diet-Supported

  • Nicotinamide (a form of vitamin B3)
  • Nicotinic acid (a form of vitamin B3)
  • Tryptophan

No evidence-supported cofactors or substrates are currently established for this mechanism.

No mapping established.

Overview

NAD⁺ helps cells release energy from nutrients and supports enzymes involved in maintenance and repair. Cells must continually recycle and replenish this molecule to keep those functions available. This PM focuses on maintaining that cellular resource. Dietary vitamin B3 forms and tryptophan provide building material, while exercise can influence part of the recycling machinery. Supporting normal availability is different from assuming that raising NAD⁺ improves cognition or wellbeing. [1] [3] [6]

  • Benefits: Maintaining NAD⁺ supports energy metabolism and cellular repair—functions needed for healthy tissue function. This explains the value of the mechanism, rather than demonstrating a benefit from extra intake. [1] [3]
  • Implementation Notes: The dietary disclosures identify the specific vitamin B3 forms and tryptophan that supply this pool. Aerobic or resistance training increased a recycling enzyme in human muscle; high-dose niacin evidence comes from mitochondrial disease and does not establish a routine supplement regimen. [1] [2] [6]
  • Biological Relevance: This resource connects mitochondrial energy metabolism with cellular maintenance and repair. It also interacts with biological timing mechanisms, linking energy regulation with the body’s daily rhythms. [1] [3] [4]

4. Mechanistic Basis

Summary

NAD⁺ accepts electrons to form NADH, which can be oxidised back to NAD⁺ [Covarrubias et al. (2021) [1]]. Separately, synthesis and salvage replenish the pool when consuming enzymes break the molecule down [Chang and Guarente (2014) [3]]. Nicotinamide salvage, the nicotinic-acid route and hepatic tryptophan conversion are major pathways [Covarrubias et al. (2021) [1]]. In mice, the clock controls the salvage enzyme NAMPT [Ramsey et al. (2009) [4]]. High-dose niacin raised NAD⁺ in mitochondrial myopathy [Pirinen et al. (2020) [2]]. Training raised the muscle enzyme, not measured NAD⁺ [de Guia et al. (2019) [6]].

Mechanism and measurement boundaries

This PM includes NAD⁺ replenishment, redox use and enzymatic consumption. Electron transport and the creatine buffer are distinct mechanisms. [1]

The precursor pathways are supported by reviewed mechanistic evidence. The high-dose niacin study involved five adults with mitochondrial myopathy; exercise measured muscle NAMPT protein, not NAD⁺ concentration or brain function. These endpoints do not establish improved cognitive energy or recovery. [1] [2] [6]

4.1 Scientific Findings (5)

Summary

NAD⁺ and NADH interconvert in dehydrogenase reactions. Separately, synthesis and salvage replenish the pool when consuming enzymes break NAD⁺ down. Nicotinamide salvage, the nicotinic-acid route and hepatic tryptophan conversion are major pathways, not every precursor entry. The findings separate that chemistry from a high-dose niacin study in mitochondrial myopathy, from a muscle-enzyme response to training, and from the absence of an NAD⁺ measurement in the ADHD papers.

NAD⁺ accepts electrons to form NADH, and that NADH can be oxidised back to NAD⁺. Separately, nicotinamide salvage, the nicotinic-acid route and hepatic conversion of tryptophan are major pathways that replenish the pool when consuming enzymes break NAD⁺ down.

What this means

These are major maintenance pathways, not every precursor entry. Identifying them does not show that eating more of a precursor, once intake is adequate, raises NAD⁺ or improves attention.

Evidence confidence: Not yet scored

Finding ID: PM2-F1

Finding Statement: Major pathways that maintain the mammalian NAD⁺ pool are de novo synthesis from tryptophan, the Preiss–Handler route from nicotinic acid, and salvage of nicotinamide. Most cells outside the liver depend on salvage. These are not the only precursor entry routes.

Synthesised Evidence Confidence: Not yet scored

Synthesis: The review describes NAD⁺ as the coenzyme that accepts a hydride in redox reactions. It is synthesised de novo from tryptophan through the kynurenine pathway, from nicotinic acid by the Preiss–Handler pathway, and by salvage of nicotinamide. Outside the liver, most cells do not express the full tryptophan route. The liver can convert tryptophan to nicotinamide, release it, and leave other cells to salvage that nicotinamide. The review states that the share of the pool coming from tryptophan is not established. Most NAD⁺ is described as coming from nicotinamide salvage.

Synthesis Limitations: This is a review of pathways, not a human feeding trial. It does not measure how much an ordinary meal changes NAD⁺, and it does not test attention or cognitive energy. Nicotinamide riboside and nicotinamide mononucleotide, as supplement doses, are outside this dietary assessment.

Finding ID: PM2-F3

Finding Statement: Circadian clock factors control NAMPT-mediated NAD⁺ biosynthesis in mice and cells, so NAD⁺ availability oscillates across the day in those systems.

Synthesised Evidence Confidence: Not yet scored

Synthesis: One study reports a feedback cycle in which clock transcription factors control NAMPT and thereby NAD⁺ biosynthesis. A companion study reports that the NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase SIRT1 modulates CLOCK-dependent chromatin control, with NAD⁺ itself changing across the day. Both place the salvage enzyme inside a circadian loop. Neither assigns a bedtime or a meal time.

Synthesis Limitations: The experiments are in mice and cells. They do not show that a regular sleep schedule, or restriction of sleep, changes NAD⁺ in people.

Evidence confidence: Not yet scored

Finding ID: PM2-F4

Finding Statement: In adults with mitochondrial myopathy, escalating niacin to 750–1,000 mg/day increased blood NAD⁺ and restored muscle NAD⁺ to the matched-control level, without a cognitive endpoint.

Synthesised Evidence Confidence: Not yet scored

Synthesis: Five adults with adult-onset mitochondrial myopathy and muscle mitochondrial DNA deletions, plus matched controls, received slow-release niacin. The dose started at 250 mg/day and rose to 750–1,000 mg/day. Patients were followed for up to 10 months and controls for 4 months. Blood NAD⁺ increased in the participants who continued, by up to 8-fold, and muscle NAD⁺ in the patients reached the control level. Muscle strength increased. Flushing occurred above 500 mg/day. Two controls stopped because of gastrointestinal irritation.

Synthesis Limitations: The sample is five patients. The dose is far above ordinary niacin intake. There is no untreated comparison group, and attention, cognitive energy and recovery were not endpoints. The result does not transfer to people whose intake is already adequate.

Evidence Considered:

  • Study
    Open niacin supplementation in adults with mitochondrial myopathy and in matched controls.

    Result
    Blood NAD⁺ rose by up to 8-fold. Muscle NAD⁺ in patients reached the control level. Muscle strength increased. Cognition was not tested.
    Effect / Magnitude

Supports Lifestyle Lever: Aerobic or resistance training

Twelve weeks of aerobic or resistance training increased NAMPT protein in skeletal muscle of young and older adults. The study did not measure NAD⁺ concentration.

What this means

Training changed the amount of the salvage enzyme in muscle. That is not a measurement of the NAD⁺ pool, and it is not a cognitive result.

Evidence confidence: Not yet scored

Finding ID: PM2-F5

Finding Statement: Aerobic and resistance training increased skeletal-muscle NAMPT protein in young and older adults, without a reported change in NAD⁺ concentration.

Synthesised Evidence Confidence: Not yet scored

Synthesis: NAMPT protein in skeletal muscle was lower at older ages across 57 people. Twelve weeks of aerobic training raised muscle NAMPT by about 12% in adults aged 35 or under and by about 28% in adults aged 55 or over. Resistance training raised it by about 25% and 30% in those same age groups. NRK2 and NMNAT1 and NMNAT3 protein did not change. In a separate group, six weeks of high-intensity interval training did not change NAMPT in abdominal fat.

Synthesis Limitations: The endpoint is enzyme protein, not NAD⁺. Muscle NAMPT is not brain NAD⁺. Cognitive energy and recovery were not measured.

Evidence Considered:

  • Study
    Twelve-week aerobic and resistance training, with NAMPT and related enzymes measured in muscle biopsies.
    Population
    Adults aged 35 or under and adults aged 55 or over. A separate group provided abdominal fat biopsies.
    Result
    Muscle NAMPT rose after both training modes. Other salvage enzymes in muscle did not. Fat NAMPT did not change after interval training.
    Effect / Magnitude
    About 12–28% after aerobic training and about 25–30% after resistance training, larger in the older groups.
    Evidence Summary
    Training increases the amount of the rate-limiting salvage enzyme in human muscle.
    Limitations
    NAMPT protein is not NAD⁺ concentration and not a cognitive endpoint.

5.2 Cross-BRS Mechanism Relationships

  • BRS-X(Circadian-FM1) — Light and Feeding Zeitgeber Entrainment — molecular clocks regulate NAMPT-dependent NAD⁺ biosynthesis in mice and cells (Ramsey et al., 2009). This supports clock–metabolic feedback, without establishing that a light or meal schedule changes human NAD⁺ availability. NAD⁺ metabolism retains its BRS4 owner.

Primary Mechanisms in other Biological Regulatory Systems that directly interact with, constrain or support this mechanism.

  • BRS2-FM1-PM4 - Methionine Cycle Flux — One-carbon reactions use redox cofactors. No assessed study shows that this turnover changes how much NAD⁺ cells hold.
  • BRS6-FM1-PM2 - Glycaemic Variability Regulation — Carbohydrate oxidation reduces NAD⁺ to NADH. Control of glucose swings is a separate process, and no assessed study shows that those swings change NAD⁺ availability.

5.3 Local BRS Mechanism Relationships

Related Primary Mechanisms within the same Biological Regulatory System that collectively support the integrated biological function.

  • BRS4-FM1-PM1 - Electron Transport Chain Function — NADH made from this pool is what complex I oxidises. The respiratory complexes are a separate mechanism, and niacin precursors belong with this supply.
  • BRS4-FM1-PM3 - Creatine–Phosphocreatine Energy Buffering — Oxidative energy production can supply ATP that this buffer stores and releases. No assessed study shows a serial path from this NAD⁺ pool through the respiratory complexes into that buffer.
