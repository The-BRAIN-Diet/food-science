  • Broccoli — Preparation-dependent — not ranked · Sous vide at 100 °C for 20 minutes, with and without 1 g brown mustard powder
    Source qualifications

    UK mature broccoli, sous vide at 100 °C for 20 minutes, with and without 1 g brown mustard powder Sulforaphane forms from glucoraphanin when myrosinase is active. In one cooked-broccoli study the amount depended on preparation, and it is not entered as a per-100 g nutrient. Okunade, Niranjan, Ghawi, Kuhnle and Methven (2018), Molecular Nutrition & Food Research 62:1700980. Formation identity also rests on Fahey et al. (1997). One UK broccoli lot, cooked sous vide at 100 °C for 20 minutes, formed 1.06 µmol sulforaphane per g dry weight. Adding 1 g brown mustard powder raised that to 8.58 µmol/g dry weight. Twelve adults who ate 200 g excreted 9.8 µmol sulforaphane-NAC per g creatinine in 24 hours, and 44.7 µmol/g creatinine with the mustard. These figures are not a sulforaphane amount for every 100 g of broccoli. UK mature broccoli after sous-vide cooking, with and without mustard myrosinase Okunade et al. (2018), Molecular Nutrition & Food Research 62:1700980, Table 1 and the 24-hour urine result. Table 1, µmol sulforaphane per g dry weight: raw 2.05 ± 0.25; sous vide 100 °C for 20 minutes 1.06 ± 0.18; the same cooked broccoli plus 1 g brown mustard powder 8.58 ± 0.33. 8.58 / 1.06 = 8.1, which the paper reports as an 8-fold increase. One results sentence prints the mustard result as 8.58 ± 0.13; the table figure is used here. Urine, 12 adults, 200 g of that cooked broccoli: 9.8 ± 5.1 µmol sulforaphane-NAC per g creatinine in 24 hours without mustard, and 44.7 ± 33.9 with 1 g mustard. 44.7 / 9.8 = 4.6 from the published means. The paper's average of each person's increase is 4.7-fold, with individual increases from 1.7-fold to 10-fold. Sous-vide myrosinase loss in the same paper: 40% after 2 minutes, 90% after 6 minutes, and no measurable activity after 8 minutes. The study did not report a glucoraphanin concentration, and the dry-weight results are not converted to a fresh 100 g amount. Fahey et al. (1997) identifies sulforaphane as the myrosinase product of glucoraphanin; it is not the source of these figures. · #food-source-4 · #food-source-4
