# PM7 SEC appraisal and placement

19 November 2026. Canonical operation: “Tune downstream cytokine signalling intensity so inflammatory tone does not remain chronically elevated.”

## 1. Capó et al. (2016)

**Exact proposition.** In male soccer players, eight weeks of an almond-based beverage supplying 1.16 g DHA/day, compared with a similar unenriched beverage, attenuated selected ex vivo cytokine-production responses to LPS in peripheral blood mononuclear cells collected two hours after acute exercise.

The primary abstract reports 15 randomized participants and erythrocyte DHA rising from 34 ± 3.6 to 43 ± 3.6 nmol per billion erythrocytes in the intervention arm only. It reports that DHA attenuated the exercise-associated cytokine-production response. Its numerical IL‑1β example is a temperature contrast and cannot supply the exercise-by-treatment contrast. The abstract identifies LPS stimulation of peripheral blood mononuclear cells and the two-hour post-exercise draw. Full assay conditions, per-marker between-group estimates and uncertainty, allocation procedures, masking and outcome analysis remain unverified. Related Capó reports cannot resolve the exact-assay gaps or establish independent replication. [Primary paper and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13105-016-0490-8)

| SEC factor | Score | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| Directness | 2 | The intervention and ex vivo endpoint match the assessed proposition. |
| Bias protection | Pending | Random assignment is reported. Enough detail to judge allocation, concealment, masking and selective reporting could not be verified. |
| Precision | Pending | The exercise-by-treatment estimates and uncertainty were unavailable. |
| Replication | 0 | One verified dataset tests the exact protocol and endpoint. |

**Disposition.** No total. Retain Scientific Finding PM7-F2 and record the assessment gap. Oral exposure and cellular uptake were documented. The ex vivo response leaves engagement of PM7’s in vivo circulating-cytokine operation unresolved. Withdraw the 26 October Type B admission for DHA from the active §3.1.1 list. The earlier decision remains in Git and the dated dietary-recovery records.

## 2. Tan et al. (2018)

**Exact proposition.** In adults with chronic venous leg ulcers, eight weeks of a defined 2.5 g/day EPA+DHA blend (approximately 1.5 g EPA and 1.0 g DHA) lowered fasting plasma IL‑6, IL‑1β and TNF‑α at weeks 4 and 8 compared with placebo, with both arms taking 81 mg/day aspirin.

The randomized, double-blind pilot used a computer-generated allocation scheme administered by someone outside the study. Five matched softgels daily were taken with the evening meal. Controls received 5.2 g/day light mineral oil. Fasting cytokines were measured by ELISA. The analysis included 35 of 40 participants, 16 of 21 in the intervention arm and 19 of 19 controls. Plasma EPA and DHA rose relative to control, supporting exposure to both fatty acids. The methods describe approximately 1.5 g EPA and 1.0 g DHA daily, but the independent analysis in Table 1 lists 324.67 mg EPA and 47.05 mg DHA per capsule, equivalent to about 1.623 g EPA and 0.235 g DHA daily at five capsules. The same table labels a 217.78 mg row C22:6n3 while naming it n‑6 docosapentaenoic acid. The manuscript does not resolve the identity or dose discrepancy. The figures above are as printed, with no assumed interchange of table rows. [Primary author manuscript, §§2.1–2.6 and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5941937/)

**Intervention composition.** The protocol described a nominal combined EPA+DHA dose of 2.5 g/day (approximately 1.5 g EPA and 1.0 g DHA). Table 1 of Tan et al. (2018) appears to mislabel the DHA and DPA rows. Table 2 of the 2021 secondary analysis of the same trial identifies 324.67 mg EPA, 217.78 mg DHA and 47.05 mg n‑3 DPA per capsule. At five capsules daily, the analysed blend provided approximately 1.62 g EPA and 1.09 g DHA, or 2.71 g/day combined EPA+DHA, plus 0.24 g/day n‑3 DPA. This reconciliation is based on the companion publication. Both reports draw on the same trial. [2021 secondary analysis, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9022442/)

All three cytokines started higher in the intervention arm. Section 3.3 reports the p values for baseline-adjusted follow-up comparisons. Table 3 gives between-arm differences in change and standard errors, which are a different estimand. Numerical point estimates and intervals for the adjusted follow-up comparisons were not separately identified in the manuscript. The paper’s percentage changes are within-arm summaries. The small sample, differential attrition, baseline imbalance, unreported multiplicity correction and eight-week follow-up limit inference. The aspirin background and population delimit the result. Receptor-level signalling, anti-inflammatory cytokines, longer-term effects and clinical benefit are outside the measured endpoint. [Primary author manuscript, §§3.3–4 and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5941937/)

## §3.2 admission reassessment

Admitted the analysed Tan protocol as a qualified PM7 SOP for the exact plasma cytokine endpoint and population, with shared aspirin in both arms. The rationale follows the Wastyk precedent: a directly relevant, measured inflammatory-marker result can support a study-specific SOP while mechanism and clinical benefit remain unresolved. Tan provides baseline-adjusted between-arm comparisons, whereas Wastyk's admitted marker result was exploratory and within-arm. Tan's small sample, differential attrition, baseline imbalance and limited effect-size reporting constrain the scope. The provisional 4/8 is pinned to the Tan intervention and does not establish comparative dominance. The companion analysis supports composition within the same trial. The earlier nominal-dose disposition is historical.
