Dietary Levers
Vitamin B6 → PLP-dependent GABA-synthesis capacity

Input = Vitamin B6

Input type = cofactor precursor

Biological role = Provides dietary B6 vitamers that pyridoxal kinase converts to PLP, the cofactor required for GAD-dependent GABA synthesis

Evidence source = [1] [2] [3] [4]

Limitation = Dietary B6 provision of PLP precursors is supported, but increasing B6 intake has not been shown to increase human brain GABA synthesis; PLP supports many enzymes and is not GABA-specific.

Dietary input = Yes — Accepted dietary provision or constituent supply relationship; no efficacy or quantity claim added.

Dietary Confidence: High — Dietary B6 supplies precursors of the PLP cofactor required by GAD. This is a capacity relationship, not evidence that extra B6 increases brain GABA.

Relevant Findings: BRS1-FM4-PM9 / PM9-F1; BRS1-FM4-PM9 / PM9-F2

Evidence & Audit

Assessment ID: DC-BRS1-FM4-PM9-PM9-DIT-3

Assessed proposition: Dietary vitamin B6 supplies precursors that sustain PLP-dependent glutamate-decarboxylase capacity for GABA synthesis.

Dietary Confidence: High

Relationship: Type A — Capacity; derived

Evidence rationale: High is justified for the defined capacity proposition by convergent GAD enzymology, human B6 activation enzymology and dietary utilisation evidence. Martin and Rimvall is secondary synthesis, not an independent experiment. Human PDXK and PNPO experiments distinguish phosphorylation from oxidation; primary nutritional-deficiency evidence supports cofactor-dependent brain GAD capacity. Missing trials of extra intake, neuronal PLP occupancy or ADHD outcomes do not weaken this narrower established dependency. Plant glucoside bioavailability and pathological activation defects limit supply in specified contexts rather than negate the relationship.

Evidence coherence: Not separately graded; see the recorded evidence rationale above.

Engagement, dietary delivery and supporting sources

PM-specific capacity (Type A): PLP-dependent GAD catalysis is established; dietary deficiency can reduce rat-brain GAD activity through cofactor depletion. The proposition is capacity, not increased synthesis at sufficient intake.

Recorded support: supported

  • https://www.nature.com/articles/nsmb1228
  • https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1471-4159.1972.tb01374.x

Dietary delivery: Human tracer studies establish utilisation of oral B6, including incomplete availability of PN-glucoside. PDXK and human PNPO experiments support vitamer-specific conversion to PLP; this does not measure neuronal cofactor occupancy.

Recorded support: supported

Scope and confidence limitations

Assessed scope: Biochemical capacity under functioning B6 absorption, activation and cofactor handling; not quantitative neuronal occupancy, increased GABA from extra intake, or clinical efficacy.

  • Pyridoxal is phosphorylated to PLP; pyridoxine and pyridoxamine are phosphorylated to PNP/PMP and require PNPO-dependent oxidation. This clarification does not edit the preserved historical atom.
  • Food matrices and glycosylated vitamers differ in availability; normal activation cannot be assumed in PNPO/PDXK disorders.
  • No predictable brain GABA response, dose recommendation, benefit above adequacy or ADHD efficacy is established; PLP is not GABA-specific.

Minced beef → ↑ dietary phenylalanine appearance
Mechanism: Physical disruption of the food matrix

Intervention = Minced rather than intact beef in the tested older-adult meal

Intervention type = food preparation/delivery practice

Biological relationship =
Mechanism of Action: Mincing physically disrupts the beef matrix and may facilitate digestive release and absorption. The preparation comparison supports a texture-related effect, but does not isolate this process as the causal mediator of the measured phenylalanine appearance. Evidence status: Mechanistic interpretation; mediator not isolated
Measured Effect: The tested texture change increased six-hour appearance of dietary phenylalanine in ten older men. See phenylalanine.
PM Relevance: BRS1-FM1-PM1 — An upstream preparation effect relevant to circulating amino-acid provision; not evidence of brain precursor delivery.

Evidence source = [22]
Study Context: 135 g beef; ten older men; controlled crossover; six-hour dietary phenylalanine appearance.

Limitations =

  • Population: Ten older men, one 135 g beef meal.
  • Endpoint: Dietary phenylalanine appearance, not brain delivery.
  • Null outcomes: No detectable difference in total plasma essential amino acids or muscle protein synthesis.

Boiled eggs → ↑ peak circulating essential amino acids
Mechanism: Heat-induced protein denaturation

Intervention = Boiled rather than raw eggs in the tested postexercise meal

Intervention type = food preparation/delivery practice

Biological relationship =
Mechanism of Action: Heating can denature egg proteins and alter their digestive accessibility. A separate ileal study supports improved digestibility of cooked egg protein. Denaturation itself was not isolated as the mediator of the postexercise circulating amino-acid response. Evidence status: Mechanistic interpretation; mediator not isolated
Measured Effect: Five boiled eggs produced higher peak circulating essential-amino-acid concentrations than five raw eggs after exercise. Separately, the ileostomy study measured greater true ileal digestibility of cooked egg protein.
PM Relevance: BRS1-FM1-PM1 — An upstream preparation effect relevant to circulating amino-acid provision; not evidence of brain precursor delivery.

Evidence source = [24] [25]
Study Context: Five boiled versus five raw eggs after resistance exercise; 45 trained young men. Separate digestibility study: five ileostomy patients.

Limitations =

  • Population: 45 healthy resistance-trained young men; five eggs after exercise.
  • Endpoint: Plasma concentration is not dietary appearance flux or neuronal availability.
  • Separate study: Ileal digestibility was measured in five ileostomy patients, not the postexercise cohort.
  • Translation: No daily five-egg prescription or demonstrated muscle-protein-synthesis advantage.

Intervention admitted = Yes — existing canonical SOP record

Lifestyle Levers
Post-meal walking → ↓ postprandial glucose exposure
Mechanism: Activity-associated muscle glucose uptake

Intervention = Advice to walk for 10 minutes after each main meal

Intervention type = physical activity

Biological relationship =
Mechanism of Action: Post-meal walking places muscle activity in the postprandial period. Increased muscle glucose uptake is a proposed rationale; the cited trial did not measure tissue-specific uptake or establish this as the mediator of lower glucose exposure. Evidence status: Proposed rationale; not measured in this trial
Measured Effect: Lower three-hour postprandial glucose incremental area under the curve versus advice to walk 30 minutes daily without specified timing.
PM Relevance: BRS6-FM1-PM2 — Related to postprandial glucose handling; does not establish reduced glycaemic variability.

Evidence source = [20]
Study Context: Two-week crossover periods; 41 adults with type 2 diabetes; 10 minutes after each main meal versus 30 minutes daily without specified timing.

Limitations =

  • Endpoint: Glucose exposure is not a variability endpoint.
  • Exposure: Advice and achieved activity differ; overall activity was greater in the post-meal condition.
  • Generalisability: Short study in adults with type 2 diabetes; not evidence of brain benefit.

Intervention admitted = Unset — no matching admitted post-meal walking record

Intervention confidence: not assigned — rubric pending adjudication.
