3. Intervention Levers

3.2 System Optimisation Practices
Tested fibre/fruit-rich meal → no observed postprandial endotoxin/LBP rise

Intervention = Tested fibre/fruit-rich mixed meal versus the high-fat/high-carbohydrate comparison

Intervention type = defined meal formulation — Dietary Protocols

Biological relationship =
Mechanism of Action: MoA unresolved. Meal composition and gut-derived inflammatory exposure provide hypotheses, but the comparison did not isolate causal meal components or demonstrate intestinal absorption as its mechanism. Evidence status: Mechanism not isolated.
Measured Effect: The tested fibre/fruit-rich meal did not produce the postprandial LAL/LBP increase seen after the comparison meal. NF-kB DNA-binding activity rose after the tested high-fat/high-carbohydrate meal but not after the isocaloric fibre/fruit-rich comparison.
PM Relevance: BRS3-FM1-PM2 — circulating endotoxin-associated readouts relate to the PM’s inflammatory-load boundary; they do not establish the source or route of endotoxin entry.

Evidence source = [15] — Ghanim et al. (2009)
Study context: 910-kcal mixed meals: oatmeal/milk/orange juice/raisins/peanut butter/English muffin versus egg/sausage muffins and hash browns. Two parallel groups of ten healthy lean adults.

Limitations =

  • Study design: Small parallel-group acute comparison: two groups of ten healthy lean adults.
  • Attribution: Multiple ingredients and macronutrients differed; no isolated fibre, fruit or constituent effect.
  • Endpoint boundary: LAL/LBP are not direct proof of gut leakage, portal origin or LPS bioactivity.
  • Reported null outcomes: CRP and TNF-alpha did not change.
  • Translation: No brain benefit is established.

Intervention admitted = Yes — existing canonical record; not newly adjudicated in this demo.

Intervention confidence: not assigned — rubric pending adjudication.
