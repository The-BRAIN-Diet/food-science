## Initial finding — proposed wording

**Finding label:** Wheat bran lowered serum estrone and estradiol in premenopausal women

**Finding statement:** In 62 premenopausal women, estimated dietary fibre intake was raised from about 15 g/d to 30 g/d for two months by wheat, oat, or corn bran, without a significant fall in dietary fat. In the wheat-bran group, serum estrone fell (P < 0.002) and serum estradiol fell (P < 0.02). Serum progesterone and sex-hormone-binding globulin did not change in that group. Serum estrogens did not change with oat bran or corn bran.

**Limitations, stated after the result:**

- The fibre figure is an estimated intake increase of about 15 g/d. A separate wheat-bran gram weight was not in the retrieved abstract.
- Assignment method, blinding, group size, means, and confidence intervals were not in the retrieved abstract.
- The estrogen result is wheat-bran-specific. Oat bran and corn bran are the study comparators, and they did not show the same estrogen change.
- Population and duration are these premenopausal women and two months.
- No microbial enzyme, receptor, brain, cognitive, or clinical endpoint was measured.
- The lower estrone and estradiol concentrations are the measured direction. They are not classified as benefit or harm.

**Citation:** Rose et al. (1991), `rose_fibre_estrogen_1991`. The same citation remains on PM1 and PM2.

## What the Rose measurement covers

Rose et al. measured circulating estrone and estradiol, and measured sex-hormone-binding globulin without a change. That is a circulating-concentration result plus a null transport-protein result. Production rate, metabolic clearance, and receptor activity were not measured. Those unmeasured parts stay outside the Finding.
