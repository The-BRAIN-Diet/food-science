ARCHITECTURE
Dietary Confidence is an optional assessment of an already admitted, precisely scoped relationship. Only High, Moderate and Low are stored. No record means Unassessed. Nonadmission remains in existing admission/hypothesis statuses; it is not a confidence value. No confidence assessment is created for a hypothesis, including one marked Supported. Six atoms, Type A/B, Direct/Derived, SEC/CC, food mappings and roll-up eligibility are preserved.

PILOT A: BRS1-FM4-PM9 / PM9-DIT-3 — HIGH
Type A Capacity; Derived. Dietary B6 supplies precursors of GAD's PLP cofactor under functioning absorption and activation. The proposition is sustaining biochemical capacity, not a measurable increase in brain GABA after extra B6. GAD's PLP dependence, human PDXK/PNPO enzymology, human oral B6 utilisation and dietary-deficiency animal evidence form a coherent supply/dependency chain. Requiring neuronal PLP occupancy or benefit from supplementation would change the proposition being assessed. High applies to this capacity relationship, not every food's bioavailability, a therapeutic dose or a human neurological effect. Martin and Rimvall is secondary synthesis, not another primary experiment. Field 2022 is not used as proof of GABA synthesis.
Primary checks:
- GAD structure/kinetics: https://www.nature.com/articles/nsmb1228
- Human PDXK: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17766369/
- Human PNPO: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2323923/
- Human stable-isotope B6 utilisation: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1995788/
- Dietary B6 deficiency and rat-brain GAD: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1471-4159.1972.tb01374.x
The existing B6 atom's abbreviated conversion wording and its pre-existing PNPO review concern are preserved. The new assessment accurately distinguishes PL phosphorylation from PN/PM phosphorylation followed by PNPO oxidation. Direct PLP's unresolved admission is unchanged.

PILOT C: BRS5-FM3-PM7 / PM7-DIT-DEFERRED-1 — LOW, BOUNDED
Type B Regulation; Direct. The existing admission is unchanged. Li's mouse imaging, vagotomy and receptor perturbations support defined intestinal linoleic-acid engagement of vagal/cNST sensing. Food presence alone would be inadequate delivery evidence. Primary lipase-inhibition experiments demonstrate triglyceride hydrolysis and luminal free-fatty-acid release, independently supporting credible digestive delivery. They measure mixed-fat digestion and CCK/hormonal responses, not linoleic-specific human vagal activity. Thus there is a supported delivery bridge, not a missing essential link concealed by Low.
Low reflects narrow model/formulation evidence and limited generalisability. It does NOT establish ordinary-meal equivalence to the 10% free-LA stimulus, a food serving's effective dose, human circuit engagement or a beneficial direction. No substantive conflict with the existing bounded mouse-stimulus admission was identified. Extending this admission to a human ordinary-food circuit effect would exceed the evidence and must be reviewed rather than enabled by Low. If credible delivery itself becomes unsupported, remove/withhold the confidence assessment and flag the admission conflict; preserve historical admission pending adjudication.
Primary checks:
- Mouse mechanism: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05266-z
- Human fat hydrolysis/free-fatty-acid release: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9428226/
- Human lipase-inhibition trial: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12684211/

Scientific refinements to Cursor's reasoning:
1. The 2020 human biopsy null is bounded to acute induced injury; it is not universal evidence against butyrate effects. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32422994/
2. A 2025 primary study reports a transcellular protective result after direct colonic butyrate perfusion in IBS. It is important context, but is not demonstrated paracellular tight-junction regulation or dietary RS delivery. https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2025.2545414
3. The potato trial's small-intestinal permeability improvement must be retained alongside its colonic-probe null; neither establishes the proposed butyrate-mediated route. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9026745/
4. Moreau's labelled butyrate was infused, not traced from starch carbon, and increased uptake followed healing. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14988436/
5. Carbon tracing is one useful research approach, not a new mandatory exclusive admission gate. Convergent mediation, perturbation and rescue evidence may also test the proposition. The missing joined relationship is not repaired by inventing a stricter universal evidence rule.
