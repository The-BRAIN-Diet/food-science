# From Food to Brain: Following a Nutrient

**Status:** Lesson draft — unpublished, for scientific and editorial review  
**Audience:** BRAIN learners, authors, reviewers and practitioners  
**Purpose:** Follow a nutrient through distinct biological stages and recognise the evidence needed at each transition.

Food composition describes what a food supplies. It does not by itself describe how much reaches a brain compartment, enters an active chemical form or changes a biological process. Following those transitions makes nutritional reasoning more precise.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Distinguish dietary provision, absorption, transport, tissue availability and biochemical utilisation.
2. Separate a dietary precursor from the active form used in a reaction.
3. Identify the point in a pathway that a measurement actually addresses.
4. Explain why delivery and dependency do not automatically establish benefit from increased intake.

## 1. Follow the stages separately

Use this sequence as a set of questions, rather than a fixed quantitative model:

**Dietary provision → absorption → transport → tissue availability → biochemical utilisation.**

At **dietary provision**, identify the substance, its chemical form and the evidence for its presence in the consumed material. A verified composition measurement establishes content under its stated preparation and measurement basis.

At **absorption**, ask what passes from the digestive tract into the body. Content and absorbed quantity are different observations. Do not invent an absorption fraction from a food-composition value.

At **transport**, ask how the substance circulates and crosses the relevant barrier. At **tissue availability**, ask which chemical pool or compartment is accessible to the receiving process. A blood measurement is not automatically a measurement inside a neuron.

At **biochemical utilisation**, ask whether the process uses that available material. Enzyme activity, incorporation and metabolic flux address different operations. A functional outcome is a further question, requiring evidence connecting those operations to the outcome.

The [DHA mechanism](https://thebraindiet.org/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation/) and [GABA synthesis mechanism](https://thebraindiet.org/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm4/brs1-fm4-pm9-gaba-synthesis-capacity/) provide worked examples of these boundaries.

## 2. Follow vitamin B6 toward GABA synthesis

Start with the accepted dietary B6 relationship in the GABA synthesis page. B6 describes a family of vitamers, or related vitamin forms; PLP names the active cofactor used by glutamate decarboxylase. Dietary provision of the family and the intracellular cofactor requirement are connected but distinct.

Activation is form-specific. Pyridoxal can be phosphorylated to PLP by pyridoxal kinase. Pyridoxine and pyridoxamine routes also involve pyridoxamine-phosphate oxidase after phosphorylation. These are alternative conversion routes, not one identical reaction for every form. See the canonical [PLP page](https://thebraindiet.org/docs/substances/nutrients/micronutrients/vitamins/pyridoxal-5-phosphate/) and [GABA mechanism evidence](https://thebraindiet.org/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm4/brs1-fm4-pm9-gaba-synthesis-capacity/).

The distinction matters when reading the dietary relationship: dietary B6 can supply precursors for the required cofactor without establishing independent dietary delivery of PLP to the reaction site. The amount eaten cannot simply be relabelled as an amount of neuronal PLP.

Once PLP participates in the glutamate-to-GABA reaction, there is still a separate question about synthesis rate. The canonical evidence does not establish that ordinary increases in B6 intake increase human brain GABA production. Nor would a behavioural change alone measure that production.

## 3. Follow DHA without skipping the exposure route

Open the DHA mechanism and identify the form being studied. Lysophosphatidylcholine-bound DHA (LPC-DHA) is relevant to MFSD2A-mediated barrier transport. MFSD2A is a membrane transporter; evidence for its substrate does not establish that every ingested DHA form takes the same route or arrives in the same amount.

The [human tracer study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19112173/) examined incorporation from circulating unesterified DHA after intravenous tracer administration. It starts at circulation, downstream of dietary absorption. It therefore cannot supply the missing meal-to-blood absorption fraction.

The neonatal-piglet delivery-form comparison addresses a different question: whether the tested dietary forms produced different tissue accretion in that model. Keep that result attached to its developmental and animal context. Neither experiment establishes that adding choline to an ordinary meal increases human neuronal DHA incorporation. Follow the current [DHA Findings](https://thebraindiet.org/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm3/brs1-fm3-pm7-neuronal-membrane-dha-incorporation/) for the separately qualified upstream supply relationships.

## 4. Distinguish a pool from a flow

A tissue concentration describes a pool at a time and place. A flux describes material moving through a process per unit time. A pool depends on entry, formation, use and removal; its size alone cannot identify which process changed.

The [GABA mechanism's concentration-versus-synthesis evidence](https://thebraindiet.org/docs/biological-targets/brs1/fm4/brs1-fm4-pm9-gaba-synthesis-capacity/) makes this distinction concrete. Do not read a GABA concentration difference as a measured synthesis-rate difference. Similarly, tissue incorporation does not by itself establish improvement in attention or another functional outcome.

## Knowledge check

1. A food contains B6. Has neuronal PLP availability been measured?  
   **Answer:** No. Composition establishes provision; absorption, conversion and compartment-specific availability remain separate questions.
2. A tracer is injected into blood. Which early stages does that experiment bypass?  
   **Answer:** Dietary release, digestion and intestinal absorption.
3. If a cofactor is required, does its intake control the reaction rate?  
   **Answer:** Not necessarily. Dependency establishes a necessary role; intake responsiveness requires evidence for the relevant conditions.

## Applied exercise

Take one dietary relationship from the GABA or DHA page. Trace provision, absorption, transport, tissue availability and utilisation in words. At each stage, mark whether the linked evidence measured the step, supports it through established biology, or leaves a question unresolved. Preserve the exposure form and species. Stop where the evidence stops; do not fill a missing quantity with an assumed conversion yield.

## Core principle

A supported supply route explains how a nutrient can contribute to biology. The amount reaching a compartment, the response to more intake and the resulting functional benefit remain distinct claims.
