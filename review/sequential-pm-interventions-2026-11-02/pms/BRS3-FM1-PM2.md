# BRS3-FM1-PM2 — Gut-derived inflammatory signalling

Continuing the BRS3 audit after the NF-κB pilot. Saved queue pointer remains BRS2-FM1-PM3.

## Screen

Mission: limit gut-derived endotoxin spillover. Existing SOP: PM2-SOP-DEFERRED-1, the same Ghanim meal, already tied to finding PM2-F5. No lifestyle record. The public six-atom row existed, but its measured effect also stated the mononuclear NF-κB result.

Linked food evidence is glycine composition for beef, pork and lamb. None measured endotoxin. Oligofructose is an admitted mouse dietary input with no verified whole-food edge. The meal comparison came from the existing finding, not a food page.

## Candidates

1. P1 — Ghanim 2009 plasma endotoxin and LPS-binding protein. Decision: retain the historical bounded admission. PMC2782991: limulus endotoxin rose from 0.39 ± 0.07 to 0.58 ± 0.10 EU/ml at 3 hours (47 ± 14%) after the high-fat/high-carbohydrate meal; LPS-binding protein rose 34 ± 14% at 2 hours; the fibre/fruit meal did not change either. Meal endotoxin content was 4,200 EU versus 5,700 EU, so the meal's own endotoxin does not explain the plasma difference. No collapsed mechanism line. The NF-κB result is not restated here.
2. P3 — oligofructose in obese mice. Decision: do not promote. Cani 2009 is already the admitted dietary input PM2-DIT-RECOVERY-OLIGOFRUCTOSE.
3. P3 — cysteine and glycine. Decision: do not promote. They remain upstream glutathione supply. Sekhar 2011 measured red-cell glutathione, not endotoxin.

## Paper use

The same defined meal can change plasma endotoxin and LPS-binding protein without proving intestinal absorption or isolating a food. That is a different endpoint from the NF-κB result.
