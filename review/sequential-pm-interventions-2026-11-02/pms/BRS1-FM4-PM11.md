## Food-source closure and magnesium preparation follow-up — 3 November 2026

The 37 mapped food pages were cross-checked against the previously read PM3 food screen. Candidate origin: existing BRAIN food preparation advice, followed by new primary-reference retrieval. Generic soaking/fermentation claims cannot inherit the iron-absorption evidence as a magnesium result.

Recovered and read the full six-page author copy of Bohn, Davidsson, Walczyk & Hurrell (2004), *Phytic acid added to white-wheat bread inhibits fractional apparent magnesium absorption in humans*, DOI [10.1093/ajcn/79.3.418](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/79.3.418). Scite returned only an abstract; the [author-hosted PDF](https://publicationslist.org/data/torsten-bohn/ref-135/Bohn-AJCN-2004.pdf) supplied methods/results. Two random-order crossover studies analysed nine and eight healthy adults after marker-recovery exclusions. Matched 200-g white-bread meals had standardised magnesium and added phytate. Fractional apparent absorption decreased with phytate; no neural endpoint was measured. This demonstrates an absorption inhibitor, not a tested household dephytinisation intervention. Higher native magnesium in whole grains can offset a lower fractional absorption: do not recommend refined bread as nutritionally superior.

Followed its intervention reference: Morris et al. (1988), DOI [10.1016/S0271-5317(88)80066-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0271-5317(88)80066-6). Publisher abstract: ten men, 36 g whole/dephytinised bran daily for 15 days each; apparent absorption and net balance differ, with no deleterious mineral nutriture conclusion for whole bran. Full processing procedure, magnesium-specific estimates and uncertainty remain unverified. **Defer** a magnesium-targeted phytate-reduction SOP pending those details. This is an exposure/methods limitation, not a new requirement for human brain trials.

Consistency clarification: animal evidence is not automatically ineligible. Existing exercise candidates remain unadmitted pending a reproducible exposure and model-specific appraisal, not because they are nonhuman. Earlier wording “defer human protocol” must not be read as a universal human-trial admission requirement. Their appraisal is therefore still outstanding; do not count this PM as completed.

Further exercise retrieval: Cheng et al. (2016), DOI [10.1016/j.cmet.2015.10.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2015.10.013), original PMC manuscript and figure 7 inspected. Wheel access preceded kainate challenge for 30 days and continued seven days afterward; neuronal survival differed by Sirt3 genotype (five mice/group). This supplies a reproducible experimental candidate and mechanistic knockout comparison. It must not be rejected for lacking a human trial. Supplementary methods retrieval returned a browser-verification page, leaving allocation/blinding, sex and detailed model procedures unverified. Candidate retained for bounded preclinical admission adjudication; no new score or premature human exercise recommendation. The unresolved fields remain an explicit appraisal task rather than a scientific Evidence Gap.
