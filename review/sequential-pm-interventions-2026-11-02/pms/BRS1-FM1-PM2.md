## Retrieval follow-up — 2 November 2026

The earlier abstract retrieval failure is resolved. PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16218925/) and the publisher abstract (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1365-201X.2005.01482.x) were accessible. The publisher full-text endpoint returned an access error; searches for an accessible original manuscript recovered bibliographic/abstract records, not full methods. No claim of full-text appraisal.

Blomstrand et al. 2005: five men completed two 3-hour cycling conditions at 200 ± 7 W, receiving 6% carbohydrate or flavoured-water placebo. Radial arterial/internal-jugular sampling and plasma flow estimated net cerebral amino-acid exchange. Placebo showed approximately 106 μmol tryptophan uptake; carbohydrate showed no statistically significant uptake. This within-condition significance pattern alone does not establish a significant between-condition uptake difference. BCAA uptake occurred in both conditions.

Interpretation: relevant human cerebral-exchange evidence, stronger endpoint specificity than a plasma ratio alone. Net exchange does not isolate LAT1 transport kinetics or demonstrate serotonin synthesis, benefit, or a general meal strategy. Reduced uptake is not automatically improvement. Exact drink volume/timing/composition, allocation order, flow methodology and between-condition uptake statistics remain unverified from full methods. Candidate remains deferred for protocol extraction and benefit/mission adjudication; no canonical admission or SEC change. Origin: existing external-literature lead; this targeted retrieval is not a new food-page-led discovery. Mandatory food-page screening remains pending before further discovery.

## CSF breakfast appraisal — 3 November 2026

Teff, Young, Marchand and Botez, Journal of Neurochemistry 1989;52:235–241 (PMID 2462018; DOI 10.1111/j.1471-4159.1989.tb10922.x). The publisher full text was not retrieved. Meal weights, participant count, allocation order, concentrations and statistics remain unverified. No SOP is admitted and no breakfast is recommended.

The authors' abstract: patients with normal-pressure hydrocephalus had three lumbar punctures in one week. Water, a protein breakfast, or a carbohydrate breakfast was ingested 2.5 hours before each puncture. The protein meal raised cerebrospinal-fluid tyrosine and did not alter tryptophan or biogenic-amine metabolites. The carbohydrate meal did not affect cerebrospinal-fluid tryptophan, tyrosine, 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid or homovanillic acid, and increased 3-methoxy-4-hydroxyphenylethylene glycol, which the authors call unexplained. Their conclusion is that these meals do not change plasma amino acids enough to cause appreciable changes in central tryptophan or 5-hydroxytryptamine synthesis.

PubMed indexing adds blood histidine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, tyrosine and 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid, plus cerebrospinal-fluid tyrosine and 3-methoxy-4-hydroxyphenylethylene glycol. Indexing is narrower than the abstract and is not the result table. Young's 2002 review, citing this paper, reads the carbohydrate effect on 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid as too small to detect. The 4% protein figure in that review cites the separate 1989 plasma-and-satiety paper, not these meals.

Adjudication: a lumbar concentration change in this patient group is not LAT1 competition, cerebral exchange, or a neurotransmitter effect. The record does not support a practical dietary strategy. DISC-CSF-MEAL-AA stays uncertain and is not admitted. Canonical page unchanged.
